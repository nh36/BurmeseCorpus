1. Introduction.
BURMAN LANGUAGES
1. MARU *
by
DR. HLA PE +
This is the first of a series of articles
on cognate words so far traced in Bur-
mese and other Tibeto-Burman languages.
This first contribution deals with Maru.
I plan to follow it up with similar arti-
cles on Kachin, Chin, and others.
My purpose is twofold. As a Bur-
man I am naturally anxious to put on
record whatever data I have collected
for scholars who are interested in this
field, which has not been explored ade-
quately for lack of material. As a joint-
editor of A new Burmese-English Diction-
ary, now being compiled (and published
in parts) at the School of Oriental and
African Studies, University of London, I
believe that further investigations into
these languages by trained scholars will
bear fruitful results, which in turn are
bound to help us in giving more satisfac-
ory etymological notes in subsequent
arts of our dictionary.
The main sources of all the cognate
words in this as well as in later articles are
dictionaries, grammars and other writings
on the language concerned. These words
are chiefly from the oral language, since
among the
Tibeto-Burman languages,
only Burmese and Tibetan have a script
of their own. It would have been more
satisfying to have re-checked the material
in hand with native speakers, or better
still to have investigated them in the
areas where the language is spoken: some
of the works are more than fifty years
old, and they were produced when the
knowledge of linguistics and phonetics
was not as highly developed as it is now.
A start has however to be made, and it
is better to make it now than later.

The following features may be noted.
1. Burmese dental fricatives 0, ð = Maru aveolar fricatives s, sh, hs, ts.
2. Some Burmese aveolar fricatives 5, sh, z=Maru palato-aveolar affricates
ch, tch, j, ky.
3. Burmese palato-aveolar affricates c, ch, j=Maru (with a few exceptions)
palato-aveolar affricates ky, khy, hky, ch, gy, j, (k, hk, r, ry)
4. Burmese palato-aveolar fricative =Maru (with a few exceptions) palato-
aveolar affricates ky, ch, (kr, ry), and aveolar fricative sh.
5. h-words with the initials l, m, n, n, and, (with one exception in each case)
p, t= non-h words in Maru: the two exceptional words are given with an
asterisk mark in the list.
6. No Maru words are found that correspond to Burmese words with the initials
h and ny.
