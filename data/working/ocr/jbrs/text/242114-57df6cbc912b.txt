SOME RECORDS OF HLESATUN.
XXIV, II, 1934.
1. This collection of records relating to Hlesatun Village in Myingyan
District is of very great interest, and apparently contains certain documents
which are probably unique.
2. The information now available regarding Burmese administration
in the 18th century is chiefly to be found in the Revenue Records or Sittans.
A complete Sittans contains a Text, giving the ancestry of the Headman,
the charge-boundaries, and revenue customs; and a Roll containing the
inhabitants, classified according to sex, age and asu.
The best known Sittans are those of 1145 and 1164. Sittans of other
dates are rare, but U Tin of Pagan used to say that the enquiries were held
every ten years. A History of Pagan that he lent me contained the Text
of some of the 1126 Sittans and some of these referred to an enquiry held
in "9-ku hnit" (1109), though without details.
Among these documents appears to be a list of revenue payments
made in "9-ku hnit"; if so, it is probably unique.
The Sittan of 1126 is complete with Text and Roll; I know of no other
copy of the 1126 Roll and, except in U Tin's history have never seen or
heard of an 1126 Sittan Text.
One document seems to be the Roll of the 1136 Sittan; if so, this is
probably unique.
3. According to local tradition, Pyinsi was formerly an athi myo, and

subsequently assigned to the Shwepyi Yan-aung Myin as a separate Division

The records show that from 1118 to 1164 Pyinsi was athi, but transfer-
red to the Myin-ne in 1217. I know of no other documentary evidence
supporting the local tradition. The records also furnish some particulars
regarding the obligations laid on the area as part of the Myin-ne.
4. One of the documents seems to be a record of the distribution of
the thathameda tax in 1211, just after its introduction. If so, this is pro-
bably unique.
5. The records also throw much light on Burmese administrative orga-
nisation and judicial procedure, and on economic customs and conceptions.
6. Probably a good many of them have been copied ; some of them
may have been forged in connection with the disputed succession to which
some of them relate. But the forgeries, if any, date from about 1217; the
points most likely to have been falsified are those, historically, of least
importance and the collection is of great interest and importance and
deserves careful study.
7. The documents belong to U Tun Win, Secretary to the Myingyan
District Council, son of the Hlesatun Thugyi. It is to be hoped that the
University will acquire them.

Notes.-Pagan Ù Tin has often told me that under the Burmese Kings,
the sittan enquiries were held periodically "like the English Census, but
every ten years instead of every eleven years". I was never quite able to
accept the statement, but if the above Sittan is of 1136, there would seem
to be better foundation for his statement than I thought. I have seen no
other Sittan of 1136.
In a Pagan History lent me by U Tin there were the texts of some
1126 Sittans, with a reference to a previous enquiry in "9-ku hnit". The
last document cited above seems to be a list of revenue payments made in
this enquiry of "9-ku nhit". If so, it is probably the only one still extant in
Burma.
