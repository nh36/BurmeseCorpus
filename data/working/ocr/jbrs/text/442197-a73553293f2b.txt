INTRODUCTION
Though a few trips to Maungmagan and the adjoining shores had been taken
before the Second World War by the teachers and advance students of the then Biology
Department of the University of Rangoon, there was no written record of the collection
or the classification of the shore animals. This was probably because these trips were
arranged to fulfil the part requirements of the B.Sc. Pass and B.Sc. Honours courses
on marine zoology.
The aim of the present trip, taken in the month of October 1958, was not only to
collect marine specimens for teaching purposes but also to classify and describe as far as
possible in order to leave a written record. This trip, I must say, was not purely zoologi-
cal as two members from the Botany Department of the Rangoon University went with us
to collect botanical specimens. The Zoology Staff who participated in this trip were
U Nyi Nyi (Demonstrator), Ko Hla Kyu (Laboratory Assistant), and myself as a
leader.
The work of collection was fairly heavy and tedious as it involved the carrying of
necessary equipment and walking a few miles every day on the sandy shore-lines and
returning with the collection. Pre-arrangement basing on the tide and distance of the place
of collecting ground from the headquarters was also necessary in order to get a large
variety of specimens in a limited time available.
Collection of the specimens was made between Pan-da-in in the north and Tezit in
the south, a stretch of coast-line covering about 18 milcs. (See the attached map on
page 224). The actual collecting grounds, however, were Pan-da-in, Maungmagan, San-
Hlan and Tezit. These areas presented most of the ecological habitats of the littoral zone,
and were, therefore, very rich in bottom fauna. These ecological habitats inculded mud
flats, sandbeds, rock beds and a combination of two or all of them. The collection
included the catches of the inshore fishermen.
This trip lasted 23 days (7-10-58 to 29-10-58), but only 17 days (8-10-58 to 24-10-58)
were spent in actual collecting, the rest of the days being spent either in travelling or in
observing the marine animals of Tavoy Bazzaar.

4056 specimens, mainly invertebrates, were collected during the trip. The speci-
mens collected daily were sorted out, roughly classified, numbered, preserved in 10%
formalin and packed for shipment. The sorting out of the specimens from the total
collection of the day occasionally took more time than the actual period required for their
collection. All these marine specimens were put on display in the Zoology building of
the Rangoon University during the Science Exhibition of December 1958.
The present paper as indicated in the title, deals with the taxonomy of only the
vertebrate group from the collection. It is ernestly hoped that the account of the
invertebrates may be presented in the next meeting of the Burma Researsh Society. The
vertebrates classified and described here include mainly fishes of the shallow shore-line
water, the rest being a very poor representation of the Classes Amphibia, Reptilia and
Aves.
414 fishes, collected in this trip, are arranged into 2 subclasses, 4 orders, 25 families,
40 genera and 48 species. The method of classification employed here closely follows the
work of Sir Francis Day (1875–1878) on the Fishes of India, the only extensive work of
taxonomic nature that includes the well known fishes of the Burmese waters.
In the brief description of fishes are included the scientific names, common English
names, Burmese names, fin formulae, total length and their comparative market value at
Rangoon. No illustrations are given in this work but those of Day in Volume II, and in
two instances of Munro are quoted.
With a few exceptions which were caught by hand either in the mud flats or rock
pools, all the fishes were caught in shore by a drag-net with a seine of about half an inch.
A good number of small teleost fishes remain unidentified. These are either too young or
rare to be included in most available references. The few vertebrates belonging to the three
higher classes are merely listed in this work.

Body depressed, quadrangular shaped, not profusely covered by tubercles on
