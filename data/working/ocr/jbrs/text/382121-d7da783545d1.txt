To the best of my recollection, I think His Royal Highness Prince Damrong
initiated the practice of distributing books to those who come to assist in cremation
ceremonies and in the distribution of monastic robes and other gifts at Kathin
(Burmese Kat'ein) ceremonies in Siam.³ The practice was in vogue when I first
arrived in Bangkok about half a century ago. It must have been started about ten or
fifteen years before my arrival, but of this I am not sure.
The usual practice was for the donors of the books to ask H.R.H. Prince
Damrong to select a work for distribution. Prince Damrong was then in charge of
the Vajiranana National Library and he usually selected some old manuscripts
which had never yet been printed and which would be worth printing. It was usual
for H.R.H. Prince Damrong to write a preface or an introduction to the work, and he
supervised the editing and printing of the work. The National Libraries gets, I think,
two hundred copies of the printed books for distribution to public libraries and for
sale. The sale proceeds go to the funds of the National Library. Personally I think it
is an excellent practice which should be adopted in Burma if circumstances permit
its adoption. But a difficulty arises in that the bodies of deceased persons are kept in
Siam for many months and sometimes for many years before they are cremated.
This practice, or I may say custom, because it is almost universal in that country,

Introduction
Explanation of the causes of war between the Siamese and the Burmese.
According to what is evident in the History in Siam, when Ayut'ia was the
capital of Siam, the Siamese forget with the Burmese twenty-four times.
Subsequently when Krung Dhanapuri and Krung Ratanakosin (Bangkok) were the
capitals of Siam we fought with he Burmese twenty times, in all forty-four times. In
the forty-four times mentioned sometimes the Burmese invaded Siam and
sometimes the Siamese invaded Burma. I shall mention first about the Burmese
invaded Siam. The invasion of Siam by the Burmese which may be considered as
great wars are two in number. The invasions by three kings of Hongsawadi
(Hanthawaddy) one after another from the time of King Somdet Phra Maha
Chakrapat (Maha Cakravadi) up to the time of Somdet Phra Naresuon Maharat,
extending over a period of 57 years, may be considered as the first occasion. The
invasions by three Kings of Ava from the time of King Somdet Phra akat'at reigning
in Ayut'iya up to the time of the reign of Somdet Phra Phuta Yotfa Chulaloke
(Buddha Yodfa Culalokya) in Krung Ratana Kosin, extending over a period of 50
years, may be considered as the second occasion. In saying that the periods
extended 57 years on the first occasion and 50 years on the second occasion, I mean
in each case the time from the beginning of hostilities to the end of them, including
the intervals when there were no hostilities. Sometimes the interval lasted many
years when there was no fighting at all.
Formerly most of the residents of Siam understood that whenever there was
war with the King of Hongsawadi, the Siamese fought against the Mons, because
Hongsawadi was the capital of Ramanna Desa, i.e. the country of the Mons,
believing that the King of Hongsawadi was a Mon. But as a matter of fact it was not
so, The Kings of Hongsawadi who invaded Siam were Burmans, everyone of them.
Their ancestors lived in Toungoo. At that time they were able to overcome both
Burma and Mon country and kept them under their sway establishing Muang
Hongsawadi (Hanthawaddy town, Muang in Siamese means a town or city) as the
capital city.

That was the reason why the three Kings were known as the Kings of
Hongsawadi. But as regards the Mons it was not evident that they were, of their
own accord hostile to the Siamese. Whenever the Mons came to fight the Siamese it
was because the Burmese ordered them to come as auxiliaries, as they were then
under the rule of the Burmese. But whenever there was an opportunity, the Mons
became the enemies of the Burmese. Sometimes when they could not bear the
oppression of the Burmese, they come and found an asylum in Siam. Such was the
case from ancient times.
But the Mons were the cause of the fighting between the Siamese and the
Burmese almost every time; this was because the country of the Siamese and that of
the Burmese were far apart, the country of the Mons lying between the two in the
lower or southern part of modern Burma. The upper or northern part belonging to
the Burmans was still farther away from Siam by any other route because they were
separated firstly by Lannat'i, i.e., Mont'on Pak Bayab (Pali, Mandala Bhaga Bayab)
when it was independent, and secondly by the territories of the Tai Yai, commonly
called Ngio (in Siamese) or Shan (in Burmese). Because of this, whenever the Mons
were able to maintain their independence, there was no war between the Siamese
and the Burmese. The reason for this was that which ever people wanted to invade
the other, must get the Mon to become an ally or conquer the Mons and use their
country as a base, and the route of march of the invading army. The Siamese never
had any cause to go to the help of the Mons in their fight against the Burmese, nor
had they any cause to invade the Mon country, because both the countries of the
Siamese and of the Mons were prosperous with plenty of consumer goods, and were
near the seashore enabling both of them to trade with other maritime countries
easily. The boundaries between the two were hills dividing them. The Siamese had
no cause to be hostile to the Mons both in respect of economy or in respect of
encroachment of each other's territory. But the relation between the Burmese and
the Mons was otherwise. The country of the Burmese was situated in the upper
reaches and that of the Mons was situated at the mouths, of the river Arawadi
(Irrawaddy) and the river Satong (Sittaung). The kingdom of the Burmese was not
so prosperous as the kingdom of the Mons. To travel to and trade with other

countries by sea, the Burmese had to depend on the Mons and must pass through the

powerful, there were wars between them almost always, as shown in a work called

the addition of auxiliaries from other countries, while the Siamese had to fight
singly, On account of this Ayut'ia was destroyed twice.
But the Burmese were not able to hold the Siamese in their sway like the people
of other countries. When Siam had been pillaged by the Burmese, there arose within
a short time a men of outstanding ability among the Siamese who was able to
overcome with a small gathering of men a large body of the enemy, by means of his
courage and wisdom, and thus to establish the independence of Siam again. From
that time however much the Burmese might try to overrun Siam, they usually
suffered defeat and had to return without achieving their object. So much so that the
Siamese were sometimes able to invade the Burmese territory, and so the hostilities
came to an end. Such was the case in both the two great invasions of Siam by the
Burmese. Therefore, it will be noticed that the number of times in which the
Burmese were successful in their invasions of Siam, might be considered four times,
including the two great invasions. The number of times in which the Burmese
suffered defeat or had to return without achieving their object might be considered
nineteen times, excluding the two great invasions of Siam mentioned above. The
Burmese only molested the outlying provinces of Siam in pursuit of Mons who
emigrated into Siamese territory with their families. Sometimes they came to
plunder and seize Siamese property or capture slaves, and sometimes they came to
take Chiengmai, but every time they suffered defeat.

I shall now relate the occasions on which the Siamese invaded the Burmese
territory. I have explained before why the Burmese and the Siamese fought with
each other. Hostilities began because the Burmese conquered the Mon country and
they consequently carried the war into Siam. For this reason the war which the
Siamese carried into Burmese territory, though it was the initiative of the Siamese,
was only a reprisal of what the Burmese had done to the Siamese territory on
previous occasions, except the invasions of Burmese territory during the reigns of
the Third King and the Fourth King of the present dynasty. According to what is
stated in Siamese history, fighting took place because the Siamese encroached on
Burmese territory sixteen times, namely to drive the Burmese out of Siamese
territory seven times and to invade Burmese nine times.
That the Siamese drove the Burmese out of Siam requires some explanation
first. When the Burmese invaded Ayut'ia during the time of the Kings of
Hongsawadi or during the time of the Kings of Ava, the territory to the north of
central Siam, for instance Chiengmai, was not under the rule of the Siamese, but
under the Burmese on both occasions. The Burmese army depended on Chiengmai
for its commissariat and transport, and in consequence was able to invade Ayut'ia.
When the Siamese again regained their independence, there was not so much
trouble in expelling the Burmese out of southern Siam as in expelling them from
northern Siam. So long as the Burmese were all powerful in Chiengmai they were
able to invade Siam easily and therefore it was impossible to allow them to remain
in northern Siam. For this reason, it was necessary to fight the Burmese to wrest
Chiengmai from their hands. When the Siamese were able to drive the Burmese
from all northern Siam, then that part of the country came under the rule of the
Siamese. Therefore, the work of driving out the Burmese round about Chiengmai
was more important in extending the boundaries of Siam than the destruction of
Ayut'ia by the Burmese.
In invading Burma in retaliation, if compared with the invasion of Siam by the
Burmese, the Siamese were at a great disadvantage, because the Burmese invaded

successful. At the time when Bangkok was capital of Siam, but after the reign of
King Somdet Phra Buddha Yodfa Chulalokya, the Siamese invaded Burma twice,
once in the reign of the Third King and once in the reign of the Fourth King of the
present dynasty, without success. The last time that the Siamese fought the Burmese
was in Chula Sakkraj 1215, Buddha Era 2396, (A.D 1853) when the Siamese

From that time there has been no fighting between the Siamese and the Burmese up

About the hostilities between the Siamese and the Burmese at the time when
Ayut'ia was the capital of Siam.
Circumstance No. 1 when the Burmese invaded Maung Chieng Kran, in the
year of the dog, Buddha Era 2081, (A.D 1539).
When the Buddha Era was near the beginning of 2080, (A.D 1537) both the
country of the Burmese and that of Mons (Siamese Raman-Ramanna) were
independent kingdoms, but both had declined in power. The governor of Toungoo
whose name was Min Kyi Nyo (Siamese Mang Kinyo) and who belonged to the
Burmese royal house declared himself independent, and was crowned king,
assuming the title of King Maha Siri Chaisura (Burmese, Maha Thiri Zeyathura).
Toungoo is situated on the river Satong (Burmese, Sittaung or Paunglaung) and lies
between the counties of the Burmese and the Mons. The people of the country were
a mixture of Burmese and Mons at the time when king Rajadhirat (Burmese,
Razadarit) fought against King Farang Mankhong and the Burmese and the Mons
ran away to escape danger and sought refuge in Tungoo. In consequence, Toungoo,
rose in power from that time. Not long after Min Kyinyo had declared himself as
king, there was dissension in the royal house of the Burmese; each claimant to the
throne invited the Sawbwa of the Shans to come and help them in the fight among
themselves, with the result that the claimants did not fare well, and the Sawbwas
came and ruled in Ava. The Burmese nobles who did not want to live under the
Shans, left Ava and came and sought refuge with the King of Toungoo, who in
consequence rose in power, introduced improvements in his country and increased
his fighting forces. He thought of extending his power, but before he was able to
carry out his schemes he died and was succeeded by his son Mangtra, then only 16
years of age, He was crowned King and assumed the title of King Tabinshwet'i

Here it is necessary to mention the route of march of the Burmese into Siam.
There were two routes between Siam and Mon country which were government
roads in existence from ancient times, starting from Mawtama. The northern rout
commenced at Mawtama leading to the village of Tapu (Chiengkran) along a river.
Thence by overland, and crossing the river Me Kleep (Kaw Krit), river Me Moi (i.e.
Myawadi which is an outpost and a duty station at the present day) and river Me
sod, came to the Me Ping opposite Raheng village which is where the district office
of Tark is situated at the present day. This route is the usual route taken by the
people of the northern provinces of Siam. In the Siamese History it was called "The
road to the outpost and duty station of Me Lamow." Another road came from
Mawtama along Ataran river (Town of Traeng) up to the town of Sami, Thence
travelling overland, it crossed over a hill and entered Siamese territory at the three
pagodas. Thence it went along the river (1) Me Noi, the meeting place of three
rivers, namely the rivers (1) Me Noi, (2) San Tay and (3) Bikee which meet together
at that place. From the meeting place of the three rivers, the route was by boat to the
road leading to the town of Saiyoke. Thence it went along the river Khwe Yai, to
the town of Kanchanaburi at Lin Chang (exactly at the place where the present town
is situated.) This was another route. Or travelling overland from the meeting place
of the three rivers to the town of Saiyoke (old) one could cut across to the river
Khwe Yai, to the town of Sri Sawat or to the timber landing place at the duty station

and fight against Nandasu and Ratsangkram Phya Sawankhaloke and Phta Pichai
both thought that the strength of His Royal Highness was small and he would not be
able to go against the King of Hongsawadi, They wanted to escape the wrath of His
Majesty of Hongsawadi and so did not mobilise their forces and go to fight against
the Burmese according to the order of His Royal Highness. They collected the
people of the two towns, went up Muang Sawankhaloke and prepared for the
defense of the town. They arrested and imprisoned all civil officers who were still
loyal to Somdet Phra Naresaon and who did not belong to their party, believing that
as Muang Sawankhaloke had town walls and forts, they would be able to defend the
town while waiting for the movements of the army of Hongsawadi. If not, the forces
of Chiengmai could come to their assistance. Somdet Phra Naresaon knew that Phya
Sawankhaloke and Phya Pichai had revolted, therefore, he gathered at Muang Tark
the forces that went in pursuit of Nandasu and Ratsangkram, and came by way of
village Danlanhoi. On arriving at Muang Sukhot'i he built his temporary resting
place by the side of the Monastery Sichum. He came to the conclusion that the
Siamese were still not of one mind and there was a large number of people who
were still afraid of the King of Hongsawadi. He therefore held the ceremony of
swearing allegiance. He had water brought from a consecrated pond called Puey Sri,
held in high esteem as potent water from the time of King Somdet Phra Ruong who
used it for drinking the water of allegiance. He made the military and civil officers
as well as the common people drink the water of allegiance, making an asseveration
to fight against the enemy and redeem Siam to become an independent country. He
then marched against Sawankhaloke and surrounded the town. He ordered a
government servant to go in and tell the 2 Phya (governors) by shouting to come
and admit their fault, when he would pardon them. The 2 Phyas would not listen,

Prince's government servants. His Royal Highness was very angry and ordered his
forces to attack the town on the northern and western sides at the same time,

The Siamese Prince was of opinion that as the northern provinces had been ravaged
much, because the people had been much harassed on many occasions, the Siamese
would not be to resist the attack if His Majesty of Hongsawadi were to come with a
large army as on the previous occasion. For one thing their fighting people were few
and there were many persons still afraid of the King of Hongsawadi. Moreover, the
army at the capital, was still small as His Majesty of Hongsawadi had taken away a
very large number of people from the capital and those who had been able to return

solely for the defence of the capital in resisting the attack in the future of the army
of Hongsawadi. The defence should be concentrated at one place only, and it was
