FOR MAUNG KHWAY' (မောင်ခွေးဘို့)—It is not often
that books of nursery rhymes qualify for notice in the journals
of learned societies, not at any rate until their origin is lost
in history or myth and so calls forth research. But to be in at the
beginning may save years of research and conjecture in later cen-
turies. For Maung Khway is a pioneer book of Burmese Nursery
Rhymes, and its preparation has united in one purpose four artists
of recognised merit. Maung Wun, better known by his pen name
of Minthuwun, has written the poems, brief witty impressions of
everyday things in the life of a child, breathing an atmosphere of
peaceful village life. The songs, short and simple as they are, have
their rhyme at the end of the lines, obviously they do not offer scope
for the more intricate pattern and zigzag rhyme of the classical poetry.

They have been set to Burmese tunes by U Khin Zaw, and
reveal more melody to a Western ear than one had thought possible
from listening to an occasional pwè. U Khin Zaw has been making
a study of Burmese music, and his recent lecture on that subject
with some forty pages of old Burmese tunes collected by him is in
preparation for a future number of the Journal.
The illustrations by U Ba Nyan are most effective, arresting in
design they have something of the simplicity of the poster.
It is always difficult to translate poems into another language
without losing something of their sense and beauty. In this case
the childlike spirit of the poems has sometimes been lost in the
translation; 'Fast Day' and ‘Parrot' are happy exceptions, but
'Observant Little One' rather misses the spirited rhythm of the
original with its curtolo: The most pleasing item in the
book is without doubt Cradle Song' (n:); words, tune and
illustration all catch the same spirit, while the translation by
G.H.L. keeps amazingly close to the Burmese both in form and
meaning.

Maung Khway ought to be very pleased with his first book of
nursery rhymes; as the editor says, it will form a pleasing relaxation
from the 'aw-wa-das' and 'sômasās' thrust upon him so often in
home and school.
For Maung Khway has been beautifully printed by the A.B.M.
Press and is published at two rupecs. A quick sale of the first
edition will make possible a cheaper second edition which will bring
this lovely little book into the hands of the many poorer cousins of
Maung Khway.
Two double-sided gramophone records (priced Rs. 2-12 each)
have been made of these songs, the singers being Ma Khin Thein
of Judson College and U Khin Zaw. English readers particularly
will have their appreciation of Burmese poetry and music quickened
by listening to these records on the gramophone, while Burmese
parents who cannot read staff notation will be the more easily able
to learn these tunes to sing to their children.
