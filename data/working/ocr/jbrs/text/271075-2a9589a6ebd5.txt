THE 27 NAKSHATRAS AND THE 8 INNER
CONSTELLATIONS.
The title suggests a knowledge of Burmese astronomy and
astrology to which the present contributor does not pretend. It is
merely an aid to star-gazing offered with the hope that perhaps
one layman will save another the trouble he has taken in search of
the nakshatras and constellations so familiar to us by name in
Burmese literature. Poet-friends sing of these in immortal verse,
but when asked to point them out in the sky express indignation.
Even astrologers who prognosticate events of dread import
and futures dire, have been known to reveal an academic rather
than an actual knowledge of the heavens.
In Brittanica Encyclopaedia, vol. 21, 14th edition, are two star-
charts in polar projection. One of them is some help in the
northern hemisphere. But the real help comes not from the chart
but from the foot-note in small type (By courtesy of J. C. Clancey,
I.S.O., F.R.A.S., from "Simplified stellar maps " published by the
Superintendent, Government printing and stationery Rangoon).
So far as I know these stellar maps are the only authority on
the aspects of the heavens of our latitudes. Having established
with this help a familiarity with our night skies many vistas of
enthralling interest are open to the star-gazer. One of them,
however, is by no means an easy road. Very few reliable guides to
Burmese constellations exist. I have seen only one Burmese Star-
chart (in the Bernard Free Library) and that is in parabeik form
opening out to form a parallelogram about 1½ by 7 feet. Three
lines divide it lengthwise into four equal parts, inner, middle, outer
and outermost: the inner division including the constellations
nearest the pole-star. But it is not drawn in polar projection. The
relative positions of stars in the inner division suffer a similar
distortion to that of the southern stars in a technical north-polar
projection extended to include both the visible and invisible
hemispheres so that the nadir, a point, is stretched out into a vast
outer circle called the reverse horizon.
Still, the parabeik is much more helpful than the circular
drawings of the twenty seven nakshatras with the mythical mount
Meru in the centre (vis, in Htoon Chan's Arakanese calendar and

Even on that parabeik the shapes of the constellations follow
more closely the figures given to them by fancy than their actual
shapes in the firmament. But a steady eye can make out here and
there the shapes of well known constellations, Orion, Ursa Major,
Scorpio, which assist in the task of comparison with star-charts
such as Ball's.

Armed with that parabeik and Clancey's charts and Ball's maps
and a superficial acquaintance with our heavens I set out to identify
the Burmese constellations. But I soon realised my temerity.
The task requires much more than a willing pair of eyes.
requires scholarship. There is an impressive list of Burmese
works on astronomy and adeemasa in Htoon Chan's calendar.
have also found in the course of my bewildered wanderings some
guide-posts which I list here without comment:
