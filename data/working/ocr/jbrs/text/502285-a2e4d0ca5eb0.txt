THE MEANING AND SIGNIFICANCE OF THE
DHAMMACAKKA
WORD
U TIN LWIN
(SUMMARY)
The importance of the Dhamma-
cakkappavattana Sutta lies in a series of
facts of which three may be mentioned
here: firstly, it is the first sermon of the
Buddha; secondly, it embodies the basic
tenets of Buddhism; and, lastly, it led the
Ven. Koṇḍañña to the attainment of
Sotapatti.
The importance of the Sutta is also
evident from mention of the occurrence of
supernatural things on the occasion of its
deliverance by the Buddha, which is one
of the major landmarks in his life. Such
mentions as that the Sutta was delivered
also by previous Buddhas, that its deliver-
ence could be compared with the roar of
a lion and that among the Buddha's
disciples only the Ven. Sāriputta was
capable of re-delivering it also point to
its importance.
There is also two-fold evidence regard-
ing its importance; it is preserved in more
than one book in Buddhist literature and
it is frequently referred to in Pali literature.
The meaning and significance of the
word Dhammacakka also suggests the im-
portance of the Sutta. As might be expected
the commentaries explain the word as a
compound. But the way they explain the
word is interesting. The Patisambhidamagga
says that the word can be split into two
and the order should be Dhamma and
Cakka or Cakka and Dhamma. As Dhamma
and Cakka it is Kammadharaya Compound
because the two have the same connotation.
When the Bodhisatta became enlightened
under the Bodhi tree the Dhamma arose
in him first and it is the Dhamma that,
like a Cakka (weapon), killed his enemies
in the form of kilesa. But as Cakka and
Dhamma it is Dvanda Compound because
they are two separate things. When the
Buddha preached the Five Bhikkhus in
the Deer Park the Dhamma, like a Cakka
(weapon), killed the Bhikkhus' kilesa first
and then there arose in them the Dhamma.
As Kammadharaya Compound it means
Pațivedha (Penetration) and as Dvanda
Desana (Teaching).
The Patisambhidāmagga further explains
the word as "Dhammeno pavattetiti Dham-
macakkam, Dhammacariyāya pavattetiti
Dhammacakkam, etc.". Here the common
words are Dhamma and pavatteti. The
Dhamma of the explanations is of course
the same as the Dhamma of Dhammacakka.
Pavatteti however represents Cakka because
from pavattei comes pavattana and from
pavattana Cakka. All these explanations
boil down to Desana.
The Commentary on the Samyutta
Nikāya however takes the word as a whole
and says that it means Pativedhañana
(Knowledge of Penetration) and Desanāñāṇa
(Knowledge of Teaching). Similar explana-
tions are given in the Commentaries on
the Patisambhidāmagga and the Buddha-
vamsa. According to the Sub-commen-
taries, the word is explained as the two

These two have their common and

characteristics are that, as Knowledge,
they arose in the breast of the Buddha
and that they are not associated with
others. Their uncommon characteristics
are as follows: the Pațivedha is based on

for the Buddha himself whereas the latter
for others; in Abhidhamma sense the former
is Lokuttara-mahakusala-citta whereas the
latter Lokiyamahākriya – sampayutta – citta;
the former earned the Bodhisatta the title
Buddha (the Enlightened One) whereas the
latter earned the Buddha Satthä (Teacher).
The word Dhammacakka is thus meaningful
and significant with Cakka explained in
the sense of weapon.
If the word is taken to be Kamma-
dharaya Compound with Cakka meaning
wheel, the significance can be seen in the
light of anthropology and history. As such,
the attributes of the Dhamma are similar
to those of the wheel. What then are the
attributes of the wheel?
The wheel is one of the most wonder-
ful things that men have ever invented.
V. Gordon Childe says that it was "the
crowning achievement of prehistoric
carpentry" though no one has the idea of
the date of that achievement as wooden
objects cannot normally last many
centuries. According to the learned profes-
sor, wheeled vehicles are represented in
Sumerian art as early as 3500 B.C and in
North Syria perhaps even earlier. In the
Indus Valley and Turkestan wheeled carts

were in use by about 2500 B.C. Apart
from revolutionizing transport, the wheel
was already applied in manufacturing
industry by 3500 B.C., the first application
being in pot-making. But the two process-
es did not always go together.
Among the ancient peoples, perhaps
the Aryans had special love for the wheel.
According to the Vedas, their chariot
(ratha) usually had two wheels (cakra).
But there is mention also of three wheeled,
seven wheeled or even eight-wheeled
chariots. But this does not mean that
chariots having more than two wheels were
actually in use. The multiplied number of
the wheel shows their intense love for it.
The chariot according to the Aryan is
also divine. Vedic gods are sometimes
described as proud possessors of chariots,
and among them, the most notable one
is owned by Surya the Sun-God and drawn
by seven horses or a horse with seven
heads. Sometimes gods themselves are
charioteers. For instance, Kṛṣṇa, the
incarnation of Visņu, was the charioteer of
Arjuna on the battle field of Kurukṣetra.
The later Aryans saw in the wheel one
of the most powerful weapons. They
believe that the weapon held by Visnu is a
discus made of effulgence of the Sun.
Perhaps from this the Buddhists derive their
meaning of the word Cakka as weapon.
The love of the Aryans for the wheel
culminated in their concept of Cakravartin
or universal Monarch which is elaborately
described in Buddhist literature. The con-
cept might not be of Indian origin, but it
was well developed in India especially by
the Buddhists. The Cakkavatti-Sihanada-
Sutta contains the story of King Daļhanemi
who by means of his treasure of Cakka
went all over the world to proclaim his

suzerainty. To the Indians, the Universal
Monarch is the supreme head of the
world.
The wheel has thus become symbolic of
victory, power, achievement, happiness and
all that are desirable. It is sometimes repre-
sented by the swastika which as an auspi-
cious sign is most welcome in India and
other Buddhist countries. But it is not mono-
polised by the Buddhists. Brahmanism and
Jainism also have long adopted the same as
such and their adoption might even be
earlier.
Coming back to the word Dhamma-
cakka, the Buddha equates his Dhamma
287
with the wheel as he does with the eye in
Dhammacakkhu, with the drum in
Dhammabheri, with the rain in Dhamma-
megha, with the lamp in Dhamma-pajjota,
with the banner in Dhammaddhaja, etc. By
joining the word Dhamma with Cakka in
compound, the Buddha tries to elevate his
teaching to the highest position. The
Dhamma to the Buddha is what the Cakka
is to the Cakkavatti or Universal Monarch.
By the expression "turning of the Wheel
of Dhamma", the Buddha proclaimed his
suzerainty over other religious schools
and established himself as the supreme
head of the spiritual world.

နယ်တွင် နွားဆွဲသော စွပ်ဖားများကို အသုံးပြု ကရိကျွန်းတွင်ကား အိုးထိန်းစက်သည် လှည်းဘီး

သည်။ ဓမ္မသည် ဝိမုတ္တိသုခနှင့် ပြည့်စုံခြင်းကို

အရေးပါသည်ကား စကြာရတနာ လည်၍ အရပ်

ဝတေးမင်း၏ အာဏာဖြန့်ချိခြင်း၊ လက်နက်နိုင်ငံ
ထူထောင်ခြင်း ဖြစ်သည်။ ဓမ္မစကြာ လည်ခြင်း
သည်လည်း ဘုရားရှင် ဒေသနာဖြန့်ချိခြင်း၊ တရား
နိုင်ငံ ထူထောင်ခြင်းပင် ဖြစ်သည်။ တနည်းအား
ဖြင့် အယူဝါဒဂိုဏ်းဂဏ ပြား၍ ဂိုဏ်းဆရာ
ပေါများလှသော အိန္ဒိယပြည်တွင် ဘုရားရှင်သည်
ဓမ္မစကြာတရားစတင်ဟောကြားခြင်းဖြင့်ယင်းတို့

၁၇၂
ဘုရားရှင်သည် ဓမ္မစကြာ သို့မဟုတ် တရားဘီး
သို့မဟုတ် တရားစကြာကို
လှည့်တော်မူသည်ဟူ၍ သို့ထူထောင်ရာ၌ ဓမ္မစကြာ သည် စကြာ
ဆိုခြင်းမှာ မိမိထိုးထွင်းသိမြင် သော တရားကို ရတနာနှင့်တူသကဲ့သို့ ဘုရားရှင်သည်လည်း စကြ
ဟောခြင်းမည်၏ ဟု သာမန်အားဖြင့် ဆိုဖွယ် ဝတေးမင်းနှင့် တူသည်ဟု ဆိုနိုင်သည်။ ဘုရား
ရှိသော်လည်း နက်နဲစွာစဉ်းစားလျှင်မူ ထိုထက် ရှင်နှင့် စကြဝတေးမင်း တူပုံထို ဖော်ပြသော
မက လေးနက်သောသဘောကို ဆောင်ကြောင်း အထောက်အထားများကိုလည်း ပါဠိစာပေများ၌
သိမြင်နိုင်သည်။ တရားဟောခြင်းကို တရားတံခွန် ဤသို့ တွေ့ရှိရသည်။စကြဝတေးမင်းသည် ၃၂ ပါး
လွှင့်သည်။ . တရားအောင်လံ စိုက်သည်။ တရား သော လက္ခဏာတော်နှင့် ပြည့်စုံသကဲ့သို့ ဘုရား
ခရုသင်း မှုတ်သည်။ တရားစည် တီးသည်။ တရား ရှင်လည်း ၃၂
ပါးသော လက္ခဏာတော်နှင့် ပြည့်
ဆီမီး ထွန်းသည်။တရားမိုး ရွာသည်စသဖြင့်လည်း … စုံသည်။ ` စကြဝတေးမင်း၌ သားကြီးရတနာ
ဆိုနိုင်ပါလျက် ယင်းသို့မဆိုမူ၍ တရားစကြာ ရှိသကဲ့သို့ ဘုရားရှင်၌လည်း ရှင်သာရိပုတ္တရာ
လှည့်သည်ဟူ၍သာ အဘယ်ကြောင့်ဆိုရသနည်း။ တည်းဟူသော သားကြီးရတနာရှိသည်။ဘုရား
