His Majesty king Mindon desiring to
treat directly with the Queen of England
on certain problems concerning the two
countries, and especially the question (accor-
ing to and Italian secret Report) of acquir-
ing Bassein as his outlet to the sea, sent
Burmese Embassy to England. This Em-
bassy passed through Italy and was recei-
ved solemney by the King of Italy. A
number of hitherto unpublished documents
throw some light on this mission, and
supplement our Burmese sources.

The Burmese Government,
like other Governments of Asia is an
absolute Monarchy. The King 18 not only
the Head of the Government enjoying all
the Royal prerogatives, but we may say
absolute Master of the Country. Among
his numerous titles he enjoys that of
Lord of the Seas and the Earth. The
present Monarch has moderated the rigid
laws and customs of the past and though
he personally directs all the highest affairs
of state and the most minute details of
his family, nevertheless in matters of
importance He does not make a decision
without asking for the advice of His
Ministers. After the Sovereign, come the
four Chief Ministers of State, who admi-
nister the affairs of the Empire. One is
especially in charge of corresponding with
other States and corresponds to our mi-
nister of Foreign Affairs. Another has
the administration of all that regards
arms and the army and is 1 ke our Minis-
ter of War. Each Minister has under
him a Chief Secretary called Woondauk
who may be called a Minister of the
second grade. Besides this chief secretary
each Minister has several subalterns to
direct and account for the daily affairs.
Besides there are four interior ministers
who are especially in charge of the affairs
which regard more directly and personally
the Sovereign, the Queens and the whole
court and are called Atwinwoon. These
are the Ministers who can approach His
Majesty more easily, receive his orders
and communicate them to others. The
whole Empire is divided into Provinces.
In each Province there is a Governor who
has wide powers. Each village has a kind
of Mayor and a Municipality.
In the Burmese Empire there is no
regular army in the strict sense as each
Province must send a certain number of
soldiers who may change places with others

made up of about twenty thousand men
and in time of war the number may vary
from fifty to seventy thousand. The prin-

military service of those who wear the
religious robe called Talapoin and all
their relations, and in the whole Empire

The army is set up like our own, namely
with a generalissimo; each regiment having
a general, colonel, officials, sergents; cor-

The Burmese Empire is perhaps the
most peaceful in the world with few

In the prisons I have been assured

The Burmese Government has little

with the English who are trying to domi-
