SOME STONE AND BRONZE IMPLEMENTS
FROM THE TRIANGLE.
[The stone and bronze implements mentioned in this article were
presented to Sir Archibald Cochrane G.C.M.G., K.C.S.I., D.S.O., while he was
Governor of Burma. The Burma Research Society is indebted to him for
permission to reproduce the photographs. On leaving Burma, Sir Archibald
Cochrane handed over these stone and bronze implements to the Society,
in trust for a National Museum.
Editor, J. B. R. S.]
These stone and bronze implements which are from time to time dug
up in the Triangle are highly prized by the Kachins as protective charms.
Most of the specimens now obtained have been in the families of the
owners for many generations; some are of recent finding and only in
December 1939 a find was made which resulted in litigation in my Court
as to its ownership: it was found in land which was a subject of dispute
as regards ownership between two Chiefs.
The Kachins say that they have occupied the Triangle for the past
1,200 years. They have no written history but they have a very accurate
handing down by word of mouth of their history. At no time during this
1,200 years has any mention been made of the Kachins being able to
work either in bronze or stone; but the story of a race of erect ape-men
has invariably been handed down in connection with the stone and bronze
implements which have always been valued by the Kachins. The stone
implements are limited mainly to axes and divining stones though spear
heads and arrow heads are sometimes found. The specimens shown on
Plate I, figures 4 to 6, are typical axes. In Kachin these are called
Mu ningwa (lit. axes from the sky) because the Kachins believe that these
were made by the Sanam people (the ape-men mentioned above) after
their death and were showered down upon the earth by them. These axes
are invariably found in the ground so it is concluded by the Kachins that
they have fallen from a great height.

Divining stones (Plate I, figure 3) are used for ascertaining the wishes
of the innumerable evil spirits which govern the lives of Kachin Animists
and also as charms in time of war. These stones were also said to have
been made by the Sanam people. The chisels (Plate I, figures 1 and 2)
are from the same source and also come from the sky.
2. THE SANAM NI.
It might be as well to give a brief outline of these extraordinary
ape-men who are responsible for these implements. The description of
these people has been handed down in the usual Kachin manner.

the Kachin race existed. The Sanam man was erect walking but his feet

produce articles in metal and so we have these bronzes. It will be noticed
from the explanation of Plate II that all the Kachin names include the
term Sanam. The Kachin word for a ring is Lakchawp: the specimens
dug up are called Sanam Lakchawp as they were supposed to be ornaments
worn by the Sanam people.
3. BRONZES.
The bronzes are limited and very difficult to obtain. They usually take
the form of blades (Kachin Shingban a blade) of implements: or of
articles of personal decoration such as rings and bracelets. Examples of
each type known are shown on Plate II. These bronzes are also valued
by the Kachins as protective charms.
4. GENERAL.
Only a brief outline of the source of these implements is possible here.
I hope to be able to publish something more substantial later. It is
exceedingly difficult to obtain detailed information and to separate any
information obtained from the elaborate mythology of the Kachins.
