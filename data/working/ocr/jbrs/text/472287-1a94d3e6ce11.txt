THE BEGINNINGS OF WESTERN EDUCATION IN BURMA
THE CATHOLIC EFFORT
(From Italian sources)
The papers of U Kaung in the Journal
of the Burma Research Society, on "The
Beginnings of Christian Missionary Educa-
tion in Burma, 1600-1824" and "1824-53
Roman Catholic and American Baptist
Mission Schools,"
are praiseworthy
attempts to unravel the history of Western
Education in Burma.
My paper is written to supplement his
study, and give additional data from Italian
sources, which U Kaung did not have the
chance to consult. If he had only had the
chance to consult these sources, several
things would have been written by him in
another way and he would have come to
more generous conclusions.
The seeds of Western Education in
Burma were sown by the Catholic missiona-
ries. Around this group must our study
thus evolve.
Nomadic Missionary Activity
The rich Kingdom of Pegu as it was
known to the Portuguese attracted several
traders and Portuguese settlers from Mala-
ya. They came to Mergui, Tavoy, Syriam,
Bassein, Akyab etc. They befriended the

St. Paul, "founded by three Italian noblemen, Antonio Maria Zaccaria, Barthelemy Ferrari
and Jacopo Morigia. The order comes second in seniority in the Orders of Regular Clerics, the
Theatines founded in 1530. They were confirmed as an Order by the Vatican in 1535 and 1579.
In addition to the three vows of Poverty, Chastity and Obedience, they took the Fourth Vow
which bound them never to strive after any honour or religious dignity unless specially com-
manded by the Holy See. The scope of their special vocation besides preaching in general was
the EDUCATION OF YOUTH. They followed the way of life as preached by St. Paul in his
Epistles. This Order one of whose greatest attributes is TEACHING AND EDUCATING, has
produced distinguished men in every branch of Literature and Science. Learning, the pursuit of
which the Barnabites regarded as a great preservative of religious observance has always been
cultivated among them in all its branches. In archaelogy they have Cronius, whose work is
praised in Ekkels “Doctrina nummorum veterum." Then they can boast of Cortenova, who

included subjects like Latin, Greek, etc.

Western Education in Burma
Why the Bannabites Opened Schools
The Barnabites being a teaching order
it was therefore in their very essence of
existence to
open schools. Besides, the
children of the Catholics had to be educated.
The town colleges they opened were
insufficient so they opened village schools
and PAID MASTERS to instruct and
teach. It was their only hope of continuity
in Burma. It was the very lifeblood of the
young Catholic Community. The schools
were primarily meant to preserve the faith
in the youth and then to fit them for their
place in society. Chances for education
were opened to anyone who so desired.
Nobody was forced to become a Catholic.
But since it was their bounden duty
to preserve and increase the faith of
their flock they did all they could to open
vernacular schools and pay lay masters to
educate the Bayingyi children in the villages.
Father Miconi, a Barnabite writing to
his brother in 1775 says: "The greatest
good, nay the only stable good on this earth
is to educate the youth...... (he then gives
his reasons, namely for preserving their
faith)......To avoid this (relapses) they must
be taught in our churches (the church
building was also used as vernacular village
school) and for this reason we must pay the
masters and board all the children especially
if they are from another village: he who
does not do this will come to no concrete
solution."
But the documents of these past Barna-
bites and reports of the others who followed
in their footsteps show that Education was
open to everybody. The Catholic insti-
tutions which can be inspected to-day, have
carried on the tradition of teaching youth
irrespective of religion, race, or rank.
