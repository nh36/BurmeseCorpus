Editor's Note on Excavation of Old Thaton
The Archaeological Survey of Burma had conducted three field seasons in
1975, 1976 and 1977. Each season lasted about one month due to unfortunate
circumstances. Perhaps, the next coming season may last longer with the hope that
more ancient objects would come to light.
So far, two basements of brick structure and some terracotta and carnelian
beads, necks of sprinkler vessels, spouts of kettle with pouch-like bulge at the base
and fragments of carinated cooking pots had been unearthed. An old laterite
figure of Gaṇeśa, a Hindu minor god had also been brought to light from this
historical site of Suvanṇabhūmi.
The archaeologist who performed the diggings U Myint Aung writes: "The
structure takes on a square plan with each side measuring 69 feet in length. The
living space in the interiors was partitioned into 14 rooms of different magnitudes.
In conformity with its plan, a square hall was designed at the middle to be enclosed
on four sides by other rooms. Access is gained to these rooms through door-ways
from the central hall. ... And the bricks used in this building and its architectu-
ral style have jointly proposed for it a date not later than the Pagan period. . . .
They (the objects yielded) have close affinities with the similar specimens which
came from the Beikthano excavation. All these data together make an inference
possible that the site had contacts with Pyu culture". (Working people's Daily
6-5-76, The Capital of Suvaṇṇabhūmi Unearthed?)
The same author describes in his second article "Dating the Excavated Site
in Suvaṇṇabhūmi” (W.P.D. 3-6-76) as follows:- "Our excavation at Winka
(a small village at the foot of Mt. Kelasa-the site of Old Thaton) could also bring
to light some more specimens. The net-work of ancient trade-routes that had
linked the Indian Sub-Continent and the South East Asian countries may probably
throw a flood of light on the problem of the spread of this particular ceramic type
if it is meticulourly investigated. In this historical perspective, therefore, our
