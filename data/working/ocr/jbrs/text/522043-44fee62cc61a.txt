B.
Criteria used for distinguishing the
Parts of Speech in this Paper
In this paper, the parts of speech
will be distinguished according to the
affixes which can be attached to them,
sometimes according to their internal
form, or the position(s) they may occupy
in the sentence. On this basis I suggest
that there are four parts of speech in
Burmese: nouns, verbs, prepositions, qua-
lifiers. The affixes cannot be regarded
as a separate part of speech (particles),
as is done by Cornyn and Stewart, since
they cannot be placed on the same level
as full words. However, the affixes must
of course be divided into various classes
according to whether they are derivative
or inflectional, and according to the type
of word they can be attached to (see
references to Cornyn above).
From the point of view of internal
structure, the nouns may consist of single
morphemes, or of various combinations
of nouns and verbs, usually in close
juncture, forming compound nouns. The
verb may consist of a single morpheme
(e.g. ago: "go", co "come") or of two

A Final Note
The Burmese qualifiers form a large
and complex topic which I have tried to
cover in as much detail as possible. This
has been rendered particularly difficult
since there is no previous study which
has separated the qualifiers from the
nouns and verbs (see part A above). I
am well aware this paper has not exhaus-
ted all there is to say on the subject
though, it has exhausted me. However,
I have endeavoured to be as accurate and
thorough as possible. To those who may
point out errors or omissions, I can only
offer the same reply as Dr. Johnson when
he was asked by a lady why he had
given the wrong meaning for a word in
his dictionary: "Ignorance, Madam, pure
ignorance”.
