A. Introduction
Once rubber was grown as far north as Myitkyina. However, the rubber growing
industry disappeared in that area during the slump of the nineteen thirties. The expansion
of rubber areas has been quite steady in the maritime rubber growing districts, notably in
Mergui, Amherst and Thaton districts.
As of 1951, Mergui, Amherst, Thaton, Hanthawaddy, Tavoy, Toungoo and Insein
districts accounted for 99% of the country's total rubber acreage. The rest was distributed
in six other districts, namely, Pegu, Maubin, Myaungmya, Bassein, Henzada and Bhamo
districts.'
The Tenasserim division, containing Mergui, Tavoy, Amherst, Thaton and Paan
districts, accounts for about 2/3 of the total rubber area of the country. The Pegu
division, containing Pegu district, Toungoo district, Insein district and Hanthawaddy
district, has about 15% of the country's total rubber acreage. The Irrawaddy division,
which includes the rubber-producing districts of Maubin, Myaungmya, Bassein and
Henzada, accounts for but .4% of the country's total rubber acreage.
Small estates (0-50-acre estates) are found most extensively in Mergui and Amherst
districts. The 50-100-acre estates are most extensive in Mergui, Amherst and Tavoy
districts. The 100-500-acre estates are most extensive in Mergui, Paan, Amherst, Thaton,
Tavoy and Insein districts. Large estates (over 500 acres in size) are most prevalent in
Mergui, Pegu, Hanthawaddy, Thaton and Amherst districts. All the four sizes of estates,
then, are most wide-spread in Mergui and Amherst districts.
The only rubber growing district, that has rubber goods factories within its bounda-
ries, is Insein district. The district lies adjacent to the northern limits of Rangoon city.
The rubber goods factories are located near the sourthern border of the district.
With a view to improve the rubber stands of the country, particularly from the
point of view of yield per tree, the A.R.D.C. (i.e. the Agricultural and Rural Development
Corporation) has established clonal and budder rubber nurseries in Mergui, Tavoy,
Amherst, Paan and Insein districts. In 1958, the nurseries in the above districts together
contained about 1,653,000 seedlings and 10,500 buddings.

B. A District by District Account
(1) Rubber industry in Mergui District:
The main advantage, enjoyed by Mergui district, from the point of view of rubber
planting, is its proximity to the equatorial zone, the natural habitat of Heavea rubber.
The district extends equator-wards to within 10° of the equator.
Mergui district is the most important rubber producing district of Burma. The
district accounts for 27% of the country's total rubber out-put. Its rubber acreage is 1.65
times that of the next most important rubber producing district, i.e. Amherst district. The
rubber out-put capacity of Mergui district is 1.7 times that of Amherst district.
Rubber areas are most extensive around Mergui and Palaw, the largest and second
largest towns of the district respectively. They are found almost on the whole length of
the Mergui-Palaw road and on an appreciable scale on the Palaw-Palauk road. They are
present also on or in the neighbourhood of the Tenasserim river up to a distance of 50
miles from the mouth of the river. Some are located on the Tenasserim-Thabawleik road,
further south, and some on the Kawthaung-Maliwun road, still further south. Small
clusters of rubber areas are found on Kisseraing island and in the King island-Kalagyun
island area.
Between the rubber estates of the Kawthaung-Maliwun area and those, lying
further north, there is a vast gap. In that gap the only rubber area-a tiny one at that-is
found near the town-let of Bokpyin.
Post-war additions to the rubber areas of the district are fairly wide-spread over
the traditional rubber areas. New rubber areas are found specially along the Mergui-
Tavoy road. Some are found on the Tenasserim-Thabawleik road. They are found also
in the northern and southern parts of King island, northern and eastern parts of Sellore
island, north-western sector of Kisseraing island lying to the east of Kisseraing island.
Some are found on the stream to the north-east of Bokpyin and some in the neighbour-
hood of Maliwun and Kawthaung.
The rubber areas of the district are largely associated with well-drained lateritic
soils and quartzite-slate, sand-shale formations of the Mergui series. Lateritic soils are
present almost everywhere in the district.
Between 1929-30 and 1931-32, the rubber acreage of the district once reached the
43,000 level. The rubber acreage reached its lowest level, i.e. about 32,000, in 1933-34,
just after the world-wide rubber slump and the introduction of the quota scheme. Since
that period, the rubber acreage has been increasing fairly steadily. Between 1945-46 and
1959-60, the rubber acreage of the district increased by 0.2%, i.e. from 42,187 to 49,179.
In 1928-29, the district produced 11.4 million pounds of rubber or 28.5% of the
country's total rubber out-put. Between 1929-30 and 1932-33, the rubber yield of the
district reached up to the 12 million pounds' level. Setting aside the Second World War

(2) Rubber industry in Tavoy District:
Tavoy district lies to the north of Mergui district. It stretches from beyond 13° to
beyond 15° north of the equator. The district produces 8% of the country's rubber and is
the 6th. most important rubber-producing district of the state. Its rubber acreage is 1.6
times that of the next most important rubber producing district, i.e. Paan district.
In Tavoy district, rubber is grown mostly around Tavoy town, to be more exact
between Egani village in the north, Myitta village in the east, Kyangyaung village in the
south and Launglon and Maungmagan villages in the west. Post-war additions to rubber
areas are found largely between Tavoy and Wagon. Myitta and Wagon are connected
with Tavoy by a motor road. During the pre-war days, rubber plantations between
Myitta and Wagon were owned by Europeans.
Between 1941 and 1960, the rubber acreage of the district increased by 32%, i.e.
from 8419 to 11,083. In 1941, the Europeans owned 1973 acres of rubber plantations, i.e.
23% of the district's total rubber area. By 1960, however, there was no longer any
European-owned rubber estate in the district.
In 1959-60, there were 1070 rubber holdings in Tavoy district. Twenty-four percent
of the rubber area of the district belongs to the 1-10 acre estates, 16% to 10-25 acre estates,
12.7% to 25-50-acre estates, 10.5% to 50-100-acre estates, 24.2% to 100-500-acre estates and
12.6% to above-500-acre estates.
Between 1928 and 1940-41, the district's rubber out-put declined by 2%, i.e. from
2,997,120 lbs. to 2,947,840 lbs. Between the latter year and 1960, the out-put capacity
increased by about 31%, i.e. from 2,947,840 lbs. to 3,872,960 lbs.
Rubber plantations are located mostly on the Mergui series of rocks of uncertain
age or on alluvium and recent deposits. Near Egani, rubber estates are found over granite
and kindred rocks. The appearance of new rubber estates would have been on a greater
scale had the supply of high-yielding type rubber seeds or grafts been greater, or, had the
rubber planters been allowed to use ordinary type rubber seeds or grafts in establishing

(3) Rubber industry in Amherst district:
The district lies to the north of Tavoy district and stretches from about 15º Ñ.
latitude to approximately 16¼º N. latitude. It is the second most important rubber produ-
cing district in the country. It yields 16% of the country's rubber. Its rubber acreage
exceeds that of the next most important rubber producing district, i.e. Hanthawaddy
district, by 3 times. Its advantages over Mergui district lie in its nearness to Rangoon city
and possession of railway facilities.
Between 1941 and 1960, the rubber acreage of the district increased by 47%, i.e.
from 21,406 to 31,466. In 1960, there were over 500 rubber estates in the district. In that
year, all the rubber estates in the district were owned by Burmese nationals. However,
back in 1941, 6,279 acres or 29% of the total rubber area of the district, were European-
owned. European-owned rubber areas were found near Thanbyuzayat and to the north
of Chaungzon (on Bilugyun island).
Between 1928-29 and 1940-41, the district's rubber out-put increased by nearly 3
times, i.e. from 1,558,690 lbs. to 5,951,960 lbs. Between the latter year and 1960, the
rubber out-put capacity swelled by 36%, i.e. from 5,951,960 lbs. to 8,088,827 lbs.
Rubber is grown between Ye and Moulmein. Rubber estates string out along the
Ye-Moulmein railway and motor road. Some estates are located to the south-east of
Amherst and some to the south of the Mudon-Kyaikdon motor-road, stretching out from
Mudon in a south-easterly direction. On Bilugyun island, the rubber estates are found to
the north-west and south-west of Chaungzon town-let. They string out in a northerly
direction from that town-let.
Post-war-established rubber areas are found most extensively near Ye, Taungbon,
Amherst, Mudon, Moulmein and in the northern part of Bilugyun. About 600 acres of
land have been newly put under rubber in northern Chaungzone township, Amherst
district.2
Rubber areas of the district are associated with older alluvium and laterite near
Amherst. To the south-east of Mudon, however, they are found either on alluvial deposits
or unclassified rocks.
According to newspaper reports, in Mudon and Kyaikmaraw townships, the insur-
gents sometimes blackmailed those rubber plantation owners, who refused to pay them
"protection money”, by destroying their rubber trees.

(4) Rubber industry in Paan district (Karen State):
185
Paan district lies to the north and east of Thaton and Amherst districts. It produces
4% of the country's rubber and is the 7th most important rubber producing district of the
country. Though the rubber acreage of Paan district is slightly bigger than that of nearby
Toungoo district, the rubber out-put of the latter district is about 3 times that of the
former. It is so because of the poorer communication facilities and the lesser degree of
general security, associated with the former.
Rubber areas are most extensive in the southern part of the Karen State, to be more
definite, around Kya-in-seikkyi and between Kawbein and Myawadi, and, between Paan
and Zathabyin. The last-named locality lies between the Salween and Gyaing rivers.
Rubber is grown also at Hlaingbwe and near Pazungyaung, lying to the east of the con-
fluence of the Yunzalin-chaung and the Salween. It is planted also at Kadaingti, Kuzeik
and Whemon villages, all lying on the Yunzalinchaung. Rubber estates are found also at
Ywagyi and Thamodaung villages, lying far in the north. Rubber areas of the district are
generally associated with gneiss and Pliocene and Quaternary deposits.
The Kya-in-seikkyi area is located close to Thanbyuzayat, a railway town, just
outside the Karen State. A motor road joins the area with the Kawkareik area. The
Gyaing river serves as a transportation route between the Kawbein area and Moulmein.
A motor road connects the Paan-Zathabyin section with Thaton, a district headquarters
just outside the Karen State. Hlaingbwe is also connected with Thaton via Paan by means
of a motor road. Rubber from Pazungyaung, Kadaingti, Kuzeik and Whemon can be
sent by boat to Moulmein and that from Thamodaung and Ywagyi can be sent to the
railway town of Toungoo, twenty miles away, by car.
In 1941, out of 5814 acres of rubber in the district, 5372 were owned by Burma
nationals and the rest by foreigners. By 1960, the acreage, owned by the Burma nationals,
had increased to 6656. Hence, between the two years, the percentage of foreign-owned
rubber acreage declined from 8 to 6. No European-owned rubber estates have been
recorded.
Between 1928-29 and 1940-41, the rubber yield of the district increased by about
7%, i.e. from 1,632,960 lbs. to 1,744,960 lbs. and between 1940-41 and 1959-60, the yield
capacity increased further by 14%, i.e. by 253,120 lbs.
(5) Rubber industry in Thaton District:
This district lies to the north of Amherst district, stretching from about 16 to
nearly 18° north of the Equator. It is the 4th. most important rubber-producing district
of the country. It produces 14% of the country's rubber. The rubber acreage of the
district is greater than that of Toungoo district, the next district in terms of rubber out-put,
by 70%.
Between 1941 and 1960, the rubber acreage of the district increased by 9%, i.e.
from 10,451 to 11,370. In 1947-48, the district produced 6,027,840 lbs. of rubber. By

(6) Rubber industry in Toungoo District :
This district lies to the north of Thaton district and north-west of Pa-an district.
Latitudinally, it ranges from about 17 to about 191° north of the equator. Toungoo
district stands 5th. in terms of rubber yield among the rubber producing districts of the
country. Its rubber constitutes 13% of the country's rubber. Its rubber acreage is 37%
less than that of Tavoy district. However, its rubber out-put capacity is 1.7 times that of
the latter district. It is so because most of the rubber areas of Toungoo district are
British-owned and they are better maintained and operated than those of Tavoy district.
In 1950-1951, the Britishers owned 70% of the rubber acreage of Toungoo district.
The percentage of foreign-owned rubber acreage has all along been high in the district.
Between 1940-41 and 1959-60, the said percentage declined but almost imperceptively, i.c.
from 78% to 77%.
Between 1928-29 and 1940-41, the rubber acreage of Toungoo district increased by
5%, i.e. from 6624 to 6961; however, the rubber out-put decreased by about 1%, i.e. from
6,359,040 to 6,305,760. Between the latter year and 1959-60, the rubber acreage increased
by 123 (or by 2%) whereas the rubber out-put capacity increased by 429,420 lbs. (or
by 7%).
In Toungoo district, rubber is grown most extensively to the north-east of Shwe-
gyin. Some rubber estates are found also to the east, south-east and south of Shwegyin.
A few estates are found also to the north of Kyaukkyi and Pyu and to the east of Toun-
goo. The estates are associated with Quaternary deposits, Oligocene rocks and other
unclassified rocks. Some rubber plantations are located on alluvium or recent deposits.
Rubber from the southern part of Toungoo district can be sent to Rangoon or
Moulmein via the Sittang river, whereas that from the northern part of the district is best
sent to Rangoon by rail.

(7) Rubber industry in Pegu District :
187
Pegu district lies to the south-west of Toungoo district and to the west and north-
west of Thaton district. Roughly speaking, the district occupies the area between the
southern part of Pegu Yoma and the Sittang river. It extends from about 17% N. latitude
to about 181 N. latitude.
In 1937-38, the district had 597 acres of rubber. In 1945-46, rubber acreage
amounted to 629. By 1946-47, however, as a result of the out-break of insurgency, the
rubber acreage of the district declined sharply to 182. In 1950-51, the acreage went down
to 106 and by 1959-60 it dwindled to 39, with producing capacity of just 8240 lbs. of
rubber. The comparative pausity of rubber areas in the district may be in part due to the
leeward location of the district in relation to the Pegu Yoma.
To the west of Pegu town, rubber plantations are located on older alluvium and
laterite. During the pre-war days, rubber estates are found to the north-west of Pegu and
between Kawa, Tawa and Intagaw. Post-war-established rubber estates are found to the
west of Pegu.
Because of the proximity with Rangoon and railway facilities, the rubber, produced
in Pegu district, can be sent conveniently and cheaply to Rangoon.
(8) Rubber industry in Insein District:
The district lies to the west of Pegu district and north of Hanthawaddy district.
Latitudinally, it ranges from about 16° 40' to about 17° 50' north of the equator.
In terms of rubber out-put, Insein district stands 8th. It produces 2% of the
country's rubber. There is a wide gap between the rubber out-put of this district and that
of Henzada district, which stands next to it in terms of rubber production.
Between 1928-29 and 1940-41, the rubber acreage increased slightly, i.e. by about
1%. Between 1941 and 1958, the district's rubber acreage declined by 24%, i.e. from 5621
to 5254. The rubber areas which have disappeared since 1941 were mostly located between
Hlawga lake and Rangoon city. Some were located to the north of Hlawga lake. The
expansion of the urbanized zone of the Rangoon city area has been responsible for the
disappearance of rubber estates in this locality.
Nowadays, rubber areas are most extensive to the north-east, east and south-east of
Hlawga lake and also between Pugyi and Wanetchaung. There are rubber estates also
between Pugyi and Taikkyi, to the south-east of Wanetchaung and about 7 miles to the
north of Hlegu.
The percentage of foreign-owned rubber acreage declined from 77 to 72 between
the years 1940-41 and 1959-60. European-owned rubber areas are found to the north-east
of Hlawga lake and between Wanetchaung and Pugyi. Before the war, a European-owned
rubber area existed to the south-east of Hlawga lake. Since 1941, the European-owned
rubber acreage has declined by 88%, i.e. from 1959 to 231.

located. They are very close to the main national harbour town, Rangoon and its rubber
works. They have also access to both rail and road transportation facilities.
(9) Rubber industry in Hanthawaddy District :
The district lies to the south of Insein and Pegu districts. It occupies the coastal
area of the eastern part of the Delta Region and extends from about latitude 16° 20′ N. to
about latitude 17° N.
Among the rubber producing districts of the country, Hanthawaddy district is the
3rd. most important in terms of rubber out-put. It produces 15% of the country's rubber.
The main advantage, enjoyed by the district from the point of view of rubber production,
is its proximity to Rangoon city. The terrain and climatic conditions of the district,
however, are not very favourable for the rubber plant.
Though Hanthawaddy district yields more rubber than Thaton district, the next
district in terms of rubber out-put, the rubber acreage of the former is less than that of the
latter by 23%. The discrepency has arisen due to the fact that more security has prevailed
in Hanthawaddy district than at Thaton district.
Between 1928-29 and 1940-41, the rubber acreage increased from 9188 to 9231 (i.c.
by 0.5%) and the rubber out-put increased from 7,944,050 lbs. to 7,965,550 lbs. (i.e. by
3%) Between 1941 and 1960, however, the rubber acreage remained constant. So did the
the percentage of foreign-owned rubber acreage, i.e. 54%. But, the rubber out-put capacity
decreased by about 4%.
The rubber, produced in the district, has to use both land and water transporation
routes to reach Rangoon city. Twante, Dala and Syriam are transhipment points between
land and water transportation routes.

C. Conclusion
Mergui district is the most important and the most promising rubber producing
district of Burma. Nearness to the equatorial zone is the main advantage of the district.
The district produces 27% of the country's rubber. Rubber areas have appeared on the
car roads, river-side locations and sea-side locations of certain islands. Since 1933-34, the
rubber acreage of the district has increased fairly steadily. In 1959-60, there were
49,179 acres of rubber area in the district, with an out-put capacity of 13,769,280 lbs. of
rubber. In order that the rubber industry of the district may develop to the fullest extent,
it is essential that Mergui and Victoria Point become linked with a motor road via
Tenasserim.
Amherst district is the second most important district in the country. It produces
16% of the country's rubber. In the year 1960, the rubber acreage of the district amounted
to 31,466. In the same year, the district had a yield capacity of 8,088,827 lbs. of rubber.
Rubber estates are found between Ye and Moulmein, mostly on the railway or the car-
road between the two towns.

district. The district accounts for 15% of the country's rubber. The district's chief
