In the seventeenth century Catholic missionaries from Portugal, Spain, Italy and France
worked out a Roman script which was to become quocngu or the national written language
of Vietnam. The Vietnamese-Portuguese-Latin dictionary compiled by Alexandre de
Rhodes, a Jesuit scholar-missionary from France, and published in Rome in 1651 is believed
to be the earliest document in codified quocngu script. The new writing system, at any
rate, assumed an increasingly important role in the program of study provided for Vietnam-
ese youth. Nowadays quocngu has deservedly acquired the full status of a "national
language," to which is due a marked increase of literacy. Inconsistencies in this writing
system do, however, slow the beginner down a great deal. The standardization of
the orthography now in use will help avoid useless confusions, make meaningful distinc-
tions, and consequently speed up the learning process and accelerate the literacy campaign.
Since a good writing system is one that is phonemic our effort will have to be based upon a
good phonemicization of the language of Vietnam. The present paper discusses briefly the
phonemic system (vowels, consonants and tones), the syllabic structure in terms of morphe-
mics, then suggests some “spelling rules.”

2. Consonantal Phonemes
Five manners of articulation are observed.
2.1. There are first three sets of stops: fortis voice-less unaspirated (bilabial /p/,
alveolar /t/, palatal /c/ and velar /k/), fortis voiceless aspirated (alveolar /th/), and lenis
voiced unaspirated (labial /b/ and alveolar /d/).
2.2. The system has four nasals, all voiced and homorganic to the four voiceless
unaspirated stops, that is, in bilabial /m/, alveolar /n/. palatal /ñ/ and velar /7/ positions,
respectively.
2.3.
Spirants include voiceless (labiodental slit fricative /f/, alveolar groove
fricative /s/, velar fricative /x/ and glottal /h/) and voiced (labiodental /v/, alveolar /z/ and
velar /g/) ones.
2.4.
There is a voiced lateral in alveolar position /1/.

Finally there are two semivowels: /w and y/.

Special letters which are used to represent initial consonants add to the
