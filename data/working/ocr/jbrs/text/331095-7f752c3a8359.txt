Book Review
Old Moulmein (875-1880), by R.R. Langham-Carter
(The Moulmein Sun Press, 1947)
This paper-covered booklet, published with the
al of the Government of Burma and dedicated
citizens of no mean city" gives a brief account
of the town and its inhabitants with especial reference
to the period 1826-80. Probably the chapter of chief
interest to those who know Moulmein is that dealing
with some of the old families, and it is gratifying to
learn from this that the line of descent from Sir Arthur
Phayre, the most distinguished Englishman who has
ever been closely identified with Burma, was flourishing
strongly in the second generation with eight represent-
atives in the Richardson family. Otherwise the book
has little historical value. It is disconcerting, for exam-
ple, to notice two or three intances in which Morrow's
book "The Splendour of God" published in 1929, is
cited as apparently contemporary authority. Some state-
ments are of doubtful accuracy and others certainly
incorrect. However as a popular account of Moulmein,
containing much information not readily accessible
elsewhere, the work should be interesting to local
inhabitants.
