Territories et Populations des Confins du Yunnan.
Translated from the Chinese by J. Siguret. (Henry
Vetch, Peking, China, 1937, 307 pp., 4 maps.)
Tchèn Yu-k'o, Chinese author of the volume which is herein
reviewed from its French translation by J. Siguret, served for a time
as Director of the Bureau of Popular Education and Chief of the
Bureau of Propaganda of the Yunnan provincial government. The
fact that at the same time he was chairman of the Koumintang
Committee in Yunnan will explain, in a measure, the general tone
of the book.

We are told that the Japanese seizure of the Manchurian
provinces in 1931 led patriotic Chinese to examine the position of
China's frontiers elsewhere. The entire study upon which this book
is based was completed in 1933, some two years before the actual
demarcation of the Sino-Burmese frontier line in the disputed sector
by the Commission under the chairmanship of Colonel Ísling. The
rather extreme claims advanced for Chinese sovereignty in sections
which now, and for several centuries past, have been undoubted
Burmese territory are decidedly interesting. The expeditions which
surveyed the Chinese side of the boundary expressed great surprise
at the state of civilization found among the "barbares des frontieres."
The inference is that Chinese officials have not concerned themselves
greatly with the peoples living on the fringes of the Republic.
The Chinese title indicates that the book is primarily a "Study
of the Question of Frontier Territories of Yunnan." And this
would seem to be a more suitable title than that given by the
French translator. About three-fourths of the book is devoted to
Yunnan along the Burmese frontier and the remainder to the
Tibetan side. A great deal of space is taken up with descriptions
of the primitive tribes living in the frontier territories.
Mention is made of the famous "five colour map
five colour map" showing the
various frontiers claimed by China, and emphasis is placed upon its
antiquity. In short, the entire study is a sort of Chinese Gazetteer
of the frontier regions. It marks a most commendable excursion
by Chinese authorities into the wide field of China's relations with
Burma, and it is fundamental to an understanding of Young China's
growing interest in her Southwest.
J. L. C.
