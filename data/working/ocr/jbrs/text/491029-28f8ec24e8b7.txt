III.
CONSTRUCTION OF THE PLAN
The plan is drawn in five colours on
a large sheet of black parabaik paper (Main-
gaing Shan paper) measuring 7 feet 6 inches
in lenght by 4 feet 9 inches in width. It
covers the whole of Mandalay plain from
the mountains on the east to the right bank
of the Irrawaddy on the west, and from the
Chaungmagyi on the north to the Myitnge
on the south. The survey is plotted on a
rectanguler grid, with origin, strangely
enough, not in the centre of the proposed
site for the Shwemyodaw, but at the point
where the Aungbinle embankment meets
the eastern side of the U Yindaw area, on
the present Yangindaung road near Mingala-
uyin. The orientation arrow, however, is
drawn through the exact centre of the area
designed for the Shwemyodaw.
The proposed boundaries of the Shwe-
myodaw and the sites of the defensive
ditches and ramparts for the new town are
demarcated parallel to the grid lines, and so
they were constructed. Here a peculiar error
must be noted. It was obviously thought
31
that the co-ordinates were aligned accurately
east-west and north-south, and, in popular
accounts, the walls of the Shwemyodaw and
the streets, etc., of Mandalay town are always
stated to run due north-south and east-west.
But, actually, the orientation is approxima-
tely three and a half degrees in error.
No scale is indicated on the plan, but
it is possible to estimate this fairly accura-
tely from the fact that the length of the
U Yindaw area is shown as exactly twelve
grid squares. Where least distorted by.
subsequent folding and crumpling the
squares on the original plan average 1.25
inches side; the total length of U Yindaw
measured on modern survey maps, from the
Yeni Canal on the north to its southern end
near the Mandalay-Maymyo railway, is
35,165 feet; therefore the side of each grid
square is 2930 feet and the scale of the
original plan is 2.253 inches to 1 mile.
From this the scale used for the origi-
nal plotting in Burmese units may be cal-
culated easily if we make the justifiable
assumption that they were the same as those
used in actually laying out the boundaries
of the Shwemyodaw. According to the Man-
dalay Gazetteer, Volume A (1928), page 227,
the length of each wall is 2255 yards, or 6765
feet, or 600 ta of 7 taung. Therefore the
taung used must have been the large taung,
equivalent to 1.611 feet, and the side of
each grid square is 1820 taung or 260 ta.
These equivalents have been used for
calibrating the approximate scales added to
the tracing of the original plan, reproduced

could with the knowledge and means at

The two major canals, the Shwelaung
and Shweta Chaung, constructed by Bodaw-
paya to improve the irrigation of the
Lebyin and Kaing lands respectively of
the Mandalay plain, and to reinforce the pre-
existing natural water defences of Amara-
pura, are both clearly shown on Mindon
Min's plan. Apparently, in 1855, the
Shwelaung was functioning satisfactorily,
having been repaired by Shwebo Min as
recently as 1841. But much of the Shweta
Chaung required attention, despite recon-
ditioning by Shwebo Min in 1837, for
the thirteen miles of its course from
Madaya to Obo are also coloured blue
on the 1855 plan.
It is extremely interesting to note that
Mindon Min and his advisors apparently
never intended that the new town of
Mandalay should spread westwards on to
the low lying Kaing flood plain of the
Irrawaddy. The whole area assigned to
the Shwemyodaw is kept well within the
Lebyin terrace, here protected by the up-
standing residual solid rock mass of Man-
dalay Hill. It is only at the northwestern
corner, near Obo, that the draftsman's
rectilinear boundary for the outer town is
allowed to transgress across the Shweta
Chaung on to the flood plain.
It would have saved a great deal of
subsequent trouble and expense if means
could have been devised to re-open and
to maintain the Shweta Chaung at Manda-
lay as a major deep water channel of the
Irrawaddy, as it was at some not very
ancient epoch. As events turned out the
westward spread of Mandalay, across the
flood plain to the present low water river
bank, soon resulted in Mindon Min having
to commence the never ending task of
building and maintaining long protective
embankments for that part of the town.

Later, after the flood of 1886 had burst
through Mindon's northern embankment
and through the low lying part of the town
along the Shweta Chaung, it was deemed
advisable to divert the flow of the chaung
into the Irrawaddy by a dam and a sluice

Shweta Chaung within Mandalay came to
degenerate into the open stagnant cesspool
and source of disease that it is today,
instead of being developed into a “Grand
Canal" and a flowing highway of commerce.

time will come for a new comprehensive
engineering survey and a long range deve-
lopment plan to be made, to integrate
efficiently all the natural resources of the
Development Plan for the Mandalay Area
Mandalay plain and its environment; to
improve the agricultural irrigation and
drainage of the whole of the Lebyin plain
