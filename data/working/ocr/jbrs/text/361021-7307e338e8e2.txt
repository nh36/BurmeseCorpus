INTRODUCTION
Scope of the work: The description of grasses
presented in this thesis is part of an endeavour to under-
stand more thoroughly the flora of the Rangoon University
Estate. The descriptions are based on actual herbarium
material collected over a period of 18 months. For two
reasons the bamboos have been deliberately left out (1)
none of the bamboos of the campus has been in flower
for the last 18 months, that is ever since the present
investigation was started; (2) practically all the bamboos
of the campus are cultivated and the present account
deals mainly with wild species. Every effort has been
made to include all the grasses of the campus. It is
possible, however, that some of the grasses might have
been left out in view of the fact that the grasses and
rank growth of the campus are constantly being cut.
Review of the literature: While the bamboos
of Burma have received much attention from previous
workers the grasses have been neglected. Hooker's
"Flora of British India" (1897) gave the first account of
the Gramineae but is now out of date and incomplete.
McKerral (Bull. No. 20 of the Burma Agric. Dept.)
deals with some of the commoner species of fodder value.
This book is of agricultural use and is of little help to
the taxonomist. Sawyer and Daw Nyun have listed a
number of species but this list is rather incomplete
and gives no descriptions. Rhind's "The Grasses of
Burma" is about the most complete work on the Grami-
neae. His book provides the keys to the various tribes
and species, but it does not describe the different species.
Descriptions: The specific descriptions are based
on actual examination of herbarium material. Several
species were examined and the range of variations given
apply only to the specimens growing in the Rangoon
University campus. Generic descriptions are taken from
"Flora of Madras Presidency”; that of Polytrias amaura,
however, is based on Camus' description in "Flore de
1'Indo Chine" because it is not included in Fischer's

THE GRASSES
The grasses are annual or perennial herbs with
jointed stems and spiral sheathing leaves. The inter-
nodes are usually more or less elongate and hollow with
age, but the nodes are solid. The leaves consist of a
prominent elongate sheath which is split on one side;
from the opposite side springs a usually long linear blade;
and at the base of the blade there is present a more or
less annular, membranous or fimbriate organ called the
ligule. Perennial grasses are usually propagated from
stolons or rhizomes, the plants may be branched or
unbranched.
The inflorescence of a grass is compound-spicate,
racemose or paniculate.
or paniculate. The unit of such an inflore-
scence is the spikelet which in itself is a simple inflore-
scence. The spikelet ordinarily consists of several florets
arranged in two ranks on a jointed axis called the
rhachilla. At the base of the rhachilla is a pair of bracts
called the glumes-which envelope more or less the
cluster of florets. At the base of each floret is insert-
ed another pair of bracts -oue, the lemma, in front of
the floret, the other, the palea, at the rear (between the
floret and the rhachilla). Actually the floret is seated
in the axil ot the lemma, which is therefore a floral
bract. The palea usually invests the floret more or less
tightly; morphologically it belongs to the perianth. This
is the situation in general, but often the upper lemmas
of a floret are empty or rudimentary, or both the lemma
and the palea are absent.
The florets are bisexual or sometimes unisexual
and the species monoecious or dioecious. Stamens usu-
ally 3, occasionally 2, Stigmas elongate and feathery,
usually 2. At the base of the ovules are 1
1-3 minute
structures known as the lodicules, which are believed
to belong morphologically to the perianth. The fruit
(grain) of the grasses is a caryopsis.

to about 6,000 feet. It is a very satisfactory and nu-
tritive fodder. It is the most commonly used lawn-
grass in Burma as it makes very good carpets. The
fresh juice of this grass acts as an astringent. It is

rudimentary upper lemma. In plants of the Rangoon
University Estate the rhachilla was joint at the base
of the lemma and not produced beyond, a condition
which is in conformity with the description of Hooker.
