THE FIRE PISTON
Various methods of fire-making are in use among the tribes of the
Northeast Frontier, the Shan and Karenni States. The methods employed
can conveniently be grouped under three heads, wood friction, percussion
and air compression. The wood friction methods comprise the fire saw, fire
plough and the fire drill and the percussion method (flint and steel) and
are so well known that no description is needed. These methods are known
and are used to varying extent in the three areas mentioned.
The production of fire by air compression is achieved by means of the
fire piston and depends for its working on the development of heat by the
sudden compression of air in a confined space. The accompanying illustra-

The piston is inserted into the mouth of the
cylinder which is held firmly in the left hand.
The flat end E is then given a sharp blow with the
palm of the right hand and this drives the piston
into the cylinder as far as it will go. The air is compressed and explodes
and ignites the lichen, the piston is then withdrawn and it is found that the
lichen is burning. From the lichen it is an easy matter to transfer the fire
to some other larger substance.
The interest in this fire piston lies in its geographical and racial distribu-
tion. It has been found in Indo China in use among the Kha of Tonkin
and among the Moi of Laos. On the Northeastern Frontier and in the
Northern and Southern Shan States I have found it used by Kachins alone.
Among the Nung, Lisaw and Daru tribes in Myitkyina district it is not
known and the Shans, Palaungs, Taungthus and other tribes of the Shan
