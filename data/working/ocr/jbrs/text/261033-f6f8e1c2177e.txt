U Htaung Bo's Rebellion
The story of U Htaung Bo current in the Lower Chindwin district may
not be accurate but is perhaps picturesque enough to deserve record. His
father was U No, of Kyaukhmaw near Salingyi in the Pagyi Wunship, who
was appointed by King Bodawpaya to be taik-oke of the three taiks (Sin-gyit,
Mahadan, and Twindin) and ninety-nine headmen of Pagyi, with headquar-
ters at Salingyi. They collected a tax of one rupee per house called chin-mi-
thwe-gye, which he and some of the headmen took to the capital. On his
deett, his eldest son, U Gya Yoe, succeeded him hearing of dacoits at
Amyin on the further side of the Chindwin, he began to cross the river near
Saka in pursuit, but was shot and died soon after, his post being given to the
younger son, U Htaung Bo.
The later is said to have fought against the British round Rangoon in
1824, to have become the first Pagyi Taik Wun in 1837, and Pagyi Mingyi
in 1839. His title was Maha Thiha Thura. When Prince Tharrawaddy rebelled
in 1837, "he vowed to make Ava, in which he had passed so many unhappy
years, a heap of ruins, and left in charge of it one Moung Thoung Bo, the
Pa-kyee Won, a notorious robber; the Myo-Won then privately advised the
foreign merchants to leave the town, acknowledging his inability to protect
them or to make restitution for any losses." (British Burma Gazetteer 1.351).
They had been at school together and had been tattooed at the same time
to make them invulnerable. The reign of his old school-mate was U Htaung
Bo's heyday. He acquired vast tracts of land in Pagyi, which were confiscated
after his death, and have been known till recent times as U Htaung Bo's
escheat. From 1839 to 1842 he was engaged in building the magnificent
pagoda, which is still called after him, and which lies to the north of the road
that runs from Salingyi to Saton on the Chindwin. At the opening cermony,
people came from all parts of Pagyi and from elsewhere and formed a huge
encampment round the shrine. It became, as it still is, the chief pagoda in
Salingyi, and it was there that the Pagyi levies held their Aung Mingala
ceremonies at the outbreak of the third Anglo-Burmese War.
By 1845, Tharrawaddy Min was too insane to rule any longer, and the
Pagan Prince took steps to assume the Kingly power, and it was in these
troubled times that the Pagyi Wun and his family thought fit to start their
rebellion. U Htaung Bo went to the capital in 1843, with his son U Kathè
the Myowun of Taloke (Myingyan), and U Shwe Doke the Mindon Myowun
his nephew, and stayed there. Two years later, U Kathè and U Shwe Doke.
played a game of cards with a Minister of the court and rooked him, and he
was so indignant that be managed to get them put in jail for a while. U
Htaung Bo was furions, and when they were released, all three returned to
Salingyi without the king's consent. The king sent him orders to send back
his wife and his kyauk-si dah if he were faithful, but U Htaung Bo refused.
That is the story heard in Salingyi, but it is not mentioned in the Konbaung-
set Yazawin, from which most of the following account of the rebellion is
derived. By that version, the king sent soldiers to arrest some men in the

Yaw country, but they were disarmed and all their properties seized by the
Mindon Myowun, who refused to deliver them up, and was joined in his
contumacy by U Htaung Bo and the Taloke Wun and the latter's younger
brother, and seven thousand men. An expedition to bring them to submission
was decided on at Ava. Among the officers were the Letwè Winhmu, and the
Shwepyi Hmangin Bo, in charge of soldiers from the Alon Jurisdiction, and
the Pakhangyi Myo Bo, Shwedon Pènin, and Amyin Nga Myo Wun, leading
detachments from Pakhangyi, Myingyan, and Amyin. In supreme command
were the Sewinhmu and the Taungdwingyi Myo Wun. They crossed the
Chindwin near Amyin and met with strong resistance outside the fortifica-
tions which the rebels had made around Salingyi, and the royal levies were
routed and dispersed into villages south-west of the town. On hearing the
news, the authorities at Ava send reinforcements from Kani, the Yaw coun-
try, and Tilin, two to three hundred in number. The king also sent presents
to the two Generals, a ruby ring and a ring set with a cat's-eye stone each.
U Htaung Bo was routed in June and fled with ten followers, who betrayed
him and killed him near Pyapon Taung, and sent his kyauksi dah and clothes
and insignia and other property to the Taungdwingyi Myowun. Two hundred
of his men were captured, and over forty of the leaders were executed by
order of the two Generals, including the Wun's son U Kathè, and other rela-
tives. In the version current at Salingyi, U Htaung Bo's intention was to
escape to the Chin Hills or Manipur. When they reached Magyitataung, his
men Maung Gyi and Maung Ngè decided to murder him and seize his belong-
ings. He was therefore seized, and shot at with muskets, and cut with dahs,
but he wouldnot die; they then pulled his intestines out, but he still lived.
Being by then rather tired of life, he told them that the only way was to give
him betel to eat and water to drink, which they did, whereupon he died. U
Htaung Bo's head was cut off, and was taken to Ava, and the knowledge
that his invulnerable comrade had been killed is said to have been the last
straw in driving Tharrawaddy Min mad.
U Htaung Bo had a wife called Ma Hla Me, and it was she who had a
huge recumbent figure of Buddha made and placed in a small rock-cut temple
at neighbouring Powundaung. It is still to be seen there, and is perhaps the
biggest statue in the caves. He had another wife named Ma Ta, who had
been a minor queen and who had been given him by the king. When the
king learnt of U Htaung Bo's intended treason, he ordered him to send back
Ma Ta, but unavailingly.
The best known son was U Kathè, the Taloke Myowun and Thenat Bo,
whose title was Minhla Thihathu, and another son had the title of Minhla
Zeyathu. U Htaung Bo's first cousin was U Win Gyi, who was his agent in
the Pagyi country, and this man's son was U Shwe Doke, the Mindon Myo-
wun, who also was killed in the rebellion. U Htaung Bo's grave is still to be
seen under a large tree near Magyitataung, which lies between Salingyi and
Powundaung.
