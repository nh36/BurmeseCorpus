INTRODUCTION
THE plants of an inhabited area fall into two categories, wild and
cultivated, In all, I have collected or have record 28 wild and 18.
cultivated species. Only 4 of these, 1 wild and 3 cultivated, are fern
allies all belonging to the Lycopdiales. There is no available literature
on fern allies for this area and so I have no means of ascertaining
whether they have been mentioned in reports before or not. I have
been unable to work out the species of two.
Since compiling the original thesis, I have discovered 3 more
species of ferns in Rangoon and have found reports on another. These
are included in the above totals. Of those previously reported I was
unable to Find 3 wild and 4 cultivated species. Of those found by me,
there is no reports in the available literature on the ferns of Burma of
9 wild and 7 cultivated species for this area.. Of the ferns collected I
have been unable to key 3 down to their species, to identify two
others and I am doubtful about the name of one.
In previous reports, Cyclophorus (Pyrrhosia) Lanceolatus and
Cyclophorus (Pyrrhosia) spissus are taken as two species. According
to the various authors. I have read they are synonyms of Pyrrhosia
lanceolata. The very few specimens I found of Davallia solida var.
Figiensis in the wild are probably escapes of the cultivated pants of
previous mention. Pityrogramma calomelanos is more under
cultivation than growing wild in Rangoon and it is probable that the
wild species of earlier reports were also escapes.
The cultivated pteridophytes of this area are mainly of
horticultural value. The edible species are not cultivated but are

The distribution of cultivated species depends on their popularity
with the public, and upon their adaptability to the enviromental
conditions to which they are introduced. Lycopodium sp. is imported
from Mergui for sale in Rangoon. It has also been successfully grown
in some gardens as a potted plant. Selaginella plana. Adiantum
trapeziforme A. capillues- veneris, Nephrolepis acuta and N.
exaltatavar. bostontensis are favourites in gardens because they are
both decorative and adaptable to the conditions of this area. The
remaining cultivated species are not as common as these. Davallia
solida var. figiensis is the only epiphyte that is reported as cultivated
here.
The wild species are found under a variety of ecological
conditions. Selaginella ciliaris, Lygodium flexuosum, Cheilanthes
belangeri, Adiantum philippense, Hemionitis arifolia and Cyclosorus
parasiticus var. subpubescens are common terrestials, almost
ubiquitus in areas where drains and cuttings are neglected and
overgrown. L flexuosum, C. belangri and philippense are by far the
commonest. The remaining terrestial, Ophioglosum, pedunculosum.
Petris pelluciad, P. cretica, Cheilanthes tenuifolia and Cyclosorus sp.
are comparatively rare. The two unidentified species are also rare
terrestials. Stenochloaena palustris roots in the soil close on watere's

holding roots. Cyclosorus parasilioas var. sulpubescens and

The following desriptions of the ferns and fern allies of the area
covered by this thesis are brief for cultivated species, but are as full as
is necessary for taxonomic purposes for the wild ones. They are
compiled from personal observation both in the field and in the
laboratory and from extensive research in the available literature.
1. LYCOPODIUM SP.
Stems erect to straggling, branching dichotomous, pubescent
rigid, green, Leaves dimorphic, microphyllous, 8-to 10-ranked, the
sterile ones linear, adnate to the stems below pubescent crowded to
irregularly whorled, the solitary midvenis dorsally prominent below
fading towards the apices the sporophylls in distinct terminal strobili
ovate-lanceolate, sessile, fimbriate, homosporangiate, the apices
narrowing to one or two elongate cells; sporangia briefly stalked,
adnate to the bases of the sporophylls on the upper surfaces,
subglobose, valvate spores globose, without perispore.
This species is not described in any of the available literature. It is
indigenous in the Mergui district, growing in wet habitats. It is
brought into Rangoon for sale on the market. It is being successfully
grown as a potted plant as well in some Rangoon gardens.
