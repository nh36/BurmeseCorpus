INTRODUCTION.
Onion seed is black, small in size and very irregular in shape.
The basal end of the seed, which is the point of attachment to the
fruit, is somewhat pointed. The embryo is rather slender and curved
and found embeded in the endosperm. The radicle is situated towards
the basal end. Some seed may have two embryos.
Onion seeds are able to germinate as soon as they are matured
on the plant. They do not seem to have any period of dormancy. A
seed is said to have germinated as soon as the radicle has emerged
from the seed coat. The time required for the completion of ger-
mination varies with different species. Seeds of some species may
begin to germinate a day after sowing and complete the process within
a week. Seeds of other species may take a much longer period. In
the case of onion it was found that the seed starts germinating after
about 1-3 days, depending upon the source of the seed, and com-
pletes the process in about 10 days. It is always desirable to have
all seed germinating as early as possible in the soil, as undue delay
may lead to the rotting of the seed leading to a lower and a gappy
stand of the crop after the 1st sowing unless the gaps are subsequently
filled up, which may not be possible sometimes. Various seed treat-
ments are known which tend to hasten the germination of different
species.
Seeds of different kinds of plants will germinate in either light or
darkness, but some show partiality for one or the other condition.
Light is said to interfere with the germination of some species of onion
but no such influence was observed with the material under study.
On the other hand exposure to light gave a slightly higher percentage
of germination.
Longevity is a variable factor in any seed. Seed of lotus is
known to retain its viability for 150 years, the seeds of legumes which
have hard coats remain viable upto about 50 years. Most agricultural
seeds are vital for 10 years or so. Onion seed, on the other hand,
loses its viability very rapidly after a storage of few months. Onion
seed under proper storage conditions shows a germination of 20-30 %
at the end of a year.
The deterioration in the viability of onion seed is rather rapid.
It actually starts after 3 months storage. The exact cause of this
deterioration is not known. It is said that death of seeds may be due

One of the factors known to be associated with the loss in via-

in sealed containers at lower temperatures retain their viability for
longer periods than those stored in open containers. It has been
found that the loss in viability of groundnut seed in Burma, as stored

Penicillium between the cotyledons and thereby causing the rotting of
the embryo. Groundnut seed properly air-dried and stored in air-
tight containers retained its viability during the season and was free
from any infection.
Regarding the effect of the plant or the environmental factors at
the time of seed production in relation to its subsequent germination
and the period of viability, the literature is very scarce. It seems that
one or the other or the combination of both does influence at least
the germination of the onion seed.
EXPERIMENTAL.
It may be mentioned here that the work done so for is of pre-
liminary nature only. The object of the experiment which is now in
progress includes the study of the following :-
-
1. The method of hastening the germination of the seed.
2. The causes leading to loss in viability with time.
3. The most suitable medium for the germination of the seed.
A few preliminary trials on the lines proposed above were carried
out with a view to find out the most suitable methods for detailed
study. Freshly obtained seed from Kyaukse was used in the trials,
unless otherwise stated. These trials have given some indications
which are enumerated below.
A. Treatment of seed with hydrogen peroxide.
Treatment of seed with varying volumes of hydrogen peroxide
(100 vols. equavelent to 30% W/v H₂O) was carried out. It was
found that treatment with 30 vols. H₂O for one hour hastened the
germination of the seed. The treatment also gave a higher germina-
tion percentage over the untreated seed, but the general condition of
the seedling was rather poor. Treatment with 15 vols. and 7.5 vols.
for one hour also slightly hastened the germination but did not show
any superiority in the germination percentage. When seed treated
with 10 vol. HO. for six hours was sown in pots containing loam
or sand, the germination percentage was higher in pots containing
loam. The treated seed however, had lower germination percentage
than the untreated seed.

B. The causes leading to loss in viability.
It has already been mentioned that the loss in viability of ground-
nut seed in Burma is due to the entry of some fungi between the
cotyledons of the seed and thereby causing the rotting of the embryo.
A test was therefore carried out to find out if similar infection
existed in onion seed.
The seed was obtained from a local dealer who supplies seed to
the growers. The seed had not been stored in any air-tight container.
The age of seed was said to be between 7 and 8 months.
100 seeds taken at random were soaked in a 0.01% mercuric
chloride solution for one minute, washed in 3 changes of sterile distilled
water and finally transferred to tubes containing Quaker-oats medium
and incubated at room temperature. Each tube contained one seed
only. After an interval of 10 days the examination of seed for fungal
and bacterial infection was carried out. It was found that 7 seeds
were infected with Aspergillus niger, one with Rhizopus nigricans and
7 with bacteria. Thus it is seen that 15% seed were infected. The
germination percentage of untreated lot was 21. Assuming that in
the untreated lot 15% were infected, the failure of the remainder
64% seed to germinate could not be attributed to any fungal or bac-
terial infection. The reasoon of the loss in viability of the seed must
therefore lie somewhere else. Majority of the ungerminated seed on
examination were found to be devoid of endosperm or embryo or both.
C.
Medium for the germination of the seed.
Trials were carried out with a view to finding out a most suitable
medium for the germination of onion seed. The media used were
sand, loan, burnt paddy husk, and saw-dust in pots. In one series
the seed was surface sown and in the other slightly below the surface.
Alongside, seed was sown on moist wool-paper in a petri-dish. Loam
gave the highest germination percentage in each trial. There was no
appreciable difference in the behaviour of other media. Seed sown on
wool-paper germinated poorly. The behaviour of wool paper which
appears to have exercised some kind of depressing effect upon the
germination of seed is not understood. Method of sowing of the seed
-surface sown or below the surface-did not indicate any preferential
response.
D. Miscellaneous Study.
During the course of the study it was found that when seed soaked
in water for about 24 hours was pressed between a pair of forceps, in
certain cases radicle emerged readily and in other cases it did not come
out, remaining inside the seed and adhering to the endosperm. A test
was therefore carried out to determine if any relationship existed
between such behaviour of the radicle and the percentage germination
