Mandalay to Chandernagore
Whenever there is bloodshed there is
weeping and Burma wept when Prince
Kanaung, the Heir to the throne was
put away by his two young nephews,
Prince Myngoon (×) and Prince Myngoon
Dine, aged twenty three and sixteen
respectively.
It is not my aim to judge the facts,
but only to give my readers a perspective
of the documents relating to this 'affair'
which are preserved in Paris and leave
it to them to meditate and to draw their
own conclusions.
The
Prince Myngoon in a Memorial rela-
ting to his legitimate claim to the throne,
speaks of the inexperience of the young
princes who SO to say changed the
History of Burma by their act.
Memorial explaining this affair, written
for the Prince by a friend and subscribed
by the Prince is appended to this first
part of my paper for the benefit of my
readers.
We read in history books of Mr
Sladen's advice to the two young princes
to take refuge in Rangoon from their
Father's wrath. A royal steamer happened
to be near by and it was commissioned
for
the two young Princes and their
followers.
By throwing themselves into
the hands of the English by accepting
their hospitality, the young and inexperi-
enced princes showed their lack of
political judgement.
Phayre treated them royally in Ran-
goon and King Mindon's friend Bishop
Bigandet did all he could to alleviate
their home-sickness. The Princes did not
however have time for idle musing as
the numerous visits from their Rangoon
'subjects' nostaligic for the days of Bur-
mese monarchy kept them busy. These
people had not for otten the wars of
1826 and 1851. The great pagoda festivals
graced by royalty, the triumphal marches
of the royal armies, the conqueror's tales
of Siam, ambassadors coming to and fro
in golden boats from Mandalay, their
velvet clad retinues and glistening salwes
remained still fresh in the eyes of the
older generation.
Suspicions were gradually aroused by
the continual coming and going of
Southern notables. The people wanted
their Princes to live royally and offered
them gifts in money and jewels so that
they could live as of old, generous to
their visitors, charitable to the monks,
and munificient to the most needy of their
worshippers.

The suspicions of the English authori-
ties of Rangoon, the increased number
of guards the additional spy service around
the Princes dwelling convinced the Prince
to leave his young brother in Rangoon and
disguised as a merchant, undertake a long
and dangerous journey into the Shan and
Karenni country.
The Sawbwa of Chiengmai received
the Elder Prince of Burma royally but
fearing the interference of Bangkok the
prince moved on to Mone'. There also
he was the centre of attraction of the
Chiefs and Sawbwas and King Mindon
fearing another rebellion sent an army to
pacify the Chiefs.
Meanwhile the younger Prince had
been banished to the Andamans and later
on to India proper. The Prince now
made a decision which exiled him for
life. He headed for Rangoon, was wel-
comed outside Rangoon, at Kemmendine,
precisely by a handful of young admirers,
and arrested by Colonel Duncan in the
name of the Chief Commissioner and
tried.
In this affair of Prince Myngoon,
the English authorities of Rangoon, acted
like other Governments, which wanted to
free themselves legally from an adversary
or a troublesome guest. Junior police
officers are often used. These in order to
have a quick advancement manifested their
zeal by giving reports which are only half
true or entirely false, but which are always
in accordance with the secret wishes of
the authorities. They enter into close
contact with the person in question, listen
to all he says, take a note of his visits,
comment his actions, and all this data
carefully manipulated make up a heap of
presumptions which are enough to justify
an arrest or a trial. Discontented domes-
tics, intriguers always in the company of
wandering Princes, were also found in
the household of Prince Myngoon.
During his trial, the Prince made an
intelligent defense of his movement and

the Western methods of Government. He

Their attempts at negotiation were
inspired by the declining health of their
Royal Father suffering from dysentry. If
the Princes abdicated, public opinion in
Mandalay, already manipulated by English
agents would have accepted the situation
of annexation, so much clamoured for by
the Chambers of Commerce with less pain.
Prince Myngoon discovered the plot, and
throwing away all his English books,
vowed never more to study that language
and thought only of escaping in order to
ascend the throne one day.

his resolve to escape from Benares as soon

In 1879 King Mindon died. We all
know from History the story of King
Thibaw's accession to the throne.
The partisans of Prince Myngoon sent
him considerable sums of money in the
form of jewels and precious stones, brace-
lets, chains, earrings, gold ingots in order
to help him have the means to reach the
shores of Burma and regain the throne.
His first plan was to land on the coast
of Arakan but this would make him
traverse a large part of India overland and
then he would have to hire a boat in
Calcutta and that would be most danger-
ous.
He understood the political situation

negotiations on Egypt, Tunisia, and above
all the negotiations for the zones of
influence in South East Asia. Myngoon
decided to flee into French territory, from
where with the official or tacit support of
the French Government he could vindicate
his throne. In these conditions, Chander-
nagore would be his first step on his long
journey to Mandalay.
The Prince proclaimed himself the
lawful heir to the throne of Burma,
refused abdication and awaited the day
when he could get into French territory.
The Prince knew several Parsi mer-
chants who often came to transact the
buying and selling of precious stones
with him. One of them was less avari-
cious than the rest and the Prince asked
his help to flee from Benares. One day
a coolie accompanied the merchant to
the Prince's residence and there was a
change of clothes and the disguise was
perfect. A few hours on the steamer
brought the fugitive to Chandernagore.
where the Prince asked for political
asylum from the French.
The French Administrator was greatly
embarassed. He telegraphed to Pondi-
cherry for instructions. Only four days
after his evasion did the Viceroy know
of what had happened and orders were
passed to arrest the prince at any price.
Police were on guard at every station
and the river was closely watched.
Paris did not want its laws of
hospitality to be violated and refused
for the moment to give up the Exile
since he could be of some use in the
negotiations for the zones of influence
in the Indochinese Peninsula.

Prince Myngoon Mentha
Prince Myngoon Mentha is the eldest
surviving son of Mend Doon Meng the
late King of Burma. He was the prime
instigator in an insurrection directed
against his father's authority at Mandalay
in August 1866. and with the assistance
of his younger brother, Myngoon Dyne
(now under surveillance at Benares) he
succeeded in killing the King's brother
who was then the heir apparent.
So great was the danger at Mandalay
during the insurrection that the European
and other foreign Residents, including
Captain Sladen, had to leave the Country.
After three months the revolt was
suppressed and Myngoon Mentha with
his brother sought refuge in British
territory and surrendered themselves to
the Authorities who sent them to Rangoon,
As however it was ascertained that the
elder Prince was concerting fresh intrigues
with a view of future operations again t
his father, he was placed under restraint,
but he managed to escape in February
1867, and immediately headed a new
rebellion against the King.
This attempt also failed, and in
August 1868 he was captured in British
territory where he was endeavouring to
forment a rising. He was consequently
detained as a political prisoner under the
provisions of Regulation III of 1818 and
deported first to Port Blair, then to the
Chunar fort, and finally (in 1870)
to Benares, where he and his younger
brother, who had been allowed to join
him, were kept under mild surveillance,
each be.ng allowed an allowance of 300
rupees per mensem by the British Govern-
ment. During his earlier stay at Benares,
unsatisfactory reports were received of
the Elder Prince's conduct but subse-
quently he improved, and until quite
recently no complaints were made of his
behaviour. In December last the Prince
quite unexpectedly effected his escape
from Benares and sought an asylum in
Chandernagore where he is still residing.
He has so far declined to surrender himself
to the British Authorities.
He has recently memorialized the
Viceroy asking for release and permission
to return to Burma where he is confident
of success in gathering adherents and
in subverting in his favour the present
administration.
In reply he has merely been informed
that if he leaves Chandernagore and
surrenders himself to the British authori-
ties any requests he may wish to make
will be considered. He has also been
informed that he may communicate his
wishes to an officer of the Government
at Chandernagore.
