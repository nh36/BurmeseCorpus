With the kind encouragement of the
Revolutionary Government and assistance
from the UNESCO, I have been able to
research on several documents lying in
private and public archives of historical
and diplomatic interest for Burma.(¹)
In this centenary year of Gallo's
manuscript, I have been able to visit the
little town where the famous personage
called the "Man in the Iron Mask" was
said to have been imprisoned. It is in
this town that I discovered Gallo's manus-
cript: STORIA DEL CRISTIANESIMO
NELL' IMPERO BARMANO PRECU-
DATA DALLE NOTEZIE DEL PAESE.
His manuscript was begun in Moul-
mein on the 15th August, 1845, a propiti-
ous date for the Oblates as it is the feast
of the Assumption of the Virgin. The
manuscript was completed on the 8th
February 1859. These dates are inserted
in his own handwriting after the words
'begun' and 'completed'. When he had
his loose manuscript bound and ready for
printing he put the words 'Moulmein 1846'
on the title page. On examination I have
concluded that his Moulmein manuscript
was completed in 1846 while the entire
manuscript of three volumes was termi-
nated in Europe in 1859. His long manu-
script papers with loosely written words
number 155 comprising about 3000 neatly
written but tiny words. His introduction
is made in 41 pages and there are 16 pages
of appendix. The additional letters
Contributed for publication.
** Corresponding Member, Paris, France,
appended to the printed volumes come
from the Barnabite archives in Rome and
I have been able to examine them also.
Bound into his manuscript there is a
large map of Burma, 2' by 1', drawn and
coloured by a fellow Piedmontese Oblate
in Moulmein in the original but reproduced
and perfected by lady Mary, Marchioness
Fassati né De Maistre, in Pinerolo in 1858.
It is termed CARIA COROGRAPHICA
of the Burmese Empire His manuscript
was also illustrated with six oil paintings
and a photograph of the Oblate Bishop of
Moulmein, Bishop Balma, but map, illus-
trations, and photograph were not included
in the printing as the venture would have
become too costly.
Gallo's illustrations in oils are bright
and colourful with the merriness of the
Burmese sunlight and the flambouyance
of our flora. They are however his com-
positions with designs from the books of
other voyagers to Burma who had left
written accounts. However, his illustra-
tion of the giant moth or butterfly
BOMBIX ATLAS which he says that he
captured near bis Church in Moulmein is
his own drawing and colouring: Una delle
qualin presi io stesso in Maulmain presso
la Chiesa ritrassi diligentamente a
colori." Among his manuscripts I have
discovered his jottings on the VAMPIRE
OF MOULMEIN. His description of this
giant Flying Fox is very detailed and he
made a pencil sketch of it.

The Andreino Manuscript
While going through the third volume
of Gallo's manuscript I have discerned
the abruptness of his style and any reader
would wonder why an Oblate so prolific
on the Barnabite period should be so
summary about the period of his own
order. But death and sickness choose
not their victims. What Gallo left incom-
plete Andreino a brother Oblate tried to
complete. This hitherto unpublished
manuscript is now in Rome and contains
several copies of edicts and judge-
ments of the Burmese Kings in good
Burmese writing, the history of the
mission of Burma entwined with the
political and military happenings of the
great Burmese Empire. There are also
chapters on the Anglo-Burmese wars and
the diplomatic missions of the oblate

On the cover it is noted that Cortenovis came to Rangoon in 1772 and died in the land
of his adoption in 1802 at the age of 70. Going through his diaries I understood his
burning passion for the natural history of Burma, a passion which took up all his
energy and which he expressed with contrition to his superior in Rome in a letter: “I
went mad after the natural history of Burma and it was my most beloved occupation,
and it is still a strong temptation for me..." Seeing that he was aging and soon he would
have to leave his beloved study of the flora and the fauna of Burma he wrote that he was
giving himself to a life of prayer and apostolate in which one could find a more dura-
ble satisfaction then discovering the most hidden secrets of nature. He then concludes:
Oh how many years have passed; Oh, how old we become without noticing it..."

In passing we may state that Don Paolo
Abbona was the eminence grise of King
Mindon and his trusted diplomat. Father
Andreino was the brother of the Italian
consul Andreino. Father Andreino's
manuscript came by accident into the
Barnabites archives. A Barnabite from
Afghanistan passing through Burma was
presented with some Italian documents
which though the Burmese Mission in
Mandalay concluded wrongly as belonging
to them really belonged to the Oblate
archives. There are also chapters on the
Myngone Mintha's palace rebellion and
the death of the Einshemin and other
details which are useful for reassessing
the statements of British and American
historians of Burma.
Other Gallo manuscripts
117
G llo left us several letters and the
diary of his journey out to Burma all
bound in book form with map illustra-
tions of his trip. He entitled this manu-
script: JOURNEY FROM TURIN TO
THE EAST INDIES THROUGH EGYPT.
Judging from the index he planned to
consecrate the second part of this manu-
script to Burma. This latter part remained
either incomplete or taken and never
replaced by an interested oblate. There
are several printed Burmese books and
perfectly shaped and wonderfully designed
black parabaiks in his collection. These
have been photographed for the interested
reader.
Gallo's printed book is very rare
now and in this centenary, it may well
be read with great interest and profit.
