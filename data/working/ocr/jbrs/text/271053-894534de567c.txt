ECONOMIC GRID FOR X-RAY INVESTIGATION OF
THORACIC AND ABDOMINAL CONDITIONS.
I have devised a simple grid which I believe will be of use to
Radiologists and students of X-ray Anatomy and X-ray Pathology
generally. It enables two photographs to be taken on one film and
when in use it is simply suspended behind the ordinary fluorescent
screen. It has a wide range of utility. Grids of various kinds
have been in use for a number of years as an aid to Screen
examinations and also in the well-known Potter-Bucky diaphragm.
I used the present type of grid first in France during the Great
War and subsequently in India, as an aid to the study of
diaphragmatic and bronchial movements. Great improvements have
been made in X-ray apparatus and it is even more useful now than
in the days when long exposures were necessary.
DESCRIPTION OF GRID.
The special grid (figs. I and II) consists of a case of three-
ply wood into which fits in envelope fashion, a lead grid suitably
mounted on ply wood as depicted in figure II. The grid is
capable of lateral or vertical movement as desired. The lead
strips are 0.7 c.m. (4") apart. Metal arms enable it to be.
suspended from the back of the screen in two positions.

METHOD OF USE IN THE GRID-VERTICAL POSITION.
The part having been centred in the usual way and using the
ordinary double fluorescent screens a first photograph is taken.
The grid is then moved just less than 0.7 cm. (1") in a horizontal
plane and a second exposure is made.
RESULT.
The developed film then contains two photographs (both on
the single film) and therefore has two representations of a moving
organ or structure. Thus in the grid vertical position we may
record:
-
(a) Rib movement.
(b) Diaphragmatic movement.
(c) Bronchial movement (lipiodol).
(d) Heart movement (gross movement).
(e) Any radiopaque structure that moves at right angles
to the vertical plane.
METHOD OF USE IN THE GRID-HORIZONTAL
POSITION.
The grid is now placed in the horizontal position and a first
photograph is taken with the grid at rest. The grid is then
dropped rather less than 0.7 c.m. (4") and a second exposure is
made.
RESULT.
The developed film as in the grid vertical method, reveals two
exposures on one film. Using the grid-horizontal method we may
record:-
(a) Heart movement.
(b) Bronchial movement.
(c) Any radiopaque structure that moves at right angles
to the horizontal plane.

In routine work I believe the investigation of and the
permanent recording (on one film) of the range of movement of
the diaphragm in cases of suspected liver abscess will have a special
appeal in the Tropics. Again a record of rib movement should
prove valuable in certain diseases of the chest. For special work
I believe that the displacements of the normal and abnormal heart
during inspiration and expiration in normal and abnormal subjects
might suitably be recorded with the aid of this simple grid. For
an investigation of diastoli and systoli it is less satisfactory.
In investigating the effects of respiration on the position of the
heart and roots of lungs the value of oblique and lateral positions
must not be forgotten.
The following conditions also suggest a field for investiga-
tion:
1. Kidney movement (during respiration and possibly in
displacement).
2.
Stone in the kidney or calcified abscess.
3. Abscess in the thorax.
4. Foreign body and its relation to a moving structure
such as a rib, the diaphragm, heart, kidney, etc.
5. Gall bladder disease or stone in conjunction with the
tetra-iodo-phenolthalein method.
6. Gastric disease and relation of barium-filled stomach
to other organs.
7. Movements of joints.
8. The intestine (barium-filled) producing a record of
the displacement effected by palpation in suspected
adhesions, tumours, etc.

meet all requirements.
