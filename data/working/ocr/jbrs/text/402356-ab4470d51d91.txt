THE UNION OF BURMA:
A Study of the First Years of Independence
by
Hugh Tinker (London: Oxford University Press, 1957.
388 pages, notes and index. K. 36.75).
Dr. Hugh Tinker has given a stimulating and thought provoking
account of Burma's progress since she attained the status of an
independent nation on January 4th 1948. The author has ably presented
a survey of the whole Burmese scene during the first eight or nine years.
after the attainment of Burma's independence.
Although the book might be regarded as a sequel to John L.
Christian's Modern Burma (1942) and Burma and the Japanese
Invader (1945) yet the study lacks the authoritativeness of the works
mentioned above. But this is due partly to the fact that the author
has not been able to examine exhaustively material, relative to
the period, written in Burmese ; and partly because it is not possible to
produce any authoritative review of the period since it is too recent
and cannot therefore be treated with any degree of objectivity by any
historian. Nonetheless as stated by Dr. Tinker himself, in the preface,
it seemed worth while to produce such a study in the absence of any
detailed account of post-war Burma.
A few observations might be made in regard to the sources and
material utilized by the author in presenting his study. While a good
number of official documents and publications have formed the basis of
most of the chapters, a number of paragraphs have been written largely
upon the information contained in the leading English newspapers of
Burma for the period concerned. Such reliance on press reports.
particularly those of the Nation, tends to make these paragraphs
biased from the point of view of a Burmese scholar. This is to be
noticed in the treatment which the author gave to certain aspects of the
subject of the civil war in the country. But this does not indicate any
shortcoming on the part of the author. It only reveals that fact that
there is an absence of reliable information on such matters.

The volume
On the other hand the book is the first of its kind to be written
in English on Burma in recent times. The study is a complete survey
of contemporary political, social, economic and religious issues and
ideologies presented in a frank and friendly manner.

On the whole the chapters, in particular those on politics and
government, throw a revealing light on fairly recent events which few
have had the opportunity to appreciate and study. The conclusions
which the author has drawn from the data at his disposal presents a
challenge to the people of Burma. Some of his ideas with regard to
the pattern of future development of the nation are both interesting
and stimulating. Besides a selective and indicative bibliography the
volume includes a glossary, an index and an annotated list of the
leading figures of Burma who played a role in the events narrated in
the book. It is eminently readable and the book should make its
appeal to a wide public both in Burma and in Britain. As a matter of
fact it may be stated without exaggeration that here is a book that
must be read and preserved as a source of reference on contemporary
Burma. It is a useful book for general reference, factually informative,
clearly arranged, well provided with footnotes that direct the reader to
the relevant authorities, and is an up-to-date account of Burma after
the attainment of her independence.
