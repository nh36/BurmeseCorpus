U_Tin Tun**
The copper deposits are situated about
5 miles due west of Monywa and cover
some 8 square miles, including the area of
Letpadaung Taung, Kyisindaung, Sabe-
daung, and Taung-Ngamauk.
The estimated ore reserves' of this
area is at least 100 to 150 million tons of
ore which is still more to be proved. This
ore reserve, if proved existing, should be
considered sufficient to extract it on a com-
mercial scale.

Test drill core samples supplied by
BGD were thoroughly mixed and three
-
quarters of the sample was crushed to
produce – 10 mesh material. Head sample
was prepared and its analysis revealed
1.44 pct Cu, 5.33 pet Fe, and 5.10 pct S.
Presence of precious metals was not detect-
ed by spectrographic analysis.

sampled and analyzed for Cu content.

Microscopic Counting
Microscopic counting of mineral
grains was made to find the economical
point of liberation of the copper minerals
from the pyrite and gangue. Visual exami-
nation of hand-picked samples indicated
the presence of chalcocite as the chief
copper mineral with minor amounts of
bornite, chalcopyrite, covellite appearing as
specks, pyrite and quartz as gangue.
The ground samples from the grinding
tests with 2000-gm charge were sampled
and concentrated by Micropanner. This
special device was used to separate heavy
mineral grains, both locked and free, from
the light gangue minerals.
Each panner
concentrate was dried and after careful
sampling was evenly and widely spread on
the glass pan of the Leitz Stereoscopic
Mineral Microscope. Free grains of pyrite,
free grains of chalcocite, and locked grains
of pyrite and chalcocite were counted. The
results are presented in Table III.
To show the variation in pct locked
grains with ball mill grinding time, a graph
was prepared and is shown as Figure II.
It is evident that longer grinding time than
24 minutes did not appreciably decrease
the pct locked grains in the ground pulp.
This curve in comparison with Fig. I
indicates a general trend in pct locked
grains in the ground pulp. The plus 200
mesh material in the ground pulp after
grinding for 24 minutes for 2000-gm charge
was about 20 pct with 18 pct locked grains
when ground for 72 minutes. This means
that although the decrease in percentage of
the plus 200 mesh material was significant,
the corresponding decrease in pct locked
grains was not sufficient considering the
required longer grinding time.

Differential Flotation Tests.
Many copper samples were readily
amenable to flotation using only lime and
sometimes cyanide for pyrite depression,
so that only a few confirmatory tests had
to be made to determine the reagent
consumption and flotation conditions.
For batch flotation tests, 500-gm of
-10 mesh ore was ground in Denver Lab
Ball Mill 12" x 5" for 7 minutes at 65
pct solids (80 pct passing 200 mesh) with
40 lbs of balls and 54 rpm equivalent
to 70 pct critical speed. The ground pulp
was transferred to the Denver Sub-A Lab
Flotation Machine, and diluted 33 pct
solids.
Effect of Lime.
The ground pulp was conditioned for
3 minutes with 0.5 lb per ton of sodium
silicate and required amount of lime to
make pH of 8, 9, 10, and 11 for each
test. It was then agitated with 0.1 lb
per ton of Aero Xanthate 350 for 3 minutes.
Flotation was made for 5 minutes after
adding 0.1 lb per ton of Aerofroth 65.
The concentrate was cleaned once. Flota-
tion products were filtered, dried, sampled
and analyzed for Cu. The results are
shown in Table IV.
The results indicate that there was
no appreciable change in concentrate grade
when the alkalinity of the pulp was in-
creased. The highest grade obtainable
with lime depressant was 10.59 pct Cu
at pH 11 with 87 pct recovery.

Mineragraphic Examinasion
Flotation tests so far conducted indica-
ted that the highest grade obtainable was
around 10 pct Cu. Hence there must be
a reason for getting such consistantly low
grade when every known reagent have been
tested. The answer must lie in the minerali-

zation of the ore itself—their combination,
the degree of locking, dissemination, oxida-
tion, tarnish and coating.
Selected drill core pieces were cut with
diamond disc cutting machine and mounted
in plastic. The mounted specimens were
ground and polished with emery paper,
corundum powder and polishing alumina
to obtain plane and scratch surface.
The polished specimens were studied
under the Leitz Metallux Microscope. The
following types of mineralization were
observed for the deposit.
(a) Principal copper mineral being chal-
cocite with fracture filling pyrite and
disseminated with pyrite and a few
grains of chalcopyrite were observed
in the chalcocite itself.
(b) Pyrite grains being completely coated
with chalcocite (rimming). The minute
coating varied from a fraction of a
micron to 15 microns thickness. (See
Plate No. 1 and Plate No. 2). Also
fracture filling chalcocite in pyrite
grains was also observed.

Cyanide Leaching and Flotation
Cynide solutions are good solvents for
many base metal minerals, particularly
most of the copper minerals. Leaver and
Woolf 2 who determined the solubilities of
copper minerals in 0.10 pet sodium cyanide
solution, showed that azurite, chalcocite,
malachite, cuprite, and bornite are readily
107
soluble in cyanide; least soluble are chal-
copyrite and chrysocolla. E.P. Cadwell³
had also described the problem of rimming
and its dissolution by cyanide.
Bulk Flotation
Since cyanide leaching would involve
only the sulfide minerals, it was therefore
most desirable to leach only the bulk
concentrate to reduce the cyanide con-
sumption as much as possible. The bulk
concentrate could be obtained by direct
sulfide flotation thus rejecting over 80 pct
of the gangue and allowing only a small
portion of the ore to be further processed.

Conclusions
1. Size assay distribution test indi-
cated no possibllity of concentration of
coarse size fractions and grinding to
minus 100 mesh must be made to achieve
effective separation.
2. Grinding tests and microscopic
counting of minerals grains in ground
product show the liberation size of the
ore to be a grind of 80 pct passing
200 mesh.
3. Usual differential of flotation of
the ore assaying 1.44 pct Cu using different
pyrite depressants in various amounts of
lime, sodium cyanide, and different types
of collectors, and frothers have failed to
increase the concentrate grade from about
10 pct Cu.
4. Mineragraphic examination indi-
cated rimming of pyrite grains with
U Tin Tun
chalcocite; such pyrite grains would float
in copper circuit as if it were a copper
mineral even when various types of pyrite
depressants were used.
5. Cyanide leaching of bulk con-
centrate to dissolve chalcocite coating
followed by selective flotation of copper
had resulted in over 23 pct Cu after
leaching for 2 hours at pH 11.8 and
33 pct solids containing 0.2 pct sodium
cyanide. Under similar conditions, leaching
time of 15 minutes gave 15 pct Cu. With a
leach pulp of 60 pct solids and with the
same cyanide concentration and leaching
time 1 hour, the concentrate assayed 17
pct Cu, with cyanide consumption of
0.24 per ton of ore.
6. Reagent requirements for bulk
flotation, cyanide leaching at 60 pct solids
for 1 hour followed by copper flotation were
2.6 lbs of lime, 0.28 lb of Aero Xanthate
350, 0.14 lb of pine oil, and 0.24 lb of
95 pct sodium cyanide per ton of ore
treated.
