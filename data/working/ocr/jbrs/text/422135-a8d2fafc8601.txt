HISTORY OF BURMA: A.D. 1400-1500.
by
Tin Hla Thaw
THE SOURCE MATERIALS of this paper are Burmese epigraphs of
14th to 16th centuries, "Mahakāssapa and His Tradition" by Dr Than
Tun, and "The Early Syām in Burma's History” by Professor Gordon
Luce. I must join with Dr Than Tun in thanking the staff of the Burma
Historical Commission for putting the estampages of those inscript-
ions at our disposal. My personal thanks go to U Po Hla, a bearer of the
Commission, for making my two hundred and fifty days of research as
pleasant as possible. The credit of this paper, if any, goes to Dr Yi Yi and
Dr Than Tun who deciphered the inscriptions together with me. Last but not
the least I must fulfill the most pleasant duty to thank my mentor, Dr Than
Tun. Very painstakingly he taught me how to decipher inscriptions, the
research technique, and the art of writing a documented paper. He helped not
only in deciphering but also in interpreting and indexing. He read the
manuscript thoroughly and made able criticisms, and converted the Burmese
dates into Christian dates. In short without Dr Than Tun's guidance and
encouragement I would not have aspired to write this paper. I must hasten to
add that I alone am responsible for whatever defect or defects there may
be. I wish to warn you that this paper is nothing more than an interim report.
Any able criticism will be most appreciated.

progress that Burma made in the 15th and 16th centuries". I propose to pick
up the thread from where he left. On the death of Tryāphyā IV, his brother
