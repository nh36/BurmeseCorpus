BURMESE PRINTED BOOKS BEFORE JUDSON
The centenary of the completion of Judson's Burmese Bible is an occa-
sion for the celebration of a very remarkable piece of work. His Bible re-
presents an achievement of vast concentration of effort and ability. It is
interesting, therefore, to consider how far Judson had the aid in his work of
the efforts of previous linguists and translators. From the various lives of
Judson which have been published, it is not possible to find much on this sub-
ject, but we know that there were already in existence printed works and
manuscript works on the Burmese language, and even some translations of
religious works into Burmese; and it is reasonable to conclude that Judson,
being a man of sense, would make use of these. Translations into Burmese
and work on the language had been commenced by the Italian priests nearly
a hundred years before Judson came to Burma. There is record of the
compilation by Father Sigismondo Calchi, who was in Burma from 1721 to
1728, of a Burmese-Italian dictionary. Father Giovanni Del Conte, 1743-
1745, produced a Catechismo Barmano, and cooperated with Father
Alessandro Mondelli, who was in Burma at the same time, in the production
of another Dizionario Barmano. Father Paolo Nerini, 1743-1756, produced
a Grammatica e Dizionario Barmano, a Catechismo, and a Libro di
Preghiere, all in Burmese. None of these works of the early Italian mis-
sionaries survives, however; probably the destruction of Syriam in 1756 ex-
plains their disappearance.
Subsequent Italians continued the work; Bishop Percoto, 1761-1776,
wrote a Dialogo sulla vera Religione tra un Talapuino ed un Cristiano; he
also translated into Burmese the books of Genesis and of Tobit, the Gospel
according to St. Matthew, and the Epistles of St. Paul; he wrote in addition
a Greater and a Lesser Catechism in Burmese, and compiled a Latin- Portu-
guese-Burmese dictionary with a Burmese grammar. Copies of all these
works were taken to Rome in 1773, but it is not clear whether or not they
were then published. The Dialogo was printed in Rangoon in 1837 and
is described as being then printed "per la prima volta" - for the first time.
Other works are definitely known to have been published in the later
eighteenth century. Father Melchiore Carpani, 1767-1773, wrote an
Alphabetum Barmanum, which was printed at Rome in 1776; and Bishop
Caetano Mantegazza, 1772-1794, also wrote an Alphabetum Barmanum, a
Catechismus pro Barmanis, and a translation of the same into Latin; these
were printed at Rome in 1785-6. Father Giuseppe D'Amato, 1783-1832,
wrote an Elegante ed eccellente spiegazione del Paternostro in Barmano;
and Father Ignazio de Britto, who was Burma-born and was a priest from
1787 to his death in 1827, wrote a Burmese grammer for Italians, an Italian-
Burmese-Pali dictionary (incomplete), a volume of sermons in Burmese,
and a Burmese pharmacology with Italian and Latin nomenclature.
these works were available to Judson when he was engaged in preparing his
dictionary and his Bible, and it is possible that Percoto's works were also
available. Two other works, whose authorship is not stated, had also been
published at Rome: Compendium doctrinae Christianae idiomate Barmano
(Rome 1776) and Preces Christianae Barmanorum Lingua (Rome 1785).

This latter may be a work of Mantegazza's, whose other works were printed
in Rome in the same year; and the Compendium may be by Carpani, whose
Alphabetum was printed in Rome in 1776 also.
It is nowhere stated that Judson made use of these works, but it is
known that he was on good terms with the Portuguese priests in Rangoon,
and he may also have had connections with the Italians. Father de Britto,
for example, was imprisoned with Judson during the war of 1824. We
know that Judson's predecessors in the Burma Baptist Mission, Chater and
Felix Carey, were helped by an Italian priest who gave them a book of ex-
tracts from the Scriptures in Burmese, which they found very useful in their
work; possibly this book of extracts was Bishop Percoto's volume of transla-
tions.

There had been one or two books on the Burmese language produced by
others than the Italians before Judson reached Burma. Dr. Buchanan's
Comparative Vocabulary of the Languages spoken in the Burma Emipre
had appeared as a paper in "Asiatic Researches" in 1798; and Towers's
"Alphabetical System of the Languages of Awa and Rachain "had appeared
in the same series in 1799. These were perhaps not likely to be of any great
assistance; but Leyden's “Comparative Vocabulary of the Barma, Malayu,
and Thai Languages", published by the English Baptists at Serampore in
1810, should have been useful. The English Baptists themselves had done
a certain amount of work, based on the endeavours of their members in
Burma, before Judson's arrival.
Chater and Felix Carey had translated the first three chapters of
Genesis, the XX Chapter of Exodus, the prophecy of Christ's birth in the
sign given to Ahaz, the history of His birth as given in Matthew and Luke,
and a summary of the doctrines referred to in these extracts; Felix Carey
also prepared a Burmese dictionary. These works were in hand in 1809.
In 1811 Carey was engaged in translating the Gospel according to St.
Mark; and in the following year when he was at Serampore he was printing
his translation of St. Matthew and a Burmese Grammar. A second edition
of the "Grammar of the Burman Language was published at Serampore
in 1814. Most of Felix's translations appear to have remained in manus-
cript, however, and to have been left in Judson's care when Felix abandoned
the Mission. Similarly the manuscript of his dictionary, which had been
completed before Judson arrived, seems to have been handed over to the
latter, and formed part of the foundation of the Judson Dictionary of 1826.
,,
It is thus apparent that, at the time when Judson came to Burma, there
was a fair amount of assistance available, in the way of both printed and
manuscript works, to aid him in learning the language and in undertaking
his translation of the Bible. A good deal of ground had already been
broken; but what had been done was, nevertheless, but a fragment of the
vast task the completion of which Judson undertook.
B. R. PEARN.
