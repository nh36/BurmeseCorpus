It will be seen that what I have been saying so far is
all about the verb and verb-formations. Indeed there is
not much to talk about without reference to the verb. Let
us sum up the activities of the verb in Burmese. It makes
the fullest use of suffixes to express not only the tenses but
also other distinctive meanings, four or five suffixes being
sometimes tacked on to the end. It makes compounds with
other verbs of identical or similar or different meanings. It
enlarges its meaning by means of auxiliary or helping --
verbs. It forms adjectives and adverbs. It forms verbal
nouns and noun-verbs and verbal noun-verbs. It forms
some of the most commonly used conjunctions. It may
dispense with the subject of the sentence. The result is that
the Burmese sentence is dominated by the verb and verb
-formations. And when we come across passages where
the subject is suppressed, we are in the midst of so much
