But they had also other influences, because "Windows to the West" had been
forcibly opened for them and for their country.
The "West Wind” that blew in asscertively at first gave them a chill. Soon they
became immuned; but they had never let out of sight and mind their national destiny -
the return once more to Free and Independent Burma without much delay.
With this end in view the early Thakins welcomed a particular breeze hailing from
Germany, because they found it most enervating. Also, because it hailed from a country
with whom they had common sympathies. Both had suffered national disaster and
dishonour at the hands of the same Imperial Power – Britain.
These revolutionary Thakins found their kindred spirit in Nietzsche, a German and
the philosopher of the doctrine of "the superman, the will to power, and the eternal
recurrence".
While these pioneer Thakins' political thinking was inspired by Nietzsche's ideas,
their political actions showed traces of the tactics of the Irish fighters for freedom of the
nineteen-tens.
Naturally, the Thakins were not "status seekers", but they were intensely and
vehemently against the “establishment" of the day - an alien corn amidst their golden
rice paddies.

But we know that ancient Greeks, whose legacy has enriched Europe and the world,

Yet, European civilization was able to develope beyond "psyche" and later produced
a culture based on science and technology.
To answer the question of how and why it is so, may I introduce two European
philosophers, one an exponent of idealist philosophy, and the other, materialist philosophy?
According to Taylor; this is due to Socrates, "who so far as can be seen, created
(out of psyche) the conception of the soul which has ever since dominated European
thinking. For more than two thousand years it has been the standing assumption of the
civilized European men that he has a soul, something which is the seat of his normal
waking intelligence and moral character, and that, since this soul is either identical with
himself or at any rate the most important thing about him, his supreme business in life is
to make the most of it and do the best for it."2
And the soul in Socratic terms is defined as “that within us in virtue of which we
are pronounced wise or foolish, good or bad.”
But in Engel's words:
"The philosophy of antiquity was primitive, natural materialism. As such, it was
incapable of clearing up the relation between thought and matter. But the need to get
clarity on this question led to the doctrine of a soul separable from the body, then to the
assertion of the immortality of this soul, and finally to mono-theism. The old materialism
was therefore negated by idealism.
“But in the course of the further development of philosophy, idealism too became
untenable and was negated by modern materialism. This modern materialism, the
negation of negation, is not the mere re-establishment of the old, but adds to the perma-
nent foundations of this old materialism, the whole thought content of two thousand years
of development of philosophy and natural science as well as of the historical development
of these two thousand years”.3

Mr. Chairman, I have at last come to the few and final “Stanzas” of the Prologue.
There are in our country three ways of thought coexisting together - our folk belief,
the Buddhist Faith, and Science and Modernism.
It is well-known how King Anawrata (1044-1077) tried to diffuse new outlook and
new values into our society of his times by introducing Buddhism to the Kingdom of Pagan.
This freshly acquired faith of the Four Noble Truths and the Eight-fold Path,
superimposed on our folk culture, turned "Pagan into a heavenly city", and left us with a
rich heritage, that has kept us well, as a people, and a civilization, to this very day, in
spite of many ups and downs of our history like Beethoven's Eroica Symphony, which
takes us in a movement of granduer to heroic heights, and then suddenly drops to a
mournful funeral march. And we know what happened in 1885.
In the nineteen-thirties, the Thakins in their own way tried to encourage new
national and political attitudes. And these have brought us to where we are today. And
where do we go from here?
There exists no doubt a large gap between our folk belief in "face", and "butterfly”,
and Einstein's Nuclear Age formula, E = MC². To bridge this is our task, and it is not
simple.
But we know where we stand, and I think that in itself will give us aid in our efforts
to get where we wish to go.
