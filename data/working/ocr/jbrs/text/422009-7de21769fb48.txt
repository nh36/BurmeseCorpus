INTRODUCTION
Burma produces annually an average of 490,500 tons of rice braħ
which contains more than 50,000 tons of edible oil. At the same time our
country is importing annually five crores worth of edible oils. We would
be able to stop this drain on our foreign exchange by extracting the edible
oil from rice bran and using it for cooking, frying and as salad oil. Why
has this not been done so far?
The main reasons are that few people know that there is such a thing
as rice bran oil and secondly that it can now be extracted profitably in
Burma. We cannot blame the people for not knowing about this latter
fact because we ourselves found it out only last year. This paper will
describe a new extraction unit which has been invented by the Union of
Burma Applied Research Institute for which patent applications have
been filed in U. S. A., U. K. and Japan. Previously it was believed that
rice bran oil could be extracted profitably only with large factories having
input capacities of at least 50 tons rice bran per day. These factories
involve large capital expenditures and what is more
function properly in Burma because the rice bran goes rancid before the
factory can process it and so most of the oil obtained can be used only
for industrial purposes.

RICE BRAN OIL EXTRACTION
Three main qualities of Rice Bran are normally obtained from the rice
mills in Burma.
(1) First Polishing Rice Bran contains 12 to 16 percent by weight
of oil. It has less fines and starch content and so is most
suitable for rice bran oil extraction.
(2) Second Polishing Rice Bran- contains less oil, 7 to 11 percent and
more starch which is objectionable for rice bran oil extraction.
(3)
Parboiled Rice Bran- has a high oil content of 20 to 25 percent
but the parboiled rice bran output is considerably lower than the
First Polishing Rice Bran.
The high pressure squeezing method of edible oil extraction used for
groundnut and sessamum is not so successful for rice bran because even
with high pressures, only a portion of the rice bran oil can be squeezed
out of the rice bran. So a solvent-extraction method has to be employed.
The UBARI Rice Bran Oil Extracting Unit is unique in the world because
it can operate profitably with a small input capacity of only 4 tons per day
which is very suited to Burmese Rice Mills whose average output is about
4 tons of Rice Bran per day. The initiative for this development is due
to Mr. Charles Schuh, who was with the Union of Burma Applied
Research Institute as Armour Research Foundation Specialist in unit
operations.
The basic principle is to percolate a solvent through the rice bran.
This solvent extracts the oil from the bran and the oilheavy solvent is eva-
porated, leaving the oil to be filtered. The bran is heated to drive off all
the remaining solvent and can be sold as a protein rich cattle feed. This
bran is better for cattle feeding because it no longer will get rancid.

THE PROBLEM OF RANCIDITY
The fresh rice bran as it comes out from the rice mill is a wholesome
powder, with a pleasant nutlike odour and possessing highly nutritive
properties. But within a few hours it starts to get rancid due to the for-
mation of free fatty acid which develops at the rate of 1 percent per hour.
Some regard the rancidity as the action of enzymes, catalysed by moisture.
A satisfactory explanation is so far not known. However, rice bran oil
has been demonstrated to oxidise rapidly in the bran, once the bran has
been polished from the grain. One of the principal causes of deterioration
of bran during storage is the hydrolysis of glycerides of the oil in bran.
The problem of rancidity is a serious one and we have solved it by
the unique design of the extraction unit which enables every Burmese rice
mill to have its own rice bran oil extracting plant attached to it. In this
way, the rice bran can be processed immediately it is polished off the
rice grains in the rice mill. So there is no time for free fatty acid formation
and the oil coming out is free from fatty acids. Also a much higher
yield is obtained as there are no losses of oil on processing.
EXPERIMENTAL WORK
Laboratory experiments were conducted to test the production of oil
and a number of tests were run with 30 pound batches. A detailed study
on the degree of vacuum, rate of percolation, height of bed, solvent-bran
ratios and estimation of retained solvent in the bran bed after extraction. •
was made.
On the basis of data collected from semipilot plant operation
in the laboratory a pilot plant was designed to conduct process research
on the large scale to confirm laboratory result and later to set up a
commercial plant of 4 tons per day capacity.
As a result of problems which were met in the pilot plant, the idea
was developed of introducing fresh solvent at intervals. Many variations
were tried. Finally a satisfactory result was achieved for commercial
application. The use of vacuum technique is justified economically because
it helps to keep the operating time to a minimum. This factor lowered
the capital outlay and cuts operating cost to an appreciable extent. The
minimum solvent to bran ratio, reduction of solvent loss and the suitable
boiling range of the solvent contribute towards the economics of the
plant.
In addition, the use of low cost, locally fabricated unit decreases the
capital cost and also the amount of foreign exchange investment.
