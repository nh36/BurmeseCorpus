ODYSSEY OF THE FIRST BURMESE TYPES
by
VIVIAN BA*
The first Burmese types ever to be cast were produced by the Society of the Propa-
gation of the Faith in Rome. Bishop Percoto, the Barnabite missionary who translated
several Buddhist manuscripts into Latin and Italian and who wrote and spoke Pali and
Burmese like a learned Buddhist monk, was the person who asked the Vatican to accom.
plish the task. Percoto was also a lexicographer and his Latin-Portuguese-Burmese
Dictionary was his life's work and he compiled the last pages even when his eyesight and
health were failing. (*)
•
Bishop Percoto had many requests to make for the Burma Mission in Rome. He
wanted a printing press and sent the Burmese alphabet neatly written to be cast in Rome.
He wanted the necessary matrices and punches to be sent to Burma so that his numerous
translations and original works in Burmese could be printed on the spot. His emissary to
Rome for this important work was Father Carpani. This missionary left for Rome in the
first days of 1774.

The casting of the First Burmese Types.
Vivian Ba
Father Carpani* arrived in Rome in December 1776 and presented a petition to
the Propaganda Fide for all the needs of the Ava Mission and for the necessary printing
materials. He also presented to them all the manuscripts of Bishop Percoto. The
Cardinals "received with the utmost satisfaction all the manuscripts of Percoto written in
Pali and Burmese, which Carpani according to his instructions from the author himself,
had offered to the Cardinals of the Sacred Congregation, which they ordered to be kept in
the Library of the Propagation of the Faith in Rome. And since Carpani himself had
taken with him the entire Burmese alphabet very neatly written, the Sacred Congregation
thus decreed that on the model of this alphabet should the first types in Burmese be cast
for future printing. This was done very soon in the year 1776 and the first book in
Burmese was printed: ALPHABETUM BARMANUM SEU BOMANUM REGNI AVAE
FINITIMARUMQUE REGIONŲM. Typis Sacrae Congreg. de Propaganda Fide.
Praesidium adprobatione."(2)
The Alphabetum Barmanum
The first printed book in the Burmese language numbered 115 pages. Pages
I-XLIV made up the prologue and preface, where the history, geography and customs of
Burma were given in outline with a description of the races and peoples of Burma, religion
and the monastic life. The history of the Burmese alphabet is also outlined, with
comments in Latin followed by printed samples of Catholic prayers in the first Burmese
types. The learned prologue was from the pen of Father Gian Cristoforo Amaduzzi,
Director of the Propagation Press and Mgr. Borgia, Secretary of the Propagation Press,
who dedicated the first printed book in our language to Pope Pius VI.
Carpani's Burmese Alphabet and outline Grammar in spite of a few errors was a
great pioneering work in the history both of Burmese printing and Burmese Grammar
compilation. It was the first attempt at casting Burmese types, and the type casters of the
Propaganda Fide in Rome had once again proved their worth. The reproduction of the
Preface to the Kammua, the Burmese and Pali alphabets and the coins or seals of the
Kingdoms of Ava and Pegu was a success in detail and clarity.

In the dedication (page a3) we read that the book which was printed in the Press of
the Propagation of the Faith for the first time with types cast in Rome was dedicated to
Pope Pius VI, by Stephanus Borgia, Secretary to the Propaganda Fide.
Types destined for Burma.
Besides this first printed book in Burmese containing interesting prologues and
prefaces in Latin and the alphabets and outline Grammar in Burmese letters, the first
Burmese Catechism was also printed in Rome. The Propaganda Fide also put aside
seventy thousand newly cut Burmese types and forty thousand Latin types with two printing
matrices to be given to the new missionaries destined for Burma, so that they might take
the types and presses to Bishop Percoto for his future publications in Burmese, like the
voluminous dictionaries he had prepared and other spiritual works (i copiosi dizionari,
non meno che tutte le altre sue opre di sacro argomento). (3)
Emissaries of the first Burmese Types.
The missionaries to whom these first Burmese types were entrusted for transporta-
tion to Burma were Fathers Luigi Grondona, John Mary Mazzuchelli and Pascal Fantasia.
"Well fitted out with everything for the long voyage, the baggage packed and placed
in seven cases, the Burmese and Latin types, the matrices, the presents for the King and
other things destined for India, Father Mazzuchelli and Father Fantasia began setting out
in the autumn of 1776." They reached Porto Oriente together and waited there for Father
Grondona. This time they wanted to go to Burma all together on the same boat, but
Providence had ordained otherwise. Each one left on a different ship. Fathers Mazzu-
chelli and Fantasia arrived at Pegu in September of that year, but Father Grondona was
wrecked and after many mishaps reached Rangoon only two years later on the 30th
August, 1778.
Father Mantegazza leaves for Rome.
After Percoto's death, Father Mantegazza who had been elected Coadjutor Bishop
and acting Provicar Apostolic for some years now had the chance to go to Rome and
receive the Episcopal consecration, with the arrival of reinforcements in 1783.
A certain learned Burman called ‘Giammaria Zau' was also worrying Mantegazza to
go to Rome and the Bishop elect thought that it would be a good chance to make a new
edition of the Alphabetum Barmanum.
Accompanied by the pious Burman aged sixty years and a young Burmese valet the
missionary left for Rome in January 1784. At Port Louis in Mauritius they were given
hospitality by the Fathers of St. Vincent de Paul, and since the travellers were heading for
Rome they were entrusted with three young students from Madagascar. They were wrecked

The second edition of the Alphabetum Barmanum.
- On the 10th September, they went to call on the Cardinal prefect of the Propaganda,
His Eminence Cardinal Antonelli, and then went to see the Secretary of the Society, Mgr.
Borgia, and since they went on foot the whole of Rome seemed to be following them and

With the help of the learned ex-phongyi U Zaw, the new Bishop supervised the
casting of the new Burmese Types. These were engraved from the models of U Zaw.
Father Amaduzzi who had written a preface to the first edition ten years ago wrote another
one for the second edition and the new enlarged Catechism was printed in Burmese besides
the Alphabet and Thinbongyi (a separate wall chart with the whole grammar laid out at a
glance).
Bishop Bigandet in his history of the Catholic Church in Burma tells us that
'U Tsau' as he calls him left an interesting diary of his stay in Italy.
The First Burmese Types and Napoleon I.
When Napoleon took over Rome, he ordered the Propaganda printing press with
all equipment, particularly "with the foreign (exotique) types to be transported to Paris for
"L'Academie de France". There is a copy of Napoleon's decree in the archives of the
Propaganda Fide to this effect, dated Rome, 7 floreal, an 6, and signed by Commissar
'Monge'. (5) Among all these 'exotique' types, there were the Burmese types also and
they are still in Paris today, in the Oriental Section of the L'Imprimerie Nationale.
Paris the home of these first Burmese Types.

centuries of official printing endeavour in France, dedicates several paragraphs to oriental
types and printing and gives the lists of oriental books printed by it among which some
Burmese books are also included. (6)

Odyssey of the First Burmese Types

"The National Press is rightly proud of its scientific vocation. We have already
described all its efforts for oriental studies, even though it was not officially founded for
that. In the XIX century with Silvestre de Sacy, Remusat, Burnouf and Renan, and in
the XXth century with eminent philologists and linguists, it would maintain its tradition
and enrich its collection with the rarest characters. Its Cabinet of Oriental punches, so
often praised, comprises to-day three hundred and fifty thousand, reproducing the writings
both ancient and modern of all nations". (7)
The present Inspector of the Oriental Section of the Imprimerie Nationale in a
letter dated 16th October 1962 to me says:" I have the honour to confirm the taking in
1798 of the exotic characters from the Press of the Propaganda Fide in Rome, though
it is not possible to give a detailed list of the characters."
"However there is no doubt that the first Burmese characters used in the National
Press came from this source; take for example the characters of the corps 18. The exact
date of its creation is unknown; we may however date it as 1787, the date when the
Propaganda Fade of Rome published the "L'Alphabetum Barmanorum seu regni Avensis".*
"These types were retouched in 1828, by Delafond, engraver at the Royal Press,
under the direction of Eugene Burnouf, one of the greatest orientalists of this epoch."
“Our collection of Burmese characters was then completed in 1833, by the same
persons, and corps 12 was produced, whose use was more practical than the big types of
corps 18".(8)
After the abdication of Napoleon I, by a decree of Louis XVIII, on the 15th
November 1815, most of the matrices and punches were returned to the Holy See
“a amaible”. But the doubles and the copies were still kept in Paris.(9)
The Burmese Grammar by Vossion was printed by the Imprimerie Nationale in
Paris and also the samples of all the Oriental types in the Press were embodied in an
interesting compilation by Fossey. Mon and Burmese types figure in this collection.(10)
