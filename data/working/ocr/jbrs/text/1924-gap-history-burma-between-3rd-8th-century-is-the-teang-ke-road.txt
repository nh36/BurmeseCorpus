Conclusions.
95
For the present, Pelliot obviously inclines to the view that Chin-lin refers to
the region of Thaton-Martaban. There are objections, of
course. The variants Chin-lin (*kiam-lien) and Chin-ch'ên.
*kiem-d'ien), both perhaps of equal antiquity, are sufficiently close in sound (cf.
Chu-li and Chü-chih) to suggest that the name may be a transcription, and not a
translation, of the native name; and this would explain the variant writings of lin,
one of which-Chin-lin-has no semantic value. The latter objection
can be explained away as due to a mistake in writing, but it is not so easy to
dismiss the first. If the I wu chih is the Nan chou i wu chih of Wan Chên, the
identification of Chin-ch'ên with Chin-lin is as old as the 3rd century; the name
Chin-ch'ên is also said to occur in the Wai kuo ch'uan, which may be the actual
