Probably the earliest reference to the compilation of the first
Burmese-English Dictionary, which was printed in India in 1826, occurs
in a publication of the Baptist Missionary Society of Serampore, entitled
Memoir of the Translations of the Sacred Scriptures Addressed to the
Society in 1813. On page 308 of this publication, it is stated that a
Grammar of the Burmese language (undoubtedly Felix Carey's A
Grammar of the Burman Language, published at Serampore in 1814)
"is now in the press, and Brother Felix Carey is busily employed in
preparing a Dictionary. His acquaintance with the language enables
him to converse in any circle; and he is now sent for to Ava on a
medical account by the Prince the Heir Apparent.
34
In the Periodical Accounts of the Baptist Mission, Vol. VI, Carey
is reported to have stated, when anticipating leaving Burma for a while
in 1815, that upon arrival in Bengal he would set two presses working
and have founts of type cut to print the Scriptures and the Dictionary.
His hope of setting up a printing press in Burma had failed because the
press and types sent by the Serampore Bretheren had been lost during
transit to the Burmese capital. It might be assumed that when Carey
finally left Burma in 1817 and settled to work with the Baptist Mission
in Serampore he had taken his dictionary manuscript with him, because
he died there in 1822 and when the first Burmese-English Dictionary
was printed at the Baptist Mission Press in 1826, the preface contained
an acknowledgement to the contribution of "the late Mr. F. Carey".
The title-page of this dictionary reads: "A Dictionary of the Burman
Language with explanations in English. Compiled from the manuscripts
of A. Judson, D. D. and of other missionaries in Burmah. Profits
devoted to the support of the Burman Mission. Calcutta: Printed at
the Baptist Mission Press, Circular Road; and sold by Messrs.
W. Thacker and Co., St. Andrew's Library, Calcutta; and by the
American Missionaries in Burmah. 1826."

In the previous year, 1825, the Rev. G. H. Hough's An English and
Burman Vocabulary had been printed at Serampore. This publication
contained an interesting preface, in which the author anticipated
the publication of a Burmese-English Dictionary by the American Baptist
Mission in Burma, he said: "A Burman and English Dictionary, both
copious and correct, will hereafter, it is expected, be a desideratum.
The American Mission, having been established several years in Burmah,
is provided with adequate mears, in regard to materials and printing in
that country, for producing it. One of the Missionaries, the Rev.
Mr. Judson, now in Ava, who has made the Burman language his
study more than ten years, has had the opportunity of collecting
materials for such a work, and has already arranged the greater propor-
tion of them according to the most approved orthography and etymo-
logy of the language. Having had access to elementary books, to many
of the religious and historical works of the country, and obtained the
assistance of several Burmans of learning and ability, it may be expec-
ted that when he shall be in a situation to complete and publish the
work, it will be a valuable addition to the many useful philological
publications already existing in the east. It is to these materials that
the compiler of this volume is chiefly indebted for his selections.
""
Perhaps the preface to this dictionary, which stresses the nced for a
Burmese-English dictionary, induced the Rev. J. Wade to make use of
the manuscript left by Felix Carey and to use the material compiled by
Dr. Judson, which had apparently been sent from Rangoon to Calcutta
on the outbreak of the Anglo-Burmese War when the printing press
and types belonging to the American Baptist Mission had been trans-
ferred from Burma. For it was the Rev. J. Wade who was responsible
for seeing through the press the first Burmese-English Dictionary
printed by the Calcutta Baptist Mission, and a large part of the preface
which he contributed, is an apologia for using Judson's manuscript.
The preface, signed "J. Wade, Calcutta, Dec. 15, 1825", contains the
following interesting passages:-
"The principal materials of the following work was originally
furnished in MS. by the Rev. Dr. Judson, and designed for the use of
the American Missionaries in Burmah.
"The late war, however, having brought a number of gentlemen.

The solicitude of Dr. Judson's colleagues "for attaining the end
for which he employed so much time and labour" led them to set about
publishing, whilst he was imprisoned, the material he had assembled
for dictionary; such solicitude might have called forth gratitude from
Judson upon his release, but such, apparently, was not the case. How
soon Judson became aware of the existence of a dictionary bearing his
name is not evident. The one reference, which has so far been traced,
that he made to it was in 1843, when he dismissed this four-hundred
page dictionary as of no consequence. Apparently, Judson considered
this publication something best forgotten, and so successful was he in
this attitude towards it that, with the passage of time, the dictionary
became entirely ignored and neglected and its linguistic significance was
not recognised until it was located in 1956.
*
The chief factor which led to this 1826 publication being over-
looked was, undoubtedly, the preface written by Judson himself in
1849 for the generally termed 'first' Burmese-English Dictionary, which
was published in 1852, after his death. In the preface, dated 1849,
Judson wrote: "It is exceedingly difficult to settle the orthography of
a language which has never been printed, and in which there are no
grammars nor dictionaries." Also, Judson's son, Edward Judson, in
the biography of his father, entitled: Adoniram Judson, the Great
Missionary of Burma, ignored the 1826 dictionary and said that his
father did not live to complete the Burman Dictionary "... the Burmese
and English part was left in an unfinished state. In articles published
in The Aryan Path, Vol. XXVIII, No. 4, Bombay, April 1957 and in
Burma, Vol. VII, No. 3, Director of Information, Rangoon, April 1957,
the writer has propounded theories why silence was maintained about
the 1826 Dictionary. It is appropriate to quote here the only known
reference Judson made to it, which was brought to the writer's
notice by Dr. Frank N. Trager of New York University, after the two
above-mentioned articles had been written. This reference occurs on
page 192 of Volume II of A Memoir of the Life and Labors of

dated from Maulmain, July 13, 1843, Judson wrote: "In May last

there were several missionaries in this place, I considered that I could
not do better than to comply."
Whatever caused the reticence about the 1826 Dictionary, whether
it was based on political variance of opinion over Burmese affairs.
between the American Baptist Mission and the Bengal Government,
which may have sponsored the publication: or whether the lack of
demand for the dictionary led to financial loss; or whether Judson was
annoyed and embarrassed to find his name and work associated in the
preface with Felix Carey's name and contribution, because Carey was
not highly regarded in missionary circles having accepted service at the
Burmese court, the real cause of the reticence will probably never be
known. But it is interesting to take into account Judson's personal
reaction to this dictionary, for it is evident from his statement that he
had resolved and re-resolved never to touch the work that this was a
deep-seated grievance. Further reading into Wayland's Memoir of the
Life of Judson reveals that in his early days in Burma Judson had
devoted much of his time to compiling the dictionary, which in his
later years caused him such antipathy. In Volume 1, page 181, in a
letter from Rangoon, dated August 5th, 1816, appears the following
statement: "... a grammar is finished to facilitate the studies of others,
and a dictionary of the language is in a very forward state. Writing
again from Rangoon on August 26, 1817, Volume I, page 190, Judson
said: "I am at present wholly absorbed in the dictionary. I hope to
have it finished by the time that Brother Rice arrives." On page 213
of the same volume, in an extract from Judson's Journal addressed to
the Corresponding Secretary, under the entry for April 1819, he had
written: "My close application to the Burman dictionary during the
year 1817, and my subsequent loss of nearly a year in the unsuccessful
attempt to visit Chittagong, have occasioned a long interruption of my
journal.

It has not been possible to ascertain the number of copies of the
dictionary printed at Serampore in 1826 and as, according to the
preface, it was probably published especially to meet the need of British
troops in Burma, (although, in passing, it may be noted that an English-
Burmese Dictionary would have been more appropriate for this
purpose), the majority of copies, it may be assumed, would not have
been very carefully preserved. Consequently, the dictionary is an item
of considerable rarity, although copies are to be found in old established
oriental libraries.
6
Two copies of the 1826 Dictionary, catalogued under A. Judson'
were recorded in A Catalogue of the Library of the Hon. East India
Company, London, 1845. In Volume III of the Fifth edition of
Manuel du Libraire et de l'amateur de Livres, by Jacques-Charles
Brunet, Paris, 1862, the dictionary is listed under 'A. Judson' and it is
stated that at public auctions a copy had been sold for 37-francs and
another copy for 45-francs.
In the Second edition of Trubner's Catalogue of Dictionaries and
Grammars of the Principal Languages and Dialects of the World, London,
1882, the dictionary is catalogued under A. Judson' and listed as
Scarce', but only priced at 15-shillings, whereas Charles Lane's
Dictionary, English and Burmese, Revised by the Prince of Mekhara,
1841, was listed at double the price-one and a half guineas. The First
edition of Trubner's Catalogue, published in 1872, did not record
this scarce dictionary.
