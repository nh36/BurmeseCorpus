Among the parabeiks which some years ago were transferred
from the Secretariat to the Rangoon University, is a document,
listed among the records of Tharrawaddy Min's reign, which is a
full translation of the Treaty of Balta Liman. This Treaty was
concluded on the 16th August 1838 between the British Government
and the Ottoman Porte; it was a commercial treaty "by which
Great Britain was given the 'most favoured nation' treatment in all
commercial matters, and permission to trade throughout the Ottoman
dominions. By this treaty all commercial monopolies were declared
abolished in the Turkish dominions" (Cambridge History of British
Foreign Policy, Vol. II, p. 170, note 2). The inclusion of this
document among the official records of the Burmese Government is
explained by the circumstance that King Tharrawaddy had on his
accession in 1837 taken the view that his predecessor's treaties were
not binding on him, and had expressly declared that he was not

By the time that any copy of the Treaty of Balta Liman could have
reached Burma say early in 1839, Burney's successor, Colonel Benson,
was on the point of leaving the capital since he could find no
common ground between himself and the Burmese Government.
The existence of this document, however, suggests that King
Tharrawaddy was anxious to be informed of the commercial rela-
tions between the British Government and other Governments, and
that if the situation had reached breaking-point, he might have been
prepared to negotiate another treaty to replace those which he was
not willing to recognise. Apart from that, the placing on record
of this Turko-British treaty indicates that the Burmese Government
was keeping in close touch with events in the outside world.
