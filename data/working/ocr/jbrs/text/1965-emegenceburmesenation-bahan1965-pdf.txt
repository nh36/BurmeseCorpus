1. Myths and legends regarding Burmese
origins
The search for the origins of any
people proves, more often than not, to
be a pursuit of the wind. This is pe-
culiarly true of the Burmese origins. For
information regarding the remotest ances-
tors of the Burmese, we are thrown back
on an infinity of myths and legends.
Notoriously, myths and legends have a
tendency to proliferate and take on the
varied hues of rainbow dreams as they
pass from mouth to mouth. When trans-
mitted from generation to generation they
are subconsciously accepted as gospel
without being consciously justified. This
way lies disaster. We shall soon find
ourselves on treacherous ground if not
in a quagmire of error. Myths and
legends may make engaging reading. But
they cannot be accepted as facts of his-

the Burmese belong to the South Mon-
golian race who migrated from the densely
populated and dessicated highlands of
Tibet and North West China into the
sparsely peopled and fertile plains of
Burma. 3 The valleys of the three
rivers the Mekong, the Salween and the
Irrawaddy - lay open for their southward
movement.

tradition that harks back to India as the
original home of the ancestors of the
Burmese people. This tradition is expressed
succintly in the popular saying: The begin-

Centuries before the Pyus founded
Sriksetra in the south of Burma, Indian
immigrants crossed the Bay of Bengal and
established settlements at the coast towns.13
Some of the immigrants hailed from Con-
jeeveram (Kanchipura) and some from
Telingana on the east coast of India. Both
towns were then strongholds of southern
Buddhism. The immigrants came mainly
for purposes of trade. Doubtless some
were fired with propagandist ardours.
Inevitably, trade and commerce brought in
their train civilisation and culture. 14
Dr. Ba Han
Be that as it may, the immigrants
brought with them their sacred books on
which their language, their religion and
their social institutions were grounded.
They were not only the spearpoint of Indian
penetration but the radiating centres of a
new intellectual and cultural force.
From the evidence we have considered
we connot escape the conclusion that the
Pyus (P'iao) who lived in the northern
border regions of Burma in about the third
century trekked downwards and founded
two or three centuries later a state at Srik-
setra under the dominating influence of the
Hindus who had settled there before them.
The enriching impact of Indian civilization
on the Pyus taught them the art of carving
on stone and writing a script similar to the
Kadamba and Pallava scripts of the time.
Above all it converted them to the Hinayana
Buddhist faith which set before them an
ethical ideal by which they could measure
themselves and towards which they could
direct their activities.

and the Return of the Pyus to the North

The cause may be either Mon pressure

Chronicle mentions Mranma for Thet.27

5. The flight of the Ch'iangs the remote
ancestors of the Burmese into Burma
It has been already pointed out that
according to informed opinion the remote
ancestors of the Burmese people came from
the highlands of Tibet and north-west
China. The earliest Chinese records refer
to a people called by the Chinese Ch'iang.
They lived to the west of the Chinese.
Owing to the relentless marauding raids of
