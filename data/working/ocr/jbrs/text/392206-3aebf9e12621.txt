An attempt is being made to devise a suitable set of weights for
the wholesale price index of agricultural products. The problem there.
is to get weights representative of the volume of transactions in each
group of agricultural products; output and import figures have been'
too unstable in post-war years to serve as such a basis.
The weight assigned to fish and fish products is 28% of the weight
assigned to food items, and 17.3% of the total weight of the Consumers'
Price Index. In the seasonal variation of the Consumers' Price Index,
the non-food items are relatively stable; therefore most of the variation
is que to the food items, of which fish and fish products form a
considerable part. It was argued in my paper that the movement in
fish prices was an important element in explaining the movement of the
index; it was not claimed that it was the only influence. It is not
surprising therefore that there are some differences between the two
series.
While I have not been able to find a satisfactory explanation of the
dip in foreign trade values and in bank clearings in April, I do not find
Dr. Tun Wai's explanation very convincing. The low levels of these
series around October are most likely related to the slack season before
the harvests. But the dip in April occurs during a period of active
business both internally and in foreign transactions. It has been
seriously suggested to me that the explanation may lie in such factors
as the difficulty of working in April, the hottest month of the year;
and some difficulty of navigation in the rivers at that time.
The remark about the part played by domestic trade in railway
activity is only a conjecture; it is not based only on the nature of the
seasonal fluctuations in railway traffic, but also on studies of the
composition of goods carried by the railway.
Regarding Dr. Tun Wai's "three factors" explaining the seasonal
decline in Notes and Coins towards the end of the calendar year, I
would like to hazard the view that all three factors are themselves
related to the flow of money in the form of notes and coins into and
from the rural sector, based in turn on the agricultural seasonality.
