A Palæolith From Yenangyaung
The small hand axe illustrated herewith (Fig. 1) was picked up recently
on the Yenangyaung Golf Course by Mr E. S. Pinfold. It is made of felsite,
now patinated various shades of deep cream and brown, and is about three
inches long.
The implement belongs to the Chelleo-Acheulean series of cultures, but
the particular stage to which it should be assigned is somewhat difficult to
determine. It is a much later type than the early Chellean hand axe which I
described in this Journal a few years ago (J.B.R.S., XXII, 19), but although
the whole of its surface is flaked the roughness of the flaking and the
relative thickness of the implement weigh against its being classed as
Acheulean. These features, however, may be due more to the poor quality
of the material employed than to primitive workmanship. There are signs
that its maker intended to carry out finer flaking around the edges, but was
deterred from doing so by the irregular grain of the rock. Further, the
edges possess a sigmoidal twist characteristic of the later stages of Acheulean
culture. On the whole I am inclined to think the balance of evidence is in
favour of an Acheulean date for the implement, rather than late Chellean.
From the archæological point of view this discovery is of importance in
that it partly fills the great gap which existed in Burma between the early
Chellean artefact mentioned above and the late Palæolithic or Mesolithic
flake industry (J.B.R.S., XXV, 6). It also promises well for a much more
complete sequence of early cultures being revealed in Burma once adequate
search can be made.
The geological aspect of the find is also of considerable importance.
The Yenangyaung Golf Course, on which the implement was picked up, is
laid out on an extensive remnant of the Konbyinmyint Terrace of the
Irrawaddy (Fig. 2). The discovery of an Acheulean hand axe on this terrace
supports the belief, previously based on the Themathauk Chaung specimen
alone, that the Konbyinmyint Terrace had been cut and the gravels deposited
on it in pre-Palæolithic time.
T. O. MORRIS
