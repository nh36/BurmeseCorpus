Maung Ba Han, M.A., Ph.D., D.Litt.,
Professor Emeritus of Law, Rangoon University.
Part I (A & B), Hanthawaddy Press, Rangoon, 1951.
Dr. Ba Han is to be congratulated on his Dictoinary.
Though Professor Emeritus of Law, he is well-equipped
to write an English-Burmese Dictionary. He graduated
with English and was for many years Government
Translator. He has been several times external examiner
in Burmese at the University. He has had experience in
dictionary work, having helped to revise U Tun Nyein's
English-Burmese Dictionary. And he is chairman of an
Advisory Board to supervise the dictionary work of U
Wun, who has just brought out the first instalment of his
University Burmese Dictionary.
We have no hesitation in saying that the author has
achieved his "first and formost" object. accuracy.

We must mention another important feature.
Dictionary is not a mere book of Burmese equivalents
for English words and phrases. It abounds in niceties
of Burmese expression and other information, which
should be welcome especially to foreigners learning the
Burmese language. Thus, examples are given to show
how break is rendered by ကျိုးသည်၊ ကွဲသည်၊ ပြတ်သည်၊ ပေါက်
and so on according to the nature of the things that
break. And under the article A a whole column is
devoted to a scholarly note to show that though we say
ဘုရားတဆူ we say ဘုန်းကြီးတပါး၊ လူတယောက်၊ ခွေးတကောင်။ and so
on, changing the numerative according to the nature
of the noun used. The fact that the author writes this
note in English and not in Burmese, as a Burman wri-
ting an English-Burmese Dictionary is expected to do
further shows-as do such entries as "Been, v. Past
Participle of Be" "Began, v. Preterite of Begin, v." - that
the purpose of the Dictionary, though it is not explicitly
said so, is not only to help Burman students to learn
English through the mother tongue, but also to assist
foreigners to acquire Burmese through the English.
