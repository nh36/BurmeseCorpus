Thiripyanchi U MYA
OBITUARY
U MYA, retired Superintendent of
the Archaeological Survey of Burma and
Honorary Member of the Burma Research
Society died on 22nd January 1973 at a
ripe old age of 94. He was physically
no imposing personality but his modest
manners, his perseverence, his powers of
concentration and his incredibly remarka-
ble memory impressed those around him.
In fact he was an example of a quiet,
simple man attaining to the heights of a
learned and erudite scholar by dint of
hard work and devotion to duty.
Born on 13th March 1879, U Mya
was one of the first batch of students to
have graduated from the Insein Technical
School. He worked as a teache in Minbu
Government High School for two years
and then as an instructor in his old
Technical School for four years before he
joined the Archaeological Department as
an Architectural Surveyor in 1905. U Mya
soon began to take interest in archaeologi-
cal research and being the only technical
hand under the Superintending Archaeolo-
gist, Mr. Taw Sein Ko, he was given an
extensive training in all branches of depart-
mental activity such as Epigraphy, Icono-
graphy, Numismatics, Excavation and
Conservation. He even spent sometime
at the excavations at Sarnath near Benares
in 1906 - 07.
By the time Mons. Charles Duroiselle
succeeded Mr. Taw Sein Ko in 1919
U Mya had already acquired a working
knowledge of Sanskrit as well as French.
Appreciating his capability and enthu-
siasm in his work the authorities appoint-
ed U Mya Archaeological Assistant in
1920 and later in 1925 promoted him
Assistant Superintendent. When Mons.
Duroiselle retired in 1930 U Mya was
mature enough to bear the burden of the
head of the Archaeological Survey of
Burma.
U Mya retired in 1935 being indis-
posed after having served thirty years
to earn a full pension. Relieved of onerous
duties U Mya continued to devote him-
self to epigraphical research. He also
recuperated his health by keeping to a
regulated and well-balanced diet so that
he was full of energy when under the
auspices of the Burma Translation Society
he gave a series of public lectures on
Burmese art and culture at the Society in
1953.

To enable him to impart his wealth
of knowledge and the fruits of his pains-
taking research work to the younger
generation of scholars U Mya was appo-
inted Officer on Special Duty in
Departement of Archaeology from 1st
September 1955 to 31st August 1960
during which period he produced two
important works, viz. “Votive Tablets of
Burma" in two volumes and "Introduc-
tion to Burmese Palaeography". In token
of his profound scholarship and esteemed
services the title of Thiripyanchi was
bestowed on U Mya in 1960.
Although by then an octagenarian
U Mya did not fail to maintain the closest
association with his former department.
Except on occasions of short spells of
sickness U Mya felt cosy and cheerful to
spend the days among the estampages,
antiques, old manuscripts and books in
the archaeological office at Mandalay.
Remarkable for his deep learning and
for his genial, broadminded tempara-
ment, he endeared himself to all the
members of the department. He would
not only advise and instruct the young
but also would keep himself afresh with
latest archaeological discoveries and epi-
graphical research. So for three more
years from 1963 he was again afforded

U Mya had contributed several learned
articles to the Journal of the Burma
Research Society, the most noteworthy
being Pumma Kran” (ပုမ္မကြည်). Apart
from departmental reports and the mono-
graphs mentioned above the following
articles are of outstanding merit: Some
Hitherto Unknown Coins in Burma, A
Note on the Nanpaya Temple, A Note on
Buddha's Footprints which were published
Thiripyanchi U Mya
in the Annual Reports of the Archaeologi-
cal Survey of India.
Recognizing his worthy contribution
to research work in Burma, particularly
in the fields of history, culture and
archaeology, the Burma Research Society
elected him Honorary Member in 1971,
a rare gesture of approbation on the part
of the Society, there being only three such
honoured scholars before he died.
U Mya had indeed left behind his
footprints on the sands of time for genera-
tions of scholars to aspire, and had filled
himself in a niche in the gallery of a few
great savants of Burma for all to admire.
U Aung Thaw
