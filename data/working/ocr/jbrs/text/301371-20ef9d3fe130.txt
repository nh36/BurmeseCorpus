"Certaine Words of Pegu Language"
Extract from a pamphlet in the Bodleian Library entitled "A True and
large Discourse of the Voyage of the whole Fleete of Ships set forth
the 20th of April, 1601…….
London 1603.”
Extract from a letter from Dr. Blagden-
"About three weeks ago Sir William Foster, formerly of the
India Office, who has written and published a good deal about the early
contacts of England and the East, made enquiry about a vocabulary
of the 'Pegu' language and enclosed a typed copy. I went through it
and with the help of Halliday's Dictionary I sent him my explanations.
In his letter of thanks he said that in the book he was going to publish
he was merely going to note that the vocabulary was in Mon, and he
sent back the vocabulary with the words Make what use you please
of the list.'
I have accordingly worked through it in detail and enclose a copy
of my results. Of the 55 items it contains, about a dozen seem to me
partly or wholly unintelligible; at any rate I have not been able to
identify them entirely with certainty. Probably you will be able to
do so, in whole or in part.
Having regard to the date of publication (1603), I think this must
be the oldest Mon-English vocabulary in existence and worth publishing
on that account. But in several cases it seems to me also to illustrate,
more or less exactly, how the language was pronounced at that time.
In a good many instances the pronunciation of the period is closer to
the spelling than to the modern pronunciation given by Halliday. It
may, however, in part be a matter of dialect. Foster thinks the 'Pegu'
vocabulary was compiled at Achin' (Northern Sumatra) 'where
vessels from Martaban, etc. were encountered' (by Sir James Lan-
caster's fleet on his first voyage (1601-3) for the East India Company).
So the Martaban dialect may give some clues, perhaps.
In the enclosed vocabulary I have given the modern spelling trans-
literated in the same way as on my dictionary cards but have copied
Halliday's spelling under the head modern pronunciation.
If thought suitable. the vocabulary might be published in the
Journal of the Burma Research Society, with acknowledgment of Sir
William Foster's kind permission.”
