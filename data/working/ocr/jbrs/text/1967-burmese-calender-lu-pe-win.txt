Every student of Burmese history is
fully aware of the fact that when the
Burmese emerged as a nation early in
the 11th century A.D. under the leadership
of king Aniruddha, popularly known as
Anoratha, the Pyüs were alrealy disintegra-
ting and in their last lap of racial extinction.
On the other hand the Mons of maritime
Burma had already become highly civil-
ised with their own advanced culture.
mostly, if not entirely, professing the
Theravada Buddhism.
Thus you will notice, the new nation,
as the Burmese were, in those early days,
had but to be inspired by their naighbour,
especially when the latter was predominantly
prosperous in culture.
Though culturally wealthy, the Mons
who were the earliest immigrants to the
entire South East Asia including probably
Indonesia and India, particularly the north-
eastern portion of the great peninsula and
even perhaps to the western-most part
of Asia, now known as the Middle East²,
had then become weak and poor politi-
cally, on account of sorrowful splits in
their camp.
The lack of unity and harmony among
the Mons offered a golden opportunity
for the early Burmese people to expand
their territory and eventually the Burmese
army led by King Aniruddha marched on
the Mon kingdom of Thaton, and con-
quering it, thence took away the king of
the Mons together with thirty sets of
Pāli Pițaka, along with all wise and learned
people, architects and artists, including
artisans and craftsmen to their capital,
Pagan, in central Burma.
In the wake of their conquest, the
Burmese conquerors began to learn and
absorb a great deal of, if not the whole
of, the culture of the conquered Mons
and thereby the political conquerors, namely,
the early Burmese, became culturally con-
quered, in due course, by the early Mons
in the early Burmese Empire.
This cultural conquest will become
crystal clear to you if I cite concrete
cases. As a result of the dissemination of
the Pāli piṭakas by the Mon monk
Dhammadassi Shin Arahan and his able
assistants, the whole kingdom of Pagan
soon became conversant with the sacred
Pali language and King Anoratha himself
could write autographs in Pali as borne
out by the terra cotta votive tablets which
have his seal on their reverse faces.
King Aniruddha alias Anoratha, marched
down to Thaton in the year 1056 A.D.
and the campaign was successfully com-
pleted in 1057 A.D. Aniruddha's immediate
successor was his son Sawloo, who was

By this piece of poetry, our sane sage, Seindakyawthu manifestly means: the kaukkyi crop of
paddy takes a long period of 126 days. Its cultivation must therefore begin befittingly from the time
the Khaya and Saga flowers bloom i.e. the months of Tagu and Kason by ploughing the fields and
applying cow-dung manure to them. In Nayon and Wazo the scattering of seed paddy is done.
Cultivators continue attending to their sprouting crops in the two months of Wagaung and Tawthalin.
In his မဟာသုဘကာရီမဃဒေ၀လင်္ကာကြီး Manle Sayadaw gives us a clearer picture of the cultivation
of not only the varieties of rice crops but also of sessamum seeds as follows:-
“မိုးကျိန်းကြိုက်ဟု၊ မိုးလိုက်မိုးခါ၊ မလွန်ရာဘဲး၊ ကြတ္တိကာကြယ်၊ ကြက်သူငယ်တို့၊ မိုးလယ်အချက်၊ အရုဏ်တက်၌၊ ထိန်လက်
