This work is designed to be a scholar's bibliography...covering
the whole Sino-Tibetan family of languages'. Arrangement is by
specific language or dialect, with the exception of Chinese whose
dialects are a alphabetically grouped under it. In major languages the
compiler provides sub-divisions such as grammar, dictionaries & inscrip-
tions. There is a little inaccuracy on page 15. The compiler puts
Carpani's Alphabetum Barmanum...' in the category of dictionaries
although it is, in fact, a grammar book. The name of the author of
'A Comparative Vocabulary of the Barma, Malayu and Thai
languages is not given (p. 15), and it is true that the title-page
of that particular book does not mention it either. But the reviewer
notes that according to · A grammar of the Malayan language with
an introduction and praxis' by William Marsden (p. xlvi), the author
is J. C. Leyden, M. D.
,
There is a general impression among bibliographers of today that
Judson's Burmese-English dictionary was firstly published in 1852.
Even the American Baptist Mission in Burma has inadvertently made
the mistake by publishing in 1953 what it calls the centenary edition.
And since the compiler accepts this without demur, it is well to verify
the 1st. date of publication of the dictionary. Henri Cordier in his
⚫ Bibliotheca Indo-Sinica' (p. 123), transcribes the title-page
Judson's work as follows:
of
'A dictionary of the Burman language, with explanations in
English-Compiled from the mss. of A. Judson... and of other mission-
aries in Burmah-Profits devoted to the support of the Burman Mission -
Calcutta: Printed at the Baptist Mission Press, Circular Road; and sold
by Messrs W. Thacker & Co., St. Andrew's library, Calcutta ; and by
the American Missionaries in Burmah. 1826'.
This is confirmed by Miss E. P. Quigly, late of the India House
library at London, in an article appearing in The Aryan Path' (p. 151,
April 1957).

There are some books and periodical articles which are worth noting
and which the compiler has failed to mention. Here are some of them:
) Pe Maung Tin, U - Some features of the Burmese language.
(JBRS 39 ( 1956) pp. 193–202. )
(2) Wright, E. The Anglo-Burmese Student's assistant, consisting
of grammatical notes with numerous examples and analysis
of sentences
...
1877.
(3) M.P.H.. Curiosities in the Burmese language. (JBRS 32
( 1948), pp. 62 – 72. )
(4) Hla Pe, U- Did Silavamsa ever write a poem on Burmese
porana? (JBRS 33 (1949), pp. 27–37 ).
(5) Luce, G. H. and Pe Maung Tin ed. - Inscriptions of Burma,
3 portfolios. University of Rangoon. Oriental Studies
Publication no. 2.
(6) Duroiselle, C. and Blagden, C.O. The Myazedi inscriptions.
(Epigraphia Birmanica. Vol I. Part I. )
(7) Tun Nyein, U - Inscriptions of Pagan, Pinya & Ava. Transla-
tion with notes. Rangoon, Government Press, 1899.
Despite these omissions, this is a most valuable reference work,
and very welcome.
