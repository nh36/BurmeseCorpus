BOOK REVIEWS
SOME OBSERVATIONS ON LIBRARIES, MANUSCRIPTS.
AND BOOKS OF BURMA from the 3rd. Century A. D. to 1886
by
Miss E. P. Quigly with a foreword by Dr. Than Tun.
Arthur Probsthain, London, W.C. I, 6s.
While struggling with the Present we have forgotten much of our
Past. The very little that we still remember, we remember vaguely,
scantily and confusedly. However, when some one sits down and works.
patiently on some topic about the Past, bits of It reveal themselves like
beautiful cameos, lost in the mediaeval dust and found again.
sources.
Such a cameo has lately come out in the form of a small book
called "Some Observations on Libraries, Manuscripts and Books of
Burma from the 3rd. Century A. D. to 1886”. This is the first modest
attempt on the subject and done by Miss E. Pauline Quigly, formerly
an Assistant in the library at India House, London. The essay is based
on archaeological finds, contemporary records and several other reliable
Her story of books and libraries among the Pyus, Mons and
Burmans is very interesting and may form a significant chapter in the
history of South East Asian culture. The author has made one very
important discovery, and that is "the first Burmese-English Dictionary
published in India in 1826". The essay also shows the beginnings of
printing in Burma. The pen-sketches are illustrative and helpful, except
the one on the inner title page, which clashes with Fytche's description
on p. 19. It may be read in conjunction with her very interesting articles
on the first Burmese-English Dictionary which appear in the Aryan
Path, April 1957 and Burma, Vol. VII, No. 3 (April 1957). We hope
earnestly, along with Dr. Than Tun, that Miss Quigly will further
enlarge her study of the subject and bring out a fuller and bigger book
in due course.
JBRS Vol. XL, Part 2 (a).
