THE FROGS AND TOADS OF THE
RANGOON
UNIVERSITY ESTATE
by
Frederick Delphin, M. Sc. 2.
The frogs and toads of the University Estate, Rangoon, have been
described because very little or no work has been done on them and the
standard references include only a few indigenous species. They fall
into six genera and eight species, namely: Rhacophorus leucomystax,
Rana limnocharis, Rana erythraea, Oxyglossus lima, Oxyglossus laevis,
Callula pulchra, Microhyla inornata, and Bufo melanostictus; of these
Oxyglossus is relatively rare.
Taxonomically, the frogs and toads belong to the Order Anura
which is divided into two Series, the Firmisternia and the Arcifera. In
the Firmisternia, the two halves of the pectoral girdle are fused in the
mid-ventral line while in the Arcifera, they overlap one another. The
Firmisternia includes two Families, namely the Ranidae and the
Engystomatidae, the former having toothed upper jaws and cylindrical
sacral diapophyses, and the latter toothless upper jaws and flattened
sacral diapophyses. The Ranidae includes the genera Rhacophorus,
Rana, and Oxyglossus, and the Engystomatidae the genera Callula and
Microhyla. The Arcifera is represented by one family, the Bufonidae
which, apart from the structure of the pectoral girdle, has the same
characteristics as the Engystomatidae, i.e. toothless upper jaws and
flattened sacral diapophyses, and is represented by the single genus Bufo.

tion of the frogs and toads of the University Estate, Rangoon :

The two species Oxyglossus lima and Oxyglossus leavis are
commonly known as "Thai-hpa" in Burmese meaning sand frogs,
because when disturbed in their natural habitat, they retreat back-
wards into the mud and debris. They are the rarest and smallest
species to be found on the University Estate. Both species found in
the same locality, i.e. a region of plentiful stagnant water; croak of
both species a peculiar nasal-like "ping-ping-ping-ping" or "keeng-
keeng-keeng-keeng"; body stretched forwards during croaking, and the
internal vocal sac appears as an inflated rubber balloon the size of the
head of the animal; when annoyed or disturbed, the air is released
instantly and the animal darts away almost simultaneously; very hard
to detect in their natural surroundings due to colour harmony between

Discussion
The specimens identified as Rana erythraea (Schlegel), 1837 need
further investigation; they are identified as such because they approxi-
mate most closely to it but the writer feels that there are many
characters which are peculiar to them and not found in Rana erythraea
(Schlegel), 1837. The writer hopes to continue the investigation when
an opportunity arises, as, at present, lack of references does not permit
him to decide whether a new species should be created or not.
The species identified as Microhyla inornata Boulenger, 1890 also
needs investigation. The writer has taken his specimens to be this
species because of the unwebbed condition of the hind feet. However,
the possession of two metatarsal tubercles places them in the species
Microhyla ornata (Dum. and Bibr.), 1841.
With reference to the pectoral girdle, the general contention is that
the arciferous condition is more primitive than the firmisternia condi-
tion, the reason being that from the former the latter can be obtained
by the fusion of the two ventral halves of the girdle. From the
firmisternia condition, by the absence of the clavicles, the condition
found in Callula and Microhyla is obtained, and in these two genera, no
omosternum and episternum are present. On the other hand, when
the clavicles are present, as in the genera Rhacophorus, Rana and
Oxyglossus, the omosternum and episternum are always present. Of
the last three genera, the sternum is cartilaginous in Oxyglossus,
denoting a primitive condition.
