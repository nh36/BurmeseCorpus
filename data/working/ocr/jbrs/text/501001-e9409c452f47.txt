Solar Energy
All our food and fuel derive their
energy originally from the sun through
photosynthesis in plants. Although our
foods are grown annually, our fuels in
the form of coal, oil and gas cannot be
reproduced. Our fossil fuels were produced
millions of years ago; but they are essen-
tially irreplaceable. In Burma we are finding
more oil, gas and coal. However. we must
be sure that they will be used up
eventually.
The solar radiation is our great source
of continuing power. The sun sends out
electromagnetic radiation from its surface
having an estimated temperature of 6000°C.¨

The first attempt was to design cheap
solar cookers using local materials as far as

It was claimed that a total of 40 sq.ft of

The next design made use of a smaller

The third cooker had non-symmetric

Wind Energy
Wind energy may be used profitably in
some areas of Burma. The Union of Burma
Applied Research Institute is at present
making a survey of regions where meteoro-
logical conditions and local circumstances
favour use of wind energy. So far, reports
show that wind power could be used for
pumping water in some Dry Zone areas.
where there is acute shortage of water, and
where the wind force is suffiient for use in
most parts of the year, and also for pump-
ing sea water for manufacture of salt in
certain coastal regions.

Atomic Energy
Apart from searching for uranium and
thorium in certain regions, Burma has not
made much effort for employing atomic
energy for the purpose of energy produc-
tion. Because of her great potential hydro-
electric power resources, it is perhaps
generally believed that the need to harness
atomic energy is not so urgent as some of
her neighbouring countries think. However,
the Union of Burma Atomic Energy Centre,
Agricultural Research Institute, Arts and
Science Universities at Rangoon and
Mandalay, and a few other government
departments are training in nuclear physics
and carrying out researches in application
of radioisotopes to certain health and
agricultural problems.
At present the Union of Burma Atomic
Energy Centre is engaged in assaying source
materials, collecting up-to-date reading
materials to keep in touch with the de-
velopment of research and power reactors,
doing researches in health physics and
studying possible applications of radio-
isotopes in industries.
At the Agricultural Research Institute
important investigations are being carried
out on proper use of chemical fertilizers in
agriculture. Valuable results have been
found by U Khin Win of the Institute on
the choice of fertilizers for rice growing in
Lower Burma, of time and place for
efficient application of fertilizer. Under the
contract with the International Atomic
Energy Agency, the Institute is participating
in the coordinated programme of investiga-
tions with radioisotopes on rice growing
together with Hungary, Egypt, Pakistan,
India, Thailand, Philippines and Japan.

Even though the immediate use in
Burma of nuclear power production for
generation of electricity is not apparent,
there are groups of scientists in the univer-
sities and research institutes who are keeping
abreast with the growing knowledge of
nuclear reactor physics, ready to be mobili-
sed should the Government eventually decide
to go in for nuclear power programmes.
