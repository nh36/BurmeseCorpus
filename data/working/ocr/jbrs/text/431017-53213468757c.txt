NOTES ON THE DISTRIBUTION OF CHINESE PARNASSIA
by
CHIEN CHO-PO (Academia Sinica)
Parnassia in its world range contains, as the present knowledge goes, about sixty species,
widely distributed in the North Temperate Zone. Of this number, about 60% are found in
China, being especially rich in the southwestern part.
The dispersal of Parnassia depends almost exclusively on dissemination, and their seeds
do not scatter very widely from the parent plants. On account of this, the ability to disperse
itself is as a rule quite limited, In such kind of plants, it is easier to trace along their route
of dispersal the correlation exting between distribution and the morphological changes that
had since occured, thereby to determine the centre of origin and to learn other problems
relating to phylogentic development. The present paper is a preliminary treatment, and its
scope is at present limited to those species that are found in China. The discussion has taken
as its basis the sections of the genus. Although various morphological characters have been
duly cared for, but for simplification, only the staminodes, as a most essential organ in the
distinction of sections and species, has been taken into account. It is to be noted here that
Sect. Nectarotrilobos Drude has been excluded from this paper, pending on further research,
the result of which will be published separately later on.
During the progress of the present work, I had received much help from Messrs. Li
An-jen, Ma Chen-kung and Misses Mao Tsu-mei and Li Ya-ju in arranging material, making
the map of distribution and in certain cases supplied valuable suggestions. I would like to
take this opportunity to express my gratitude to them. To Mr. Feng Tsin-jung, who has
kindly done all the illustrations, I owe many thanks. The author is also indebted to Prof.
Tsoong Pu-chiu for many helpful suggestion during the course of my work.
Sect. Nectarodroson Drude (see Map 1)
In China, this section has only one species, P. palustris Linn. (number of setae of stami-
nodes 7-15), its var. multiseta Ledeb. (number of setae 17-23) and one other variety recorded
in Taiwan ¹. P. palustris var. palustris has a very wide distribution, being found in the North
Temperate Zone in Europe, Asia and North America. In China, it has the North-eastern
Provinces, the eastern part of Inner Mongolia and North China as its eastern area, while in
the west, it occurs in Sinkiang. The var. multiseta is distributed in Sajan and Kamtchatka,
USSR. This variety from the north comes down to China; here its eastern range is approxi-
mately the same as the var multiseta 2. From the phylogenetic point of view, the staminodes
are derived from the stamens, and the numbers of setae change from less to more numerous
(see Fig. 1 a-k), thus it may be surmised that the direction of distribution of var. multiseta in
China is from north to south. Viewing from the fact that there is only the presence of var.
palustris in Sinkiang, it might be that the Flora of Sinkiang is influenced more by Central
Asia.

Sect. Fimbripetalum Drude (see Map 2)
The plants of this section are distributed in India, China and Japan. The centre of area
of this section is in China. There are six species in China. The member with the widest
range is P. foliosa Hk. f. et Thoms. (see Fig. 2 a). In eastern China, it has its larger area to
the south of Yangtze River in Anhwui and Kiangsi Provinces, and is discontinuous in
Pinshan district in Szechuan and Tali in Yunnan. It also reaches Khasia Mts. in Assam
and Japan.
It may be looked upon as an old species formerly widely distributed along a
line of dispersion from Assam in the west through Yunnan, Szechuan, Hunan, Kiangsi,
Anhwui to Kiangsu and also to Japan (Nagasaki) in the east. Along its line of dispersion,
three specics with 4-6 setae were derived in the southeastern Szechuan, namely, P. perciliata
Diels, P. davidii Franch. and P. arenicola C. P. Chien (in sched.) (see Fig. 2 b and c). The
first one was found on rocks at about 1100 m. alt. in Mt. Kingfu of Nanchuan District,
Szechuan, the second one was collected between Ichang and Chungking in humid place in
the valley of Yang-tze River, and the last one from the sandy beaches along the same river
in Wan Hsien, Szechuan. Among the three, P. davidii and P. arenicola are particularly
allied to each other in the characters of petals and staminodes, but the leaf-bases in the
former is cordate while those in the latter is cuneate. The staminodes in both are 4-5 in
number, but those P. perciliata may reach six in number; and it seems to denote therefore,
that the letter is more advanced than the other two. P. foliosa begot P. amoena Diels
(see Fig. 2 e) in southeastern Szechuan near Nanchuan District. And at last P. amoena, in
its migration northward produced P. noemiae Franch. (see Fig 2 d). From the above-stated,
it may be surmised that the centre of origin of Section Fimbripetalum is along the Yangtze
some distance south of Nanchuan and Iping (of Szechuan province). It may be illustrated

Sect. Saxifragastrum Drude (see Map 3)
According to present knowledge, this section is found to be confined solely to the
southwestern part of China and upper Burma. There is a total of seven species. The chief
distinguishing character lies in its simple staminodes. The origin of this section very
likely may be traced back to Section Nectarotrilobos Drude. In 1896, Franchet when
describing his new species, P. yunnanensis, pointed out "Le P. yunnanensis forme ainsi
le passage entre la section Nectarotrilobos et celle des Saxifragastrum”. From my observa-
tion of P. yunnanesis and P. longistipitata C. P. Chien (in sched.) (see Fig. 3b), there has
been detected too the tendency in the staminodes being faintly trilobed, and this proves that
the observation of Franchet is correct. Since from the morphological point of view, P.
longistipitata and P. yunnanensis are comparatively primitive, thus the centre of origin of
this section is supposed to be somewhere between Dawu and Kangting in western Szechuan.
Two species with very similar staminodes were produced along the south-westward migra-
tory route of P. yunnanensis and P. longistipitata, i. e. P. longipetala Hand.--Mzt. and its
more advanced relative P farreri W. E.Evans 1. (see Fig. 3 c and d), while in its way south-
Although W. E. Evans describes Parnassia Farreri without denoting the section to which it
belongs, yet it is clearly understood from his original description "Staminodia...limbo in-
tegro supra obscure sinuato" and his notes "Inter species staminodiis integris munitas haec
caulibus floriferis simplicibus, petalis niveis infra longe fimbriatis, stigmatibus sessilibus est
valde distincta" that he regards it as a species of Section Saxifragastrum. Subsequently,
however, Handel-Mazzetti put it under Sect. Nectarotrilobos, considering it as a close ally of
P. chinensis. My observation, especially on the staminodes, shows that its nearest ally is P.
longipetala Hand-Mzt. which was transfered later by the author himself from Sect. Nec-
tarotrilobos to Sect. Saxifragastrum. On the same reason given by Handel-Mazzetti in Symb.
Sin. 7:435 (1931) on the transference of P. longi-petala, I prefer to put P. farreri under Sect.
Saxi-fragastrum. The type locality of this species is Chimili valley of Upper Burma.
The structure of staminodes is the particular character. As may be seen in the text-figure, the
longitudinal section of staminodes is in T shape, the top-surface being either triangular or semi-
spherical with the anterior side crenate and slightly grooved, and the posterior side entire. It
is interesting that my observation is quite different from the Eichinger's figure shown in Engler
Pflanzenfamilien ed. 2, 18 a: 182, f. 100 V (1930), and I hope to draw the attention of botanists
who have the opportunity to study on this species.

Sect. Cladoparnassia
(see Map 4)
According to the past record, this section contains only a single species, i. e. P. faberi
Oliv., with a form(f. abbreviata Engl.); their range being restricted to Mt. Omei in Szechuan
Province. Recently, a new species, P. labiata 1. C. P. Chien (in sched.) was discovered in
Pao-hsing and Mapien, two districts each distanced about one hundred kilometers to the
south and north of Omei. The essential difference of this new species is in the form of
its staminode which has one "lip" entire, while the other "lip" is 2-5 lobed (see Fig. 4 b
and c).
