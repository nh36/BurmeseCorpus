DR. BA HAN
An Obituary
It is often said that men of the law
live long. If it is not true of some
lawyers, it is certainly true of Dr. Ba Han,
who, at the time of his death on 13th
May, 1969, was more than 79 years old-
only a few months short of 80. This
longevity is especially remarkable in a
country where the average expectation of
life is said to be less than 50.
Born in Rangoon on 30th January
1890 of 'U Kye and Daw Thein Tin,
young Ba Han began his education at the
Government Middle School, Maubin, at
the age of six. In 1900 he joined the
then prestigious St. Paul's Institute, a
Christan High School in Rangoon, together
with his younger brother Ba Maw, who
later became Burma's Head of State
under the Japanese.
'
In 1905, Maung Ba Han joined the
Rangoon College, which was then a con-
stituent College of Calcutta University.
In 1909 he graduated B.A. 'with distinc-
tion in all the subjects.' Three years later,
he joined the Subordinate Civil Service
and became an Assistant Government
Translator in 1913 under the well-known
Burmese lexicographer U Tun Nyein, who
was then Government Translator.
Maung Ba Han then registered himself
as a part-time non-collegiate student with
Calcutta University for his M.A. degree
in English, which he obtained in 1915.
Later, he succeeded U Tun Nyein as
Government Translator, But circumstances
made him decide to study law.
In 1922 Maung Ba Han took study
leave and left for England to join the
Lincoln's Inn. At the same time he was
admitted to Cambridge University for a
course leading to the Ph. D. degree. Before
the completion of this course, Maung Bu
Han joined the Bordeaux University in
France for his D. Litt. degree. The title
of his thesis was William Blake: His
Mysticism. He later received his Ph. D.
degree in literature from the University
of Freiburg in Germany. His Ph. D.
thesis was again entitled: Evolution of
Blakean Philosophy.
With all these degrees in letters, Dr.
Ba Han was well on the road to a brilliant
academic career. Instead, after being
called to the English bar in 1925, he
returned to Burma and decided to follow
the more mundane but highly lucrative
legal career. Being a man of great in-
dustry and intellectual ability, Dr. Ba Han
soon made his mark as a practising lawyer.
In his own words: "Those were boom
days in the rice market. The people
were flushed with money. I was thus
enabled to forge ahead in the legal pro- ·
fession with scarcely any struggle.”

of law Dr. Ba Han joined the Faculty of
Law in the University of Rangoon as a

part-time lecturer in 1928. Seven years
later, in 1935, he became part-time Pro-
fessor of Law and then Dean of the
Faculty of Law when the latter position
was created in 1945. In 1950 Dr. Ba Han
retired from the Chair of Law. In con-

Emeritus Professor of Law.
As a practising lawyer, Dr. Ba Han
was a dominating figure in the profession
and was for many years a member and
Vice-Chairman of the Bar Council. In
1958 he was invited by the then Govern-
ment to serve as Attorney-General. To
secure his services Parliament had to
amend the Attorney-General's Act
raise the chief law officer's monthly salary
from K 2,500 to K 3,000. When a change
of Government took place in the same
year, Dr. Ba Han returned to private
practice where he remained till the last
moment of his life.

Dr. Ba Han was a life-member of
the Burma Research Society and became
its President in 1963-64. He contributed
many articles to the Society's Journal.
His magnum opus is undoubtedly the
monumental University English-Burmese
Dictionary, the compilation of which he
began in 1951, when he was already 61
years old, and completed in 1966. The
successful completion of this huge under-
taking obviously brought relief to the then
76-year-old lawyer-lexicographer, for, in
the 'Valedictory,' which appears at the end
of the big volume, Dr. Ba Han wrote:
“The last final proof of my Dictionary
has been passed. I now lay down my
pen with a deep, deep sense of thankful-
ness to God for having spared me to
complete the Dictionary single-handed
for a period of some fifteen years."
Dr. Ba Han's lesser-known works
are: The Planned State, published in 1945
and A Legal History of India and Burma,
published in 1951.
