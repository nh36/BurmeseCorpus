STATISTICAL ANALYSIS OF FARM SIZE
AND CURRENT EXPENDITURES*
[A brief consideration of the statistical aspects of farms enumerated in
the 1953-54 Agricultural Census of Burma.]
Introduction
U Saw Win**
The first post-war census taken in
1953-54 introduced simultaneously both
the Agricultural census and the popula-
tion census for the first time. Therefore
a wealth of new information became
available. Unfortunately complete enumera-
tion was not possible mainly due to
unsettled conditions of the country at
that time.
The Agricultural Census was obtained
by asking a trigger question so as to
determine whether a respondent was a
farmer in the "complete population census".
If so, the farmer is given the Agricultural
Schedule which is given as appendix to
this report. The questionnaire contains
12 sections dealing with, farm operator;
total area tenure and location; Crops;
Storage, marketing and wunza; land utiliza-
tion; agricultural population and employ-
ment; equipment; livestock and poultry;
irrigation and drainage; expenditure; loans,
interest and debts; and value, rent and taxes.
In pre-war days great deal of infor-
mation was collected as a by-product of
land revenue Adminstration and published
in settlement reports, Season and Crop
Reports; and Marketing-Section surveys.
**
The settlement Reports were parti-
cularly valuable but they were conducted
non-synchronously, district by district,
covering the whole country in 20 years.
As land revenue was fixed on per acre
basis, there was no interest in the size
of farms except in some settlement reports.
The result of the 53-54 Agricultural census
gives farm size for the whole country
for the first time. This may be con-
sidered as the high-light of this Agri-
cultural Census.
This paper is divided into two sections.
The first section will deal with the size
of farms enumerated in the census in
which the relation between the size of
the farms and the location, and the size
distribution of farms have been examined.
The second section will deal with the
current expenditures on the operation of
farms. A comparison between the Urban
and the Rural farms will be made and
the reasons for these differences will be
given in the last part of the second
section.

on farm sizes. But owing to the nature
of this scheme, no synchronous data was
collected for the whole country at any time.
The Agricultural Census obtained data
about farm sizes among others and most

censuses were published, cross-tabulated
by farm size. Therefore data on farm-

According to the census department,
a farmer is a person who operates a
farm, and a farm is a piece or pieces
of land standing close together or far
apart managed by one farmer. This farm
concept is new to this country. The
common unit is a holding which is a
unit of assessment. It is a piece of land
which may be owned or occupied by a
person. He may, however have more
than one holding. He may work on one
or more holdings and may rent out others.
There are also large holdings which may
be partly worked by the owner and partly
by tenant or tenants. A holding therefore
has no economic significance and the new
concept of a farm has to be adopted for
the census purposes.

The 1954 Census was taken in 2129
village tracts all over Burma. The plan to
cover all village tracts in 1954 had to be
abandoned owing to unsettled conditions in
many parts of the country. Therefore only
a part of the area not covered by the 1953
Census would be covered by 1954 Census.
It is estimated that rural population in 1954
was about 17 million,' so on a population
basis the 1954 Census covered about 17.55%
of the rural population. The actual village
tracts entering the 1954 Census were chosen
primarily for their security condition. It
may therefore be suspected that most of the
village tracts were close to the towns and
that they may therefore be much influenced
by their urban neighbourhood. The 1954
Census cannot be considered a representative
sample of the rural areas.
