RECORDINGS OF BURMESE CLASSICAL MUSIC
These are times in which the Buddhist word
(Impermanence)
is uttered with greater conviction than ever by Burmese lips. All things
are impermanent. But there is one thing which shows signs of being even
more impermanent than it should fairly be - Burmese Music, whose
continued existence is in such jeopardy that the preservation of it has come
to assume the aspect of a lost cause.
The beautiful old tunes have never been written down. No technical
study of them has yet been undertaken. For years the gramophone record
companies have abandoned the recording of classical music, as it has been
to them, no doubt, a dead loss. Instead there has been a crescendo of what
is called modern Burmese music. Modern, it unquestionably is to us. But
whether the majority of it is either Burmese or music may be questioned.
However, that is the phenomenon which has overspread our musical skies.
"Like the bright hair uplifted from the head
Of some fierce Maenad, even from the dim verge
Of the horizon to the zenith's height
By 1939 the position of true Burmese music was desperate. The
forces arrayed against her were many and powerful-radio-broadcasting,
gramophone business, cinema films, American jazz and western musical
instruments. In addition to these external forces, there was the internal
defection of the modern 'taste.' Meanwhile, her faithful devotees, in whose
memory alone her truest traditions resided, neglected and poverty stricken,
old and infirm, were ever drawing nearer to the grave, whither Burmese
music must willy-nilly follow, since it was being neither written down nor
recorded. It seemed, unless a champion appeared miraculously, Burmese
music was doomed.
At the eleventh hour the miracle happened. On 11th August 1939, the
Burma Research Society resolved to inform the Government that "... the
Society views with grave concern the approaching extinction of the old
Burmese musical tradition and is convinced that the only practical method
of preserving it is to make gramophone records of the voices of those
singers in whom the tradition resides; that, in view of the age and infirmity
of most of the singers, the making of the records is a matter which admits
of no delay; the society is devoting what funds it can spare to the recording
of [verse-recitations] but is quite unable to deal more adequately with the
emergency, and would earnestly press on the Government the urgent need
for the immediate provision of funds for the purpose, estimated at about
Rs. 5,000, pointing out that if the work is not done now it can never be
done."
The Government responded generously to the Society's appeal. The
music Sub-committee laboured with enthusiasm. In November 1940 with

It must be pointed out that disc numbers BRS.15-BRS.20 are not
recordings of music but of the way of reading' various Burmese literary
forms, such as ratu, rakan, etc. recorded straight from the lips of the one
remaining exponent, Aweyaukmin U Aung Gyi, court-reciter during the
reigns of King Mindon and Thibaw. To please the ear is beyond the
powers of vocal cords 88 years old. But there is one specimen BRS.18,
side-OMH 6706, which is a rendering of Eingyin by Saw Mya Aye Kyi in
implicit obedience to the teaching of Aweyaukmin. Ears unattuned to our
ways may find it not impossible to be tolerant to this specimen.
BRS. 21 is a record of customary music Nayee played in court to
mark certain hours of the day for royal ears. Presumably this was what Mr.
Collis heard at Mongmit and referred to as the Retreat in his book "The
Lords of Sunset." The prominent feature is the drumming of Bôndaung.
BRS. 22 contains two opposite types of drum music. Royal drum
Hmyauk-st is on side -OHM 6950, and folk-drum Bôngyithan is on side
OMH 6951.
The remaining fourteen records represent the various forms of
Burmese classical music-Kyoh, Bwè, Thachingan, Patpyo, Yodaya, each
having a distinct mode of its own, viz. dominant, sub-dominant, and so on.
These points are discussed in "Burmese Music, A preliminary enquiry," by
Khin Zaw. Journal of the Burma Research Society, December 1940.

not taught to singers. Nobody explains to them the nature, nurture, and care
of vocal organs. To us, learning to sing means learning the mysteries of
singing different classes of songs with special emphasis on time, ornamenta-
tion, and getting in and out of the accompanying instrumental patterns
(တီးကွက်), If a singer is blessed with a beautiful voice she has so much
more advantage over her rivals. If in addition, she is well up in the
mysteries, and equipped with a large repertoire, her fame is assured. She
proceeds to exploit her voice mercilessly. The greater her fame is, the
greater strain her vocal cords have to suffer. Generally she ends up some-
where in the middle thirties with her voice in ruins, and her reputation at
the mercy of younger rivals, who in their turn, will travel the same road as
she has travelled, to arrive at the same state of learned impotence. Mean-
while, she pits her cunning against their vocal powers. More and more
recourse is taken to clever dodges - alternation of registers; more and more
learning is displayed in the appurtenances (mm) of singing, in short, there
is less and less singing. There is a Parlophone record (B. 42128) of Saw
Mya Aye Kyi's singing when she was much younger than she is now. That
was beautiful singing. Alas, one notices also that she was in all innocence
murdering her voice. This sort of murder, so far from shocking the
musicians, impresses them, and they encourage it. Poor singers! What-
ever the real compass of their voices, they all turn Coloratura Soprano.
They vie with one another in singing at a higher and yet higher pitch.
Often they strike notes one octave higher than is necessary - notes not
being fixed for them on staves as in written music. Little wonder their
voices are ruined. Little wonder the art of singing resolves into the artifice
of fitting the vocal song into the instrumental pattern (08:05), so that,
(တီးကွက်),
quite young girls with easily manageable voices may often be seen lazily
refusing to raise or sustain the voice in passages where it might be raised
or sustained, but just getting through the passage with no more than a
correct fitting of the song to the pattern. Thus singing comes to be defined
by many authorities as knowing the way in and out of me, and a singer
as one who knows by heart many songs. Such unfamiliar methods of
valuation have to be borne in mind when listening to Burmese singing.

ရှေးခေတ်ဟောင်းအောင်းမေ့ဘွယ်။
မြန်မာပြည်၏ သုခုမပညာတရပ်ဖြစ်၍ မြန်မာတို့၏ ယဉ်ကျေးမှုအဝင်အပါ၊ ပျို့ကဗျာလင်္ကာ၊
မြန်မာစာကြီးပေကြီးတို့သည်။ တိမ်ကော ပျောက် ကွယ် ခြင်း မရှိသေး သော်လည်း၊ မြန်မာ မင်း
အဆက်ဆက်တို့၏ ရွှေနားတော်သွင်းဖြစ်သော ထိုကဗျာလင်္ကာတို့၏ သီဆိုဘတ်ရွတ်နည်းတို့ကား။
တတ်ကျွမ်းသောပုဂ္ဂိုလ်ကြီးများ ကွယ်လွန်ကုန်ကြခြင်းကြောင့် တကြောင်း၊ အားပေးချီးမြှင့်သူတို့မရှိ
သဖြင့်အလေ့အလာ နည်းပါးကြခြင်းကြောင့်တကြောင်း။
တစတစတိမ်ကော ပျောက်ကွယ်လာခဲ့ရာ။
ယခုအခါတွင်၊ ရတု၊ရကန်၊အဲ၊ အဏ်၊ဧချင်းစသောကဗျာမျိုးတို့ကို၊ မှန်ကန်စွာသီဆိုဘတ်ရွတ်နိုင်သူ
တို့မရှိသလောက်ဖြစ်နေတော့သဖြင့်၊နောင်ခေတ်၊နောင်အခါတွင်၊ နောက်သားနောင်လာတို့သည်။
ဤထီးသုံး နန်းလေ့ ဖြစ်သော မြန်မာယဉ်ကျေးမှု ဆိုင်ရာ၊ စာပေရွတ်ဘတ်နည်းတို့ကို၊ အမွှေခံနိုင်ကြ
တော့မည်မဟုတ်ဘဲ၊ အရှုံးကြီး ရှုံး၍၊ အဆုံးကြီးဆုံးရတော့မည့်အခြေသို့ ရောက်ရှိနေပေတော့ရကား။
“မြန်မာပြည်သုတေသနအသင်းကြီး”က နောက်သားနောင်လာတို့၏ အကျိုးကိုမျှော်ကိုး၍ ဤယဉ်
ကျေးမှုဆိုင်ရာပညာရပ်တို့ကိုမတိမ်ကောမပျောက်ကွယ်ရအောင်လုံခြုံစွာသိမ်းဆည်းသိုမှီးရန်ရည်ရွယ်
ချက်ဖြင့်၊မင်းတုန်းမင်းတရားကြီးနှင့် သီပေါမင်းတရားကြီးတို့ ရွှေလက်ထက်တော် နှစ်ဆက်တိုင်တိုင်၊
နေမျိုးမင်းထင်ကျော်ခေါင်၊နေမျိုးဇေယျကျော်ခေါင်ဟူသောရွှေဘွဲ့တံဆိပ်ဖြင့်အဝေးရောက်ရာထူးကို
ထမ်းရွက်ခဲ့ရသော“အဝေးရောက်မင်းဦးအောင်ကြီး”အား၊ပညာအမွေပေးဝေဖြန့်ချီပါမည့်အကြောင်း
တောင်းပန်ကာ၊ ရတု၊ရကန်၊အဲချင်း၊ အဏ်ချင်း၊ လူးတား၊မော်ကွန်း၊ တောလား၊ ဧချင်း၊သာချင်း၊
လေးချိုကြီးတို့၏မူမှန်သီဆိုဘတ်ရွတ်နည်းတို့ကို၊ဓါတ်ပြားသွင်းယူလိုက်ပါကြောင်း။ ထိုမှတပါးမဟာ
ဂီတဆိုင်ရာရွေးကဗျာသီချင်းကြီးများ၏။သီဆိုနည်း။ တီးမှတ်နည်းတို့ကိုချွတ်ယွင်းပျောက်ကွယ်ခြင်းမ
ရှိစေရန်ရှေးဆရာကြီးများ။ အဆိုတော်ကြီးများကို မှီလိုက်ကြသဖြင့် သီဆိုတီးမှုတ်နည်းတို့ကို၊ အာစရိ
ယဝါဒဟတ္ထမုဋ္ဌိလက်ဆုပ်လက်ကိုင်ရရှိလိုက်ကြသောအဆိုတော်၊စောမြဧကြည်၊အဆိုတော်ဒေလီညွန့်၊
ကွမ်းရည်တော်စောင်းတော်တီး ဒေဝဣန္ဒါ၏ တပည့်ရင်းဆရာကြီးဦးအောင်လှ၊ ပတ္တလျားဆရာကြီး
များဖြစ်ကြသောဆရာန်ကြီး၊မန္တလေးဆရာမောင်တို့နှင့်ရှေးသီချင်းဓါတ်ပြားများကိုလည်းနောင်လာ
နောက်သားတို့အတွက် မြန်မာပြည်သုတေသနအသင်းကြီးကဓါတ်ပြားသွင်းလိုက်ပါသဖြင့်၊ မြန်မာ
