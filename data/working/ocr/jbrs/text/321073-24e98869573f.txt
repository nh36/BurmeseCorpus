THE POSITION OF
PASTEURELLOSIS IN BURMA.
By
II. Kraw. Ph.D. (Cantab.) M.R.C.V.S.
Pasteurellosis is a very serious disease in Burma.
The Annual Reports of the Civil Veterinary Department,
Burma, 'put pasteurellosis in cattle (hæmorrhagic Septi-
cæmia) and that in buffaloes (baibone) under one title:
"Hæmorrhagic Septicemia in Bovine". No attempt has
been made to distinguish between the two diseases in
these returns.
Pasteurellosis of wild cats in captivity in the Zoo
has been reported once (1938).
Hæmorrhagic Septicemia in bovines is the cause of
the second greatest annual mortality amongst these ani-
mals. The disease is more prevalent in buffaloes than in
cattle. The distribution of the disease is of particular
interest. Although it has been reported from almost all the
provinces, it is particularly prevalent in the Irrawaddy
Delta and in the maritime provinces. The bovines of
Upper Burma appear to suffer from this disease more
than those of the Shan States which appear to suffer very
little. The incidence of the disease reaches its peak
during the monsoon months, i. c. from May to October.
From the accompanying graphs, it will be seen that
there is a very sharp rise in the mortality rate with the
approach of the monsoon, and an equally sharp fall when
the rains are over.
Attempts to control the disease had been made
by subcutaneous injection of killed cultures of strains
isolated from cattle and buffaloes in various parts of
Burma; i. e. to say, by the use of a polyvalent vaccine.
It was found that best growth was obtained in broch at
pH 7.2 at 37°C. The culture was killed by formaline
and preserved in 1:500 of the same.

From the accompanying figures it will be seen
that the vaccine proved fairly effective in preventing the
disease.
Due to the difficulty of carrying a miseroscope
to outlying areas and perhaps also to delay in reporting
outbreaks it had not been possible for the officers in the
field to differentiate hæmorrhagic septicemia from
anthrax. The Civil Veterinary Department, therefore, has
asked its field officers to send in "dried cars" of dead
animals to the Research Laboratory at Insein for con-
firmation of the diagnosis. There it was found out that
some cases suspected to be anthrax were hæmorrhagic
septicemia and vice versa. It was also not uncommon
to encounter these two diseases at the same time in the
same area. Therefore, starting in 1938, anthrax spore and
hæmorrhagic septicemia vaccines. were inoculated
simultaneously into cattle in such areas. This method of
inoculation proved to be extremely satisfactory.
Inoculations were most effective when given
before the rainy season, but it was found very difficult to
persuade cultivators to have their animals inoculated
while there was no actual outbreak of the disease. As
soon as there was an outbreak there was a demand for
inoculation. However, as the disease is acute and
outbreaks pass their peak periods so rapidly, it is not
possible to check them and thus impress the cultivators
with the value of preventive inoculation.

It is noticable in the above tables that the number
of outbreaks, mortality, doses of vaccines issued and the
number of animals inoculated, all show a steady increase.
This is probably accounted for by the gradual education
of the mass of cultivators.
The increase in number of outbreaks where anthrax
spore and Hæmorrhagic Septicemia vaccines were
simultaneously inoculated into correspondingly larger
number of cattle, actually exceeding the numbers inocula-
ted only with hæmorrhagic septicemia vaccine in 1939-40
and 1940-41, may be taken as a reflection upon the greater
reliability and efficiency of the simultaneous incoulation,
though other reasons for the increase in vaccines issued
could be adduced.
