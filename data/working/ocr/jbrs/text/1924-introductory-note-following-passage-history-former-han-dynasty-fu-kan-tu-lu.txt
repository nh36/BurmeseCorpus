Han dynasty (206 B.C.-25 A.D.) was first translated into French by Professor
Pelliot in Toung Pao, Vol. XIII, pp. 457-461, in a review of Hirth and
Rockhill's translation of Chao Ju-kua. He introduces his version with the
following words :-
"Messrs. Hirth and Rockhill state that there is no trace of Chinese relations
with the countries of the south, India and the west by way of the sea before
the arrival of the strangers who, in 166 A.D., gave themselves out to be envoys
from the emperor Marcus Aurelius. I have already suggested a possible.
identification, with Java¹, of a kingdom of ☑☐ Yeh-tiao, from which an
embassy arrived at the court of China in Jan-Feb. 132 (B.E.F.E.-O., IV, 266);
this explanation still seems to me probable, and I remind the reader that here.
we shall find, half a century before Ptolemy, the first dated mention of the name,
Java.
But above all I am surprised not to have seen quoted anywhere a
passage of cardinal importance, in the Ch'ien han shu, which is not the less
valuable because the names given in it are mysterious."

1. Pelliot, who translates "seven inches", says: "I have taken wei in its
technical sense of "a measure of five inches"; for the moment, however, I can think
of no other instance in which "seven inches" is expressed, as here, by "a wei and two
inches." The Rev. S. Wan Hui prefers to take wei in its ordinary sense "to sur-
round,” i.e, “2 inches in circumference," and this is perhaps more probable.
2. "It refers to the time when Wang Mang's influence was all-powerful, but before
he became regent and anyhow before he had yet himself usurped the throne."
(Pelliot).

islands of this name. See J.A., juillet-août 1919, pp. 46-47, 60.

(Pelliot). See also T'oung Pao, Vol. XI, pp. 322.324; B.E.F.E.-O., 1914, No. 9,
