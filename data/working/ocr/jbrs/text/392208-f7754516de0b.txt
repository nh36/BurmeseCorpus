THE FERNS AND FERN-ALLIES OF RANGOON-INSEIN AREA-
AN ADDENDA
by
P. S. Bell, M. Sc., A.H.B.T.I.
Continuing my work on "The Ferns and Fern-Allies of the
Rangoon-Insein Area", after it was published in the Journal of the
Burma Research Society, Vol. XXXVI, Part 2, December 1953, I have
been able to establish the names of some of the unidentified species
recorded in the paper.
Specimen No. 1 is LYCOPODIUM CASUARINOIDES Spring as
given in Hooker, Ic. Pl. X, t. 968, 1854.
Specimen No. 15 is PTERIS CRENATA Sw. according to Bed-
dome, Ferns of Southern India, page 12 and plate No. XXXV, 1863.
He quotes the following synonyms,- P. ensiformis Hook. Sp. Fil.
II. 163; P. multidentata Wall. n. 2681; P. caudata Loureiro Cochin.,
p. 835; P. heterodactyla Reinv. in Hook. Journ. of Bot. III p. 405.
No. 37 of my paper is EGENOLFIA APPENDICULATA J. Sm.
as given in the author's work, "Ferns British and Foriegn ", III,
1866. The synonym he quotes are, - Acrostichum appendiculatum Willd.
. in Hook. Exot. FI t. 108%; A. viviparium Sw.; Polybotrya viviparia
Hook. Exot. Fl. t. 107; Acrostichum setorum Wall.; A. hamiltoniana
Wall. Egenolfia hamiltoniana Schott. Gen. Fil. 34. Beddome
describes it under Polybotrya appendiculata J. Sm. on page 66, Ferns
of Southern India, 1863. His specimens, as shown in his drawings
on plate CXCIV, were much larger than mine, but there is close
similarity between the pinna he has shown in detail and the ones I
examined. Mine were probably immature plants. The fronds are
dimorphic. The pinnae on the fertile ones are more or less
contracted, sometimes showing a moniliform appearance; the venules
are distinct and the placentas amorphous.
Species No. 45 in my work is SALVINIA NATANS Hoffm.
Germ. II. I., described in "Cryptogames Vasculaires" of Flore
Generale de L' Indo-Chine, 1023. Tardieu-Blott et Christensen give
the synonyms as,-S. verticillata Roxb in Calc. Journ. t. 1820, 1845;
S. sprengeli in Corda Rhizos. t. 2; Marsilea natans L. Sp. 1562, 1753.

In addition, I have discovered four more ferns in this area, one
cultivated (Pteris wallichiana Ag) and three wild (Microsorium
punctatum Cop., Schizoloma ensifolia J. Sm. and Aspidium zollingeria-
num Baker). These are described below as well as the material I
collected has made it possible.
1. MICROSORIUM PUNCTATUM Cop. in Un. Calif. Publ.
Bot. XVI. III. 1929.
Acrostichum punctatum L. Sp. Ed. II. 152. 1763.
Polypodium punctatum Sw. in Schrad. Journ. 21. 1801.
P. irioides Pioret in Enc. V. 513. 1804.
Pleopeltis punctata Bedd. Ferns Brit. India Suppl. 22. 1867.
Microsorium irregulare Link Hort. Berol. II. 110. 1883.
Pleopeltis iriodes Moore Ind. LXXVII; in Bedd. Ferns of
Southern India, 59. 1863.
-
Rhizomes creeping, about 6 mm. in diameter, densely covered by
much-branched fibrous roots; ramenta peltate at the base, the
connectives very brief, the shields clathrate, 0.6 0.8 mm. long and
about 0.3 mm. wide, appressed, the appendages attenuate with straight
or curved tips, toothed, the cell walls brown, mostly thick, thinning at
the margins, the lumina more or less turbid. Fronds monomorphic,
simple, staggered about 8 mm. apart, the stipes short, about 8 mm.
long about 3 mm, in diameter, green, articulated to phyllopodia, the
laminae linear-lanceolate, about 43 cm. long and about 5.5 cm. wide,
attenuate towards both the base and the acute or sometimes rounded
apex, rarely dichotomizing, glabrous, the margins entire, narrowly
callose, the texture coriaceo-carnose, the mid-rib continuous with the
stipe, green, rounded on the dorsal side, flattened ventrally, the main
veins fine, indistinctly visible, traversed by veinlets larger than the others
with copious small areoles in between, the ultimate ones terminating in
or anastomosing below hydathodes; sori small, circular, irregularly
scattered in the upper half of the blade, sometimes so compact
as to appear confluent, compital, non-indusiate: sporangia with
stalks about 0.2 mm. long, the capsules sub-globose, about 0.2 mm. in
diameter, the annuli vertical, incomplete, 10 – 16 celled; spores 64,
reniform, about 0.05 mm. long and about 0.03 mm. in diameter,
yellow, filled with oil droplets, without perispore.

2. SCHIZOLOMA ENSIFOLIA J. Sm. in Journ. of Bot. III. 414.
Lindsaea ensifolia Sw. in Schrad. Journ. 77. 1801.
L. lanceolate Lab. No. Holl. Fl. Sp. II. 98. 1806.
Adiantum ensifolium Poir. Encycl. Suppl. I. 139. 1810.

Schizoloma billardicri Gaud. in Ann. Sc. Nat. III. 508. 1824.
Lindsaea pentaphylla Hook. Sp. I. 39. t. 67A. 1846.
Schizoloma pentaphylla Fee Gen. 108. 1850-52.
S. javae Fee Gen. 109. t. 29. f. I. 1850-52.
S. agatii Brack. Expl. Exp. XVI. 216. t. 30. f. I. 1854.
Lindsaya billardieri Carr. in Seem. Fl. Vit. 337. 1873.
Rhizomes creeping, ramenta linear, formed of 2-3 rows of small
narrow cells, the cell walls reddish or blackish, the lumina clear. Fronds
monomorphic, growing close to each other, the stipes canaliculate,
about 25 cm.. long, pliant, yellowish, sometimes brown at the base, the
laminae pinnate, about 10 cm. long and about 10 cm. wide, the lateral
pinnae similar to the terminal one, in 1-5 pairs, opposite or sub-opposite
about 2 cm. apart, forming an angle of about 50° with the rachis,
shortly petiolulate or sub-sessile, linear-lanceolate, 7-12 cm. long and
1.5-3.0 cm. wide, glabrous, the base sub-equal, the apex attenuate, the
texture firm, herbaceous, the veins dichotomising, loosely reticulate;
sori confluent along the margins, indusiate; spores tetrahedral, smooth,
yellow.
I found this fern in Golden Valley in late November 1954, but it
had been cleared when I went to collect some more specimens during
the following rainy season. It grows on shaded, water-soaked, embank-
ments.

Lithobrochia wallichiana Fee Gen. 135. 1850-52.
Campteria wallichiana Mogre Ind. 221. 1861.

This is a terrestial fern cultivated for its beauty. I found speci-
mens at the Globe Cinema premises. The manager kindly gave me a
small plant for study, but it is still not sufficiently matured to be
useful.
4. ASPIDIUM ZOLLINGERIANUM Baker Syn. Fil.; in Bedd.
Ferns Brit. India 251. 1866-68.
A. immersum Hook. So. Fil. IV. 58. 1846.
.
Phlebigonium impressum Griffs. Pl. Ind. n. 34; in Bedd. Ferns
Brit. India 251. 1866-68.
Aspidium an trifoliatum Sw. (Wall. M.S.); in Bedd. Ferns
Brit. India 251. 1866-68.
Rhizomes creeping, bearing ramenta. Fronds monomorphic, the
stipes stramineous, 15-30 cm. long, the laminae broadly ovate-
acuminate, 15-30 cm. long and 85-30 cm. wide at the base, pinnate, the
apical pinnae pinnatipartite, broad below, attcnuate above, the lateral
ones 2-3 pairs, linear-lanceolate, deeply serrate, the lowest pair again
pinnate, sometimes sub-opposite, the veins anastomosing into large.
costal areoles with smaller areoles and free veinlets at the margins, the
larger ones slightly pustulate on the upper surface; sori circular to
triangular, in rows corresponding with the centres of the large areoles,
terminal on the single included veinlet, indusiate, the indusium
subpeltate, orbicular, membranaceous.
Found wild in Golden Valley.
