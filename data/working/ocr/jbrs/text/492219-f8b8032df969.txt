SILURUS BURMANENSIS
A NEW SPECIES OF FISH FROM THE INLE LAKE,
SOUTHERN SHAN STATE, BURMA.
Ma Khin Thant*
Inle lake is a solution lake. It is
situated on the Shan Plateau at the height
of 2915 feet, above sea level in the lui.
20°35' North and long. 95°57' East.
It occupies the central part of a trough
between two ranges of hills. Several small
streams flow into the lake from the north,
from the west and the east, out of which
the most remarkable stream is the Heho
stream which originated from the old Heho
lake and flows from west.
Many streams run beneath the ground
for some miles before reaching the lake.
This is the nature of the streams of the
Shan States and this peculiarity of the
course of the streams has marked influence
on the distribution and adaptation of the
fresh water fauna. On the whole the lake is
a closed system with certain ecological
peculiarities of its own, consequently the
fauna of the lake is highly peculiar.
New species of Nemachilus and Barilius
were discovered by Dr. N. Annandale in
the year 1917 in the months of February
and March in this lake. The midget eel
Chaudhuria caudata. only a single genus
and species was also discovered by Dr. N.
Annandale in the same lake (Records of
the Indian Museum, 1918, vol. XIV, pp.
38-42). He had described Chaudhuria caudata
basing on 5 specimens, including the
holotype No. F 9402/1. Indian Museum.

Diagnosis. A medium size fish with a
pair of long maxillary barbels and two pairs
of short mandibular barbels. Dorsal rays 5,
pectoral rays I.1] (1.10-1.13), pelvic rays
8( 8-9), anal rays 73 (73-75), and caudal
rays 17(17-19).
Description.– Lower jaws exactly below
upper jaw with no open pores along margin
of lower jaw. Body moderately depressed
anteriorly but markedly compressed poste-
riorly. Length and depth of body 10.6 (9.0-
11.1)and 1.7 (1.5-2.1)respectively. Pectoral
spine stout with serrated lower, inner edge
and about half as long as pectoral rays. Anal
fin inserted just behind ventral. Head mode.
rately depressed, length 1.4 (1.3-1.5) and
width 1.6 (1.6-2.1) Snout in dorsal outline
elongated and rounded, 0.6 (0.5-0.6) in
head. A very small, narrow, projecting
dermal flap near inner margin of maxillary
barbels. Eye 0.2 (0.2-0.3) in bony
interorbital space, 1.2 (1.2- 1.7) in head.
Maxillary barbels dusky-grey, mandibulars
pale white; former 2 times latter.
Colouration. Above dorsal line.greenish
grey and below dorsal line, leaden, light
greenish grey. Some grey spots scattered
above anal fin. Caudal fin rounded and
not very large.

Relationship. Silurus species belonging
to India, Burma and Ceylon are namely
wynaandensis, afghana and cochinchinensis
(Francis Day, 1878).
The new species Silurus burmanensis
evidently is very closely related to Silurus
wynaandensis, because it has 6 barbels, 1.10
pectoral rays, 8 pelvic rays and 19 caudal
rays. The possession of 6 barbels shows
close affinity between the two species,
because barbels count being constant
character and not a variable character like
other counts.
However, burmanensis differs wynaan-
densis in having 5 dorsal rays instead of
2, and 73-75 anal rays, instead of 58-62.
The difference in counts of dorsal and anal
rays suggest a departure from wynaandensis
because these characters are of specific
taxonomic level.
Silurus afghana and Silurus cochin-
chinensis differ from burmanensis in having
4/4 barbels, 2/4 dorsal rays, and 10/10 pelvic
rays, instead of 6,5 and 8-9 respectively.
The lower counts of barbels and dorsal rays
show an early departure of afghana-
cochinchinensis stock from wynaadensis-
burmanensis stock. However, the ranges of
anal, caudal and pectoral rays counts of
burmanensis overlapped with that of afghana
whereas, only pectoral and caudal rays
counts overlapped with cochinchinensis.
