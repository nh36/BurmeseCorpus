REVIEWS OF BOOKS
Burmese Syntax
By
U Pe Mg Tin, M.A., B.Litt., (I.E.S. Retired),
Emeritus Professor of Pali,
University of Rangoon.
It is a commonplace that literature has its roots in
life. Literature therefore either reflects contemporary
life or revolts against existing conditions. In either event
it gives us certain aspects or features of the life of the
times.
Since the regained independence of Burma there
has been a revival of literary activity. This activity has
been canalised in two directions. On the one hand,
there is the Burma Translation Society which aims at the
dissemination of modern knowledge among the masses.
On the other hand there is the Department of Translation
and Publications in Rangoon University which attempts
to satisfy the needs of the more educated classes. These
two efforts at the extension of the frontiers of knowledge
among the masses and the intelligentia can be seen to
converge in the circumstances leading to the publication
of Professor U.Pe Mg Tin's Burmese Syntax.
The Burmese Syntax has been written by Professor
U Pe Mg Tin as a representative of the Rangoon Univer-
sity Department of Translation and Publications, and the
Burmese Translation Society has undertaken its publica-
tion.
There is a long-standing complaint by foreigners
regarding the absence of any scientific study of the Bur-
mese Language. Of course, there are those who champ-
ion the direct method of teaching a language by conver-

sation and pictures without rules. Justification for this
method is sought in a child's ability to speak his mother
tongue without any rules. This argument though plausible
is fallacious. In the first place, the child's experience is
limited and his vocabulary must needs be small. As he
grows his experience widens and his vocabulary enlarges
correspondingly. He then starts word-building to ex-
press himself. In that process his constant contact with
those who can use the home language correctly helps
him to express himself according to accepted standards.
He thus unconsciously imbibes and applies rules regar-
ding word-buliding.
Word-building is a first step in syntax which in the
ultimate analysis deals with the due arrangement of words
in a sentence. Professor Pe Mg Tin has in his book shown
how a little thinking would make syntax easier and how
a little knowledge of syntax would make thinking easier.
There can be no sentence without syntax. Words
have to be arranged to make complete sense, and their
meanings and relations to one another have to be con-
sidered. This is what Professor Pe Mg Tin has sought
to do in his book. The classification he has adopted is
a mere device to introduce order into the study of
Burmese sentence- structure. But language being an
ever-growing human process cannot be classified with
mathematical precision. Professor Pe Mg Tin's method
though not the last word makes a constant appeal to
common sense without taxing the memory with too many
details. His book is a pioneer effort at a systematic
study of the Burmese Language, and it will remain a
milestone in such study.
M. B. H.
