2. 1 THREE DIFFERENT DICTIONA-
RIES
The following description and com-
parison of different alphabetical orders is
based primarily on three dictionaries:
U Wun's Burmese Burmese dictionary,
Judson's Burmese-English dictionary, (1893

edition), and Cornyn and Musgrave's
Burmese-English glossary. All three are
well known and widely used, and con-
veniently provide extreme examples of
different approaches to the problem. The
ordering systems in U Wun and Judson
can be seen almost as opposites, while
Cornyn's system combines features of
both. Most other dictionaries, encyclo-
paedias, word-lists, spelling-books, etc.
follow one or other of these three fairly
closely. Before looking at the overall
systems in the three it will be useful to
deal with a number of minor points of
variation between them. These mainly
concern matters on which the traditional
list is deficient and gives no guidance.
2,2
MINOR VARIATIONS
The traditional list of 12 'vowels' co-
vers little more than half the possibilities
used in the full writing system, and so
leaves considerable room for variation in
devising alphabetical orders.
Each vowel may be written in one
of three tones, which are referred to here
by Stewart's terms: creaky, level, and
heavy ( = အောက်မြစ်သံ၊ သံလွင်၊ ရွှေဆီးသံ). These
terms refer to their pronunciation, as the
method of marking tone in the script is
not uniform for all the vowels. It is in
fact not possible to write a vowel without
indicating its tone, because the script is
so arranged that the tone in which a
vowel is to be pronounced is clear even
when there is no overt tone-mark. How-
ever, it is convenient to be able to refer
to the vowels without specifying the tone,
and for this purpose I use the unmarked
forms, i. e. those with tone-mark.
