5. The only apparatus necessary for the ancient
game was the dice and a board or a prepared flat surface.
There was no dice box such as is used in modern dice throwing.
The aksavapana, which is mentioned in Sanskrit works,
appears to have been a container or carrier for the dice
and is described as being provided with a cord (for
suspension?). In the Vidhura Jataka the dice are evidently
thrown from the hand, and their size and shape are not
suitable for throwing from a dice-box.

8. The number of pasaka thrown and the counting.
The Jatakas do not expressly mention the number of dice
(pasaka) used in playing the game. The frequently recurring
expression pasake, or pase, khipati, in the plural number, and
the description of the game in the Vidhura Jataka shew
clearly, however, that several dice were thrown together
in each throw. In the introduction to the first dice-oracle
in the Bower Ms., Luders says; pasakas are spoken of in
the plural: and, as the various throws thereafter specially
mentioned consist in every case of three numbers, it may
safely be presumed that three pasakas were used in each
throw. In the throws there mentioned, however, the order
of the numbers in the reading of the throws was impor-
tant eg. the throws 421, 214, 142, 241 and 412 are
distinguished from one another. So there must have been
some means by which the dice could be recognised as
first, second or third. Lüders thinks that the explanation
is to be found in a phrase, which he quotes from the Bower
Ms. (Bl. Ib. 23,) kumbhakārimātangayukta patantu, which

9. The pasaka game. The description in the
Vidhura Jataka shews how the game with dice of the pasaka
type was played. Each of the players named the throw on
which he wagered. The player, whose turn it was to throw,
then took the dice in his hand, rolled them round over
one another in his hand, and then threw them up into the air.
If they appeared to be falling unfavourably for him, he had
the right to catch them while still in the air and to repeat
the throw. The player's skill (which is mentioned) con-
sisted, according to the narrator in the ability to perceive
in a moment whether the dice were falling right or not.
Rājājūtasippamhi sukusalataya pasake attano parajayaya
bhassante natva. (Jataka VI. 281. Fausboll. Cf. Vidhura
Jataka Wutthu p. 39.)
NOTE. In a very ancient form of dice playing in

into the air by a player and he had to try to catch them.
on the back of his hand or to perform other feats with them.
Dice-playing in this form was, of course, a game of skill.
