FURTHER ARAKAN FAMILY ARCHIVES.
A selection of Arakan papers appeared in this Journal last
August. Another interesting series of documents are those in the
possession of U San Shwe Bu of Akyab, a former member of the
Legislative Council and a contributor to this Journal.
His great-
grandfather U Shin accompanied Mr. Robertson, Agent for the
Governor-General in Eastern Bengal and Arakan, in the invasion
of Arakan in 1825, and was rewarded with a headmanship, as were
the next two generations, U Mra Phaw Rhi and U Kyaw Zaw Pru.
Their descendant says they were fierce men, who usually got what
they wanted from Government. The series ends with his father's
efforts to be made a Township Officer.
Five
There are altogether thirty-one documents in the series.
of these are written in Burmese, and the rest are in English. All
are originals. With the exception of the earliest one, they are in
excellent condition, though their owner tells me they have several
times been in burning houses. Ten of the latest dated documents
are omitted, six are prior to 1900, and four are later.
I
The 'parawana' appointing U Shin to be kyun-ok or headman
of Angarat Kyaing island, now called the Inner Boronga, to the south
of Akyab. At the top is the seal of the East India Company, with
inscription in Persian and Urdu. To the right is the signature of
T. C. Robertson, B.C.S. (Bengal Civil Service). The duties of
U Shin are enumerated. He is to keep the peace and collect
revenues. The Burmese spelling of 'Robertson', and the expression
'ashin Konpani' will be noticed, and the word 'that' after 'Robertson'
which appears as 'thet' in other documents. It is the Burmese
form of 'Sahib'. There are some spelling mistakes. The date is
July 1825. Below is a copy of the order in Persian.
II
To
To the left of the Company's seal in Persian and Burmese is
the note that the Myothugyi, Musa Ali, has seen the order.
the right is a signature, probably of the Commissioner, or his
initials. Possibly they are those of H. Hopkinson, an early Com-
missioner, after whom an Akyab road is named. The document
states that the revenues of Angarat Kyaing were only Rs. 2,000,
which was insufficient to maintain U Shin and his family. He had
therefore applied on the 7th of June 1845 for transfer to Pra Kyun,
a neighbouring village-tract. He is appointed kyun-ok of Pra

III
To the left of the Company's seal is a note that the document
was copied by interpreter Maung Bein.
To the right is the signature of a famous Commissioner, who
was later Colonel Sir Archibald Bogle. He fought against the
Burmese in Assam in 1824, and at Martaban and Rangoon in 1852,
being wounded in the attack on the Shwe Dagon He was also
Commissioner of Tenasserim. The modern lay-out of Akyab is
largely to his credit, and Bogle Road commemorates him. At one
end of it is an old tree, on which is a stone inscription that it was
planted in 1831 by Bogle and his wife.
The document states that U Shin found that there were only
one hundred doons of cultivated land on his island, and that the
revenue only rose to Rs. 2,000. He was therefore appointed to
Pra Kyun. The writer is given as clerk La Pru.

To the left is the note that temporary Myothugyi Sanda has
seen the order. This states that Mra Phaw Thu's parents had come
into Arakan with Robertson, and his father had become a kyun-ok.

for Pra Kyun. Ambari, too, is in Akyab town. The previous
kyonook had been deposed for letting prisoners escape, though this
