A NOTE ON ELEMENTARY BURMESE ASTROLOGY
In his article on Elementary Burmese Astrology in the August number
of this Journal, U Ba Sein attaches great importance to the conjunction of
the Moon and Venus at the time of the Pegu earthquake. He describes the
stress of the Venusian tide on top of the more considerable lunar tide as
the last straw; a very apt phrase, but I fear he has left the solar tide quite
out of account.
In H. A. Marmer's book "The Tide", with which U Ba Sein is I
suspect familiar, the stress of the solar tide is stated to be about four ninths
that of the lunar. Evidently the last straw is imposed upon the patient
earth when Venus, the Sun and the Moon are concurrently in conjunction,
or, for it is indeed the same stress, when any two of them are in conjunction
and the third is in opposition. The conjunction of the Sun and Moon is of
more significance than the conjunction of the Moon and Venus.
According to H. L. Chhibber in "The Physiography of Burma" the
Pegu earthquake occurred at 8.18 P.M. on May, 5th., 1930. It is not
difficult to reckon back by the Moon's synodic periods and the calculation
clearly shows that at the time of the earthquake the age of the moon was
eight days; that is to say that the Moon was in quadrature. So far from
adding to the lunar tide, the solar tide diminished it at this time by four
ninths of its value and the last straw was added to a much lightened burden.
It would seem that the astronomical phenomena of the epoch of this earth-
quake do not admit of the conjunction of Venus and the Moon being regar-
ded as a decisive factor.
F. GEORGE
