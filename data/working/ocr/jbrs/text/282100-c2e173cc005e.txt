ARCHIVES OF AN ARAKAN FAMILY.
I recently had the pleasure of looking through a set of twenty documents
in the possession of U Po Yin of Maungdaw in Akyab district, relating to his
family for a period of over a hundred years. Documents even a hundred years old
are rare in Burma cutside the Record-rooms, and family papers especially so The
present series are in excellent condition, considering the damp climate of Maungdaw.
Of those reproduce here, Nos 2,5 6,7 and 13, are copies ( n several cases nearly
as old as the originals), and the other seven are originals. There are a futher
seven which I did not consider of sufficient general interest to reproduce, a list of
them being given at the end,
The first of the family to be mentioned is Maung Nyo. His father had
obtained a grant of land from the Government of Bengal soon after his flight from
Arakan, at the time of the Burmese conquest in 1781. The grant was on Shahpuri
Island which lies across the Naaf nearly opposite to Maungdaw. Maung Nyo went
through the Arakan campaign of 1821-1825 with Mr T. C. Robertson, civil officer
with the expedition, who was one of the two British signatories of the treaty of
Yandabo in the following year Maung Nyo received a grant of land in Arakan
for his services and was made a headman (kyun-ck) of Maungdaw. His son Aung
Pru and grandson Aung Kyaw Zan were grantees and headmen after him. One of
Aung Pru's sisters was the grand-mother of the late headman of Maungdaw, father
of U Po Yin. The family has thus maintained a proud tradition of one hundred
and twelve year's public service in Bengal and Arakan.
My Dear Paton,
I
Camp Yandabo, 26th February 1826.
Moungneo Chowdree being about to return via Aeng to Arracan
I take the opportunity of recommending him to your particular con-
sideration and at the same time requesting that he may not suffer for
the alleged misdemeanour of his kinsman Chigh Po but in the event of
his agreeing to your terms and conditions to be placed in possession of
the whole or a part of the Chota Anuk division whence you write that
you have been obliged to eject the others. Maungneo is really one of
the best men of his tribe and has been of such essential service to me
in this quarter that I should be sincerely grieved were he to suffer from
the remissness of Chigh Po during his absence. I should feel obliged by
your letting me know what arrangements you may conclude regarding
the Chota Anuk division, directing your letter to the Care of Messrs.
Colvin and Co., Calcutta whither I shall immediately proceed. I
consider this as a demi-official letter entirely, so conclude.
Your most truly,
T. C. ROBERTSON.
C. PATON Esq.
Sub Commissioner.

TRANSLATION.
This certificate is hereby granted to Aung Proo, son of Mg. Nyo
the late Keouk, resident of Tatkhyaung village of Naaf Circle, for the
appointment of Keouk of Naaf Ali Circle (which is one third portion of
the said Naaf) on a Revenue of Rupees 7,193-5-5 as supplied by him.
His character and qualifications appear to be good and intelligence.
Also great assistance of his father the aforesaid Maung Nyo during his
time in regard to the affairs of the said circle. He received a certificate
of Ameen from the Revenue Surveyor. His father the late Maung Nyo
did serve as a subedar without pay in Arakan Local Battalion since
the year 1823 when the English fought a war with the Burmese in
Chummaproo [Shahpuri] and in the year 1187 M. S. as Keouk of the
said Naaf on Revenue of Rs 700. He received certificates from the
Collector and General four in number for his utmost exertions in the
said war with Captain Robertson the Agent of the Governor General of
India. Beside this he did serve as Myothugyi and Taramathugyi of
Akyab. Moreover he called the Chittagonians and established villages
in the said Naaf Circle for the purpose of increasing the Government
Revenue and therefore the Revenue of the said circle has become more
than twenty thousand rupees. You are hereby nominated and appointed
as Keouk of the said Ali Naaf Circle. [Boundaries and revenue details
omitted]. Given under my hand and seal this 17th. September 1860 or
3rd waxing moon of Thadingyut 1223 M. S.
G. FAITFUL, CAPTAIN.
Deputy Commissioner

Chariphoo Chowdry has asked me to certify what I know of him.

the way of doing business connected with (?) Mocuddumas. He can

old Chariphoo prospers and that he may live to see his chicks grown up
in his footsteps.
Chittagong

XIII.
Calcutta, 28th February 1884.
The humble petition of Aung Kyaw Zan,
Chowdhury, Father Wonfroo Chowdury, District
Chittagong, Circle Teknaaf, Thana Teknaaf.
Most respectfully showeth:-
The petitioner supplicates the favour of your glancing through the
petition and to pay heed to his cries and have mercy on his deplorable-
ness. That the petitioner's great grandfather was a near relation to
the king of Arakan who on the subjugation of Arakan by the king of
Burma not being willing to submit to Burmese rule and oppression,
fled to India in the year 1146 B.C. [sic] and applied for a grant of land
with 500 of his relatives from the creek of Ramoo to the south [i.e.
southwards to] Thapoored [shahpuri], and obtained a free grant for the
same.
That his son Maung Nyo Chowdury succeeded him while he and
his followers were engaged in clearing the immense jungle that covered
the aforesaid land, a war broke out between the English and the
Burmese Government in consequence of the king of Burma having
illigally taken possession of Thapoored which was and still is in the
possession of the petitioner. That on the said outbreak, General [sic]
Robertson called on petitioner's grandfather to supply new provisions
boats etc, as he being then the owner of Thapoored Island, which
petitioner's grandfather did and followed the Battalion with General
Robertson from 1186 B.C. to close of the war without pay, and did the
British Government every faithful services, for which certificates are
herewith annexed for your perusal.
[Request for reduction of revenue, omitted].
Your most obedient servant,
AUNG KYAW ZAN, CHOWDURY.
