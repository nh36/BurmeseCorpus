STANDARDS FOR NGAPI'
by
U Chit Thoung 2
From time immemorial the two staple foodstuffs of the Burmese
and the Siamese have been rice and Ngapi. The Siamese equivalent
of "Ngapi" is "Kapi". Our knowledge of rice from the time it is
cultivated right up to the time it is served at our table is complete.
The same cannot be said of Ngapi, Though Ngapi is consumed
throughout the length and breadth of the Union of Burma, standards
for genuine and wholesome Ngapi have not been fixed up to now.
Therefore it is extremely desirable now to make a serious attempt to
fix standards for Ngapi with the object of safeguarding the health of
people who consume it.
The word Ngapi" literally means "Pressed Fish." In reality
Ngapi is salted fish or shrimps which have been partially dried in the
sun for about three or four days and then stored in earthern jars for
about three months to allow it to mature. In the manufacture of Ngapi
common salt is added as a preservative. There are several kinds of
Ngapi. They are Seinsa Ngapi, Hmyin Ngapi and Nga Ngapi. Seinsa
Ngapi is manufactured mainly from shrimps and small fishes. Hmyin
Ngapi is prepared exclusively from a special kind of shrimps which
occur in great abundance near the sea in Bassein District. It is a
delicious foodstuff which compares very favourably with Anchovy
paste manufactured in some European countries. Nga Ngapi comprises
Ngapigaung, Yegyo Ngapi and Damin Ngapi. The word "gaung"
literally means one whole body." "Ngapigaung" consists of salted
whole-fish of fairly big size. A particular kind of Ngapigaung is
named after the fish from which it has been prepared, e.g., Ngakhu
Ngapigaung, Ngamyin Ngapigaung, Nagyin Ngapigaung, etc. Yegyo
Ngapi consists of more than one kind of fish of medium size. Damin
Ngapi contains many kinds of comparatively small fish.

Ngapi is rich both in calcium and phosphorus, the two indispensable
elements for the formation of bones. A deficiency of these elements in
our diet causes rickets. The daily phosphorus requirement of an adult
is about 1.0 gram. The extent to which calcium and phosphorus are
absorbed from our food depends not only on the amount of these ele-
ments present in the food but also on the ratio between the amounts of
these two elements. That is why all scientific discussions on rickets to-day
lay such stress on what is known as the calcium phosphorus ratio of
the diet (Ca/P ratio). Adults assimilate these two elements best from
the food consumed if the calcium phosphorus ratio in food is 0.5 to
0.7. It has been found that the calcium phosphorus ratio in all kinds
of Ngapi is approximately 2. As this is not an optimal ratio, adults
would not be able to assimilate these two elements from Ngapi to the
fullest extent unless the
unless the ratio between the amounts of these
two elements is rectified by consuming Ngapi together with other
foodstuffs which contain more phosphorus than calcium. Fortunately
for us rice which is always consumed with Ngapi is rich in phosphorus
and poor in calcium. Ordinary milled rice which we consume daily
contains about 0.15% of phosphorus and 0.01% of calcium. Therefore
by eating rice and Ngapi together the phosphorus and calcium which
occur in them become highly available.
As Ngapi is a very important source of amino acids, phosphorus
and calcium which are indispensable for maintaing ourselves in good
health, it is extremely desirable now to prevent the adulteration of
Ngapi by fixing standards for genuine Ngapi. The adulteration of
Seinsa Ngapi, Hmyin Ngapi and Damin Ngapi which are in the form
of paste is very common in all the markets in the big towns of the
Union of Burma. It is therefore extremely desirable to fix standards
by means of which it is possible to detect whether a sample of Ngapi
is genuine or adulterated. For this purpose it is necessary to determine
the percentages of water, sodium chloride, fat and protein respectively
in every sample examined. Let us suppose that on analysing a sample
of Ngapi the following results have been obtained.

If the percentages of protein and fat are expressed on dry and
sodium chloride-free basis, they become 62.50 % and 7.50 % respectively.
As a result of the analyses of several hundred samples of genuine
Ngapi from the manufacturing centres in the neighbourhood of Mergui,
Tavoy, Pyapon and Bassein it has been found that all genuine and
wholesome samples of Ngapi contain not less than 60% of protein and
5% of fat if these percentages are expressed on dry and sodium
chloride-free basis. Therefore, on analysing a sample of Ngapi as
explained above, if the percentages of protein and fat are less than
60% and 5% respectively on dry and sodium chloride-free basis, it is
safe to presume that the sample of Ngpai which has been examined is
not genuine.
It is possible to improve the quality and food value of Ngapi by
reducing the percentage of water as well as sodium chloride. More
than 90% of the samples of Ngapi sold in the markets contain well
over 40 % of water and 30% of sodium chloride. The storage life of
Ngapi could be prolonged considerably if the percentage of water is
not more than 40%. Besides, it is not uncommon to come across
samples of Ngapi containing as much as 35% of sodium chloride. Too
much sodium chloride spoils the taste of Ngapi. As a preservature
10% to 20% of sodium chloride is quite sufficient. There are two other
constituents of Ngapi which we should consider. They are sand and
silica. Any sample of Ngapi always contains minute traces of sand
and silica which have been blown into it by wind during its
manufacture in the open air. As a result of analyses of several hundred
samples, it has been found that a sample of Ngapi containing more
than 0.8% of sand and silica is definitey gritty and almost inedible.
Therefore a good sample of Ngapi should contain less than 0.8% of
sand and silica.
In consideration of the results of the investigations which have
been described above, I would propose the following standards for
genuine and good Ngapi.
1.
A genuine sample of Ngapi should contain not more than
40% of water and 20 % of sodium chloride.
2. A genuine sample of Ngapi should contain not less than
60% of protein and 5% of fat on dry and sodium
chloride-free basis.
