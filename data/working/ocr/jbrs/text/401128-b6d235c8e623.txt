BOOK REVIEW
Thakin Kodaw Hmaing Tika (U Lun: Man and Poet) by
ZAWGYI (Shumawa Press, Rangoon)
The prewar practice of reviewing Burmese books in the Journal of
the Burma Research Society should be revived but it should not be
confined to Burmese Novels but should embrace other departments
also such as biography. Burmese works on biography are just as
novel now to Burmese readers as Burmese novels were fifty years ago.
While Burmans are learning to write biographies of all sorts, we would
like to draw attention to U Lum: Man and Poet by Zawgyi as a
welcome addition to Burmese biographical literature. It describes the
life of a contemporary man of letters as revealed in his writings-the
first of its kind in Burmese. A more popular subject could hardly
have been chosen, for U Lun is none other than Thakin Kodaw
Hmaing, the Stalin Prize winner. When King Thibaw was being taken
captive by the British in 1885 young Maung Lun, then ten years of age,
went to the Pagoda and gave vent to his feelings in this Prayer: "I and
my people have now become slaves. May I never be born a slave in
my future births, Lord". And he played his part in having the prayer
fulfilled during his life-time. He did not take up the sword of open
rebellion but chose the pen as the subtler and mightier weapon in his
fight for political freedom. He has used the pen effectively, a single
outburst of his quiet sarcasm being often more telling than the vitupera-
tions of hot headed extremists. He has employed both verse and
prose in the service of freedom and his ganthis and tikas are famous.
For his contributions to political literature and his activities in the
course of independence he is popularly known as the Grand Old Man
of Burmese literature and politics.
The book under review should be read not only because of its
subject but also because of its style. Zawgyi writes like an angel and
has produced a delightful book. He has shown us how to write
simple and charming Burmese prose.
P. M. T.
