If someone were to ask a historian
in the next century what was the most
significant scientific research in the 1960-
70 decade, 1 think he or she would reply
that it was space research. Here I am
assuming that men are still living on this
earth in the next century and that the
advances in atomic science of the 1940's
and 50's which produced the atomic and
hydrogen bombs had not wiped out civili-
sation in the years to come.
But let us not spend our time this
evening discussing further these dismal
possibilities. Instead let us consider the
happier events in scientific research of the
past few years.
The first time in the history of
mortals, men have set foot on the Moon.
I think most of you are already familiar
with the scientific research and develop-
ment which made possible the flights to
the Moon. Many different scientific dis-
ciplines had to be combined together with
immense effort and superb coordination to
produce the various instruments, rockets
and communications equipment necessary
to successfully complete a mission to the
Moon. The outstanding feature of space
research and other modern scientific
research is the multidisciplinary nature of
this research. But more than the scientific
achievements, I think that the importance
of the Moon Mission may be said to be
the achievement of unification of the
people. This was very apparent during
the Apollo 13 moon flight during which,
due to a malfunction, the a stronauts could
not land on the moon. However they

after the combined prayers and good
wishes of the millions of people who
followed the whole flight by television and
radio.
Some may question the usefulness of
spending billions of dollars on the moon
missions but from the USA or USSR
governments' viewpoints the money was
well spent because these missions have
unified their people through the identifi-
cation of their hopes for world supremacy
and singleness of purpose in carrying out
their missi .ns. The space research pro-
grammes have captured the imagination of
the people, not only in the USA and
USSR but in many other countries as well.
In addition to the direct benefits of
space research such as the encouragement
of progress in rocket fuels, the develop-
ment of all kinds of instruments and
equipment and of advances in basic
sciences such as physics, chemistry and
metallurgy, there have been many other
benefits commonly referred to as the
“spin-off from the space programme."
One of the outstanding developments has
been the introduction of the Intelsat
system whereby television, telegraph and
telephone signals from one part of the
world can be relayed to other parts by
means of artificial satellites placed in orbit
over the Atlantic, Pacific and Indian
Oceans. Better communications between
different parts of the world are to be
welcomed as they can lead to more trade
and commerce and also hopefully, to

One of the interesting related resear-

space programme is that of thermoelectri-
city. The possibility of converting heat

long time due to the economic advantages

been discovered by Faraday. However
from a scientific point of view, the genera-
tion of electricity by the conventional
method is a roundabout way of converting
heat into electricity. Whereas in a ther-
moelectric generator, heat applied to a
thermopile (ie. a device containing n and p
type semiconductors) directly causes electri-
city to flow between the hot and cold
junctions of the two thermoelements.
Though the Seebeck Effect was used by
scientists for some experiments it was
only in the 1930's that Academician A.F.
Ioffe of USSR and his team of researchers
investigated the phenomena of thermoelec-
tricity and deduced a theory for it and
methods of calculation. By about 1960 a
number of thermoelectric devices had been

The research on these units costs
about 30 million dolars which indicates
the complexity of the design of a ther-
moelectric generator even though the
unit itself seems outwardly simple. The
basic reason for spending so much on
these units is that they are much more
reliable than other electrical generators
and their power to weight ratio is very
much higher than batteries e.g a radio-
isotope thermoelectric generator can pro-
duce 10,000 watt-hours per kg weight
whereas a storage battery can produce
only 300 Wh/kg and an electrochemical
cell can produce only 200 Wh/kg. Also
their useful life is much longer.
Closely related to the semiconductors
used in thermoelectric generators are the
semiconductors used in solar cells which
generate electricity from sunlight. They
are used in many man made satellites
to provide electricity from solar energy
to operate the radio transmitters and
receivers in the orbiting satellite. They
are the lightest of all electric generators
from the point of view of longlasting
ability to produce electricity though they
are expensive. That is why we cannot
use them for ordinary electrical genera-
tion. The cheapest electrical generators
are the hydroelectric generators. Some
of the large hydroelectric stations in the
world produce electricity at a cost of
only half a pya per unit (KWH). In
the final analysis these hydroelectric
stations derive their power from solar
energy and gravitation because the water-
falls on which they depend could not
exist without the sun which evaporates
the water from the oceans; this water
vapour later falls as rain and flows along
a river till it comes to a cliff and falls
down due to the earth's gravitation. If
we analyse further we see that solar
energy is derived from nuclear fusion
reactions in the sun. So when we use
solar energy we are really using nuclear
energy in the ultimate analysis.
In the past few years there has
been an increased interest in the utilisa-
tion of solar energy because it is pollu-
tion-free. It is even better as a pollu-
tion free source of power than electri-
city because some forms of electricity
generation and utilisation give rise to
heat-pollution and carbon dioxide pollu
tion of the environment.
The latest research in which the
United Nations and we are interested is
development of solar refrigeration.
I think all of you will be intrigued.
by the possibility of using the heat of
the sun to overcome this same heat
which is causing us discomfort by making
it work an air conditioner to cool our
room. In theory this is quite feasible
but much research and development will
be necessary before it becomes techni-
cally and economically feasible. Much
nearer possibility is to develop a solar
ice maker and I have initiated some
work on such a device. This device, if
economically feasible, could be of great
benefit to our country and to all deve-
loping countries where electricity is at
present too expensive,
Solar Energy is really nuclear energy
received through solar radiation from a
distance of 93 million miles. Why?
Because the heat and light energy of
the sun is derived from thermonuclear

nuclei. Though nature has been using
this vast source of energy for millions
and may be even billions of years, scientists
are still trying to find a practical way
of harnessing this energy for peaceful
uses.

I have already mentioned in connection

one of its striking features was the mul-

same feature may be noticed in petro-
chemical research which has been one of
the recent areas of chemical interest.
Petrochemicals is that branch of science
or industry which deals with the many
different products that can be made from
petroleum or the derivatives of petroleum.
In this area of research, all the big research
labs employ thousands of post graduates
and doctorates to attack the difficult pro-
blems of production of new compounds.
These researchers belong to many different
scientific disciplines such as organic che-
mistry, physical chemistry, physics, me-
tallurgy and engineering because one type
of chemist alone could not successfully
complete the task of producing a new
compoud on an industrial scale. Many of
these new compounds are of potential
interest to us in Burma. For example
starting from petroleum and its refinery
products as our raw materials there is the
possibility of making plastics such as
polyethylene, polypropylene, polyvinyl-
chloride (PVC), the synthetic fibres popu-
larly known as tetoron, nylon, vinylon,
the fertilisers such as urea and ammonium
sulphate, the resins and adhesives such as
urea formaldehyde, phenol formaldehyde
and melamine formaldehyde, and other
products such as insecticides, synthetic
rubber and so on. In considering these
possibilities we must also take into account
the natural and synthetic products which
are similar to the above items such as
rayon and polynosic fibres (made from
cellulose raw materials) natural glues, and
natural rubber which are also polymers.
In fact, the term polymer is a general
term which includes both the petrochemi-
cal synthetic products and also the cellu-
losic and the naturally occuring products.
Polymer means all those organic compounds
which contain long chains of molecules.

monomer compounds and the polymer

Dr Mendelssohn's theory is based
