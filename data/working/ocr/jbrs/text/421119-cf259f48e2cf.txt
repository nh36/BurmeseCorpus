OBITUARY
Sithu U Kaung, late Director of Education
A great loss to the cause of education in Burma, to Burmese
intellectual life, and to the Burma Research Society was caused by the
death of U Kaung, Director of Education and Vice-President of the
Burma Research Society, on 19 February 1957. U Kaung was born in
1904, and educated in Burma and in England. He entered the Education
Service of the Government in 1930, and very rapidly rose to be an
Inspector of Schools. When the Asian War came to Burma he was
serving as Chief Education Officer, Shan States. In 1951 he headed the
Directorate of Education. In February 1957, while engaged in official
work in Calcutta, U Kaung was killed in a motor accident. He is
survived by Mrs. U Kaung, a son and two daughters.
U Kaung was one of the most widely experienced educators that
Burma had ever had. He has left his imprint on many aspects of
educational and intellectual activity in Burma. As secretary of the
University Re-organisation Committee in 1946, he brought out a com-
prehensive Report. As Senior Education Advisor in charge of Burmese
State Scholars in the United Kingdom from 1946 to 1949, he guided
and assisted many promising young Burmans to lay the foundations of
their careers. He was also at that time on the Burma Currency Board,
and the Burmese currency notes printed and issued at that time bore
his signature, so that these currency notes were generally called Maung
Kaung by the populace. U Kaung led or served on various official
delegations abroad, such as the Burma Delegations to Unesco in 1948
and 1949. He led the Burma Educational Mission to the U.K., U.S.A.,
Canada and Mexico in 1951-52. He was Director of the Unesco Seminar
for Youth Movements held in Tokyo in 1953. He was one of the most
travelled Burmans. He served as Chairman of the Burma Historical
Commission from 1955 till the time of his death. U Kaung had a
variety of intellectual and cultural interests, and was always ready to
assist and encourage literary, musical and artistic pursuits. He was
deeply interested in painting and sculpture.
The Burma Research Society had always benefited from his
inspiration and advice, and his death has been indeed an irreparable
loss to the Society and to the country.
