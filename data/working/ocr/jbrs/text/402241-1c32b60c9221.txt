WAR No. 20
When the Burmese attacked Muang Saiyoke in Siamese territory. In the year of the
hare, Buddha Era 2206.
The cause of the war on this occasion is connected with the attack of the Haws on Ava
as narrated in War No. 19. Before the war with the Haws, the King of Ava appointed Min
Nandameik, his uncle, to be an Uparat, and go down, and govern Muang Mawtama and the
Mon provinces to the south of Mawtama. When the Haws attacked Ava, the King ordered
Min Nandameik to levy a Mon Force and come up for the defence of the capital. The Mons
were unwilling to assist the Burmans, and the greater number of them ran away. Min
Nandameik was able to bring up to Ava only a Force of 3000 Mon soldiers. It appears that
the King of Ava censured Min Nandameik in some way and warned him for future
punishment. When the Haws' affair was over, Min Nandameik returned to Mawtama and
he caused the arrest of the Mons who ran away from being conscribed, with the intention of
punishing them according to martial law. Some of those who were arrested were put in
prison, and when a sufficient number had been obtained, they were all to be burnt alive.
The Mons became afraid, and a party of Mons about 5,000 strong united and rushed unto
Muang Mawtama and were able to arrest Min Nandameik and confine him. When the news
of the Mort rising at Mawtama became known to the Uparat in charge of Muang
Hongsawadi, he raised a force and came down to Mawtama. At the time not all the Mons in
the southern provinces became partisans of those who forcibly captured Muang Mawtama.
Those Mons who captured the town found that they were unable to resist the Burmese and
keep the town in their hands. They, therefore, migrated into Siam with their families, in all
about 10,000 persons. They also took with them Min Nandameik, the uncle of the King of
Ava, and proceeded by way of the Three Pagodas. Their intention was to ask leave of the
King of Siam to be subjects of Siam and to be allowed to stay in Ayut'ia. Somdet Phra
Narai, the King, became aware that a large number of Mons had migrated into Siam and
asked to be subjects of Siam, and he pitied them, and ordered the Mon nobles of the former
Mon immigrants to levy a force among the old Mort residents and go out and receive the
Mort families who had newly entered into Siam. The Samings, who were the leaders of the
party, were presented to the King of Siam. Min Nandameik did not live long, but became
ill and died.

in his possession most of the provincial towns of the Mons as his base, and even then he
was able to attack only Muang Hongsawadi. When he marched his army to attack Muang
Ava, he found that it was easier to march by way of Chiengmai. During the reign of
Somdet Phra Narai, only the Mons at Muang Mawtama revolted against the Burmese and
migrated into Slain and obtained protection from Siam, but the provincial towns of the
Mons still remained in Burmese hands, and did not become Siamese subjects as in the reign
of Somdet Phra Naresuon. On consideration of the facts mentioned above, it was
impossible for the Siamese army to go up to Muang Ava at that time. But the circums-
tances narrated in the royal history of Siam are very detailed and very positive, and it is
impossible that they had no foundation. In addition to this, the Mon history contains an
account of the war which is the same as that in the royal Siamese history in all particulars,
the only difference being that the Mon history says that the attack was against Muang
Pagan (Siamese, Phukam ) and not against Ava. Pagan lies below Muang Ava, and the
Siamese did not go up as far as Muang Ava. Therefore, I decide to follow the statements
made in the royal history of Siam and believe in what was stated in Mon history that the
Siamese went up and attacked Muang Pagan only.
The cause of the Siamese attacking Burma on this occasion was because the Burmese
had arbitrarily entered Siamese territory first, in pursuit of the Mon Families who had
migrated into Siam.

Alaungpaya dynasty came into power in Burma, when there was a great war between the

During the 95 years when there was no lighting between the Siamese and the Burmese,
there were times when both countries remained inactive, and sometimes there were times
when the two countries formed friendships. In both countries many changes took place. I
shall narrate the history of the two countries here shortly, to enable readers to understand
the circumstances when war took place subsequently. In this way they will understand the
causes and the effects of the war clearly.
I shall first of all speak about the events in Siam during the reign of Somdet Phra
Natal. During that reign, although no major wars took place as at the time of Somdet Phra
Naresuon, many important events occurred several times which would have deleterious
effects on Siam. If the King Somdet Phra Narai had not been wise, able and skillful in
diplomacy, it would have been difficult to govern the country efficiently. For this reason,
the Siamese as well as people of other countries in former times praised Somdet Phra Narai
as one of the great kings.
One of the events in the reign of Somdet Phra Narai was that when he assumed regal
power, he had had to fight his royal uncle in a civil war in the middle of the city itself.
Although he was able to defeat Phra Sisudhamraja, he still had many enemies at the
beginning of his reign, so that he suspected many people, even in the royal family. He had
to do away with many of his internal enemies, and to change many old government
servants and replace them with new ones. By the time he had suppressed his internal
enemies, fighting with the Burmese took place on 3 different occasions, as has been already
narrated. But in the three wars the ability and skillfulness of the new government servants
became evident. Many good men were obtained, for instance, Chow Phya Kosadhibadi,
Khun Lek, and Phra T'epraja, an old servant who was the son of the wet-nurse of the king.
Besides the above, there were others, for example, Phya Siharaj Tejochai, Phya Sunt'on
Songkhram and Phya Ram Tejo, etc., some of whom were Siamese, and some foreigners
who belonged to a different nationality and held different religious beliefs. Because the old
government servants were inefficient, he had to look for new employees to replace the old,
from the time when he fought against the Burmese. When the fighting with the Burmese
ended, there arose events in the history of Siam in which the Europeans were the cause
throughout the reign of Somdet Phra Narai.

The events in which the Siamese became involved with Europeans in the reign of
Somdet Phra Narai are recorded mainly in the writings of the Europeans. I will endeavour
to collect the facts and tell them intelligibly here, because the account is not given in the
royal history of Siam. But I am afraid that it will be impossible to write about it shortly. If
any reader find it wearisome, I would advise him to skip over this portion.
In the reign of Somdet Phra Narai, Siamese contact with foreigners and entering into
trade with foreign countries, reached the utmost limit of what they ever were at the time
when Ayut'ia was the capital of Siam. Other nationalities including the Chinese, Chams,
Indians, and Europeans, who came and resided in Siam were more numerous than at any
previous time. Of the Europeans who came and resided in Siam at that time, there were
four principal nationalities, namely the Portuguese, the Hollanders, the English and the
French. The Portuguese came into Siam in the reign of Somdet Phra Ramadhibadi the
second, in the year of the tiger, Buddha Era 2061. The Hollanders came in the reign of
Somdet Phra Naresuon about the year of the major dragon, Buddha Era 2141. The English
came in the reign of Somdet Phra Akat'otsarot about the year of the rat, Buddha Era 2155.
But the French came in the reign of Somdet Phra Narai about the year of the tiger, Buddha
Era 2205.
The subject of Europeans coming out to the eastern countries has already been
mentioned in narrating War No. 16. Here I shall mention about the French coming out to
the east, in continuation with what had been narrated previously.
There is an account which says that the French government knew that other European
nations trading with eastern countries profited much and amassed large fortunes. It was
desirous that the French should also go out to the east, and induced many times the French
traders to form companies to trade with eastern countries, from the time of King Louis the
13th, but they were unable to come out to the east for trade purposes, because French
traders were not enterprising as the Hollanders and the English. In the reign of Louis the
14th (contemporary with the reign of Somdet Phra Narai) the minister of the name of
Colbert formed a French company to trade with eastern countries. But the French company
spent many years in going about, looking for a suitable place wherein to commence their
trading operations.

The French company came and traded in Siam towards the later portion of Somdet
Phra Narai's reign, in the year of the monkey, Buddha Era 2223, many years after Somdet
Phra Narai had entered into friendly relations with Louis the 14th.
The reason of the Siamese entering into friendly relations with the French was because
of the French priests coming into Siam to preach Christianity. They induced Louis the 14th
to enter into friendly relations with King Somdet Phra Narai with a view principally to
spreading Christianity. Therefore the subject of the connection of the Siamese with the
French is different from that of other European nationalities. I should explain a little in
detail.
The history of the French priests coming to Siam for the first time was because Pope
Clement the 9th thought that Christianity in eastern countries was declining very much,
because the Portuguese had extended their power in the east, and there was nobody to
uphold and support it as in former days. At present King Louis the 14th was more powerful
than any other sovereign, and held also the beliefs of the Roman Catholic Church, so that
the Pope praised him as the upholder of religion. He therefore asked King Louis to assist in
the prosperity of the Christian religion in eastern countries as formerly. Louis the 14th had
strong religious beliefs, and was a supporter of the priests and the Jesuits who were already
spreading the religion. King Louis undertook the task set him by the Pope, and selected 3
French priests who were intelligent and clever and willing to come out to eastern countries
to propagate the religion in the east. The Pope appointed them as bishops, namely bishop
Bract (transcribed according to Siamese spelling but according to Mr. W.A.R. Wood in his

yore. The subject of the account of the wars between Siam and Burma when Ayut'ia was
the capital of Siam ends here. I am of opinion that the book is already long enough, and
therefore I write another book about the wars between Siam and Burma when the city of
T'onburi was the capital of Siam, and also in the time of the present capital of Siam--Phra
Maha Nakon Amara Ratanakosin (Bangkok). Whoever wants to know about the sequel,
should seek for it in that book.

TRANSLATOR'S EPILOGUE.
I have completed the translation of the Siamese book, written by His Royal Highness
Somdet Krom Phya Damrong Rajanubhab, giving the Siamese point of view of the wars
between Siam and Burma. It is impossible for a subject of this kind not to be patriotic, and
His Royal Highness's patriotism has been evident in many places of his narration of the
events of the wars. He has taken infinite pains to arrive at the truth, by reading several
copies of Siamese history, the history of Burma, the history of Mons and the records left by
European traders and teachers of Christianity, and I really marvel at the extent of his
reading. His Royal Highness wrote two books, the first volume relates about the wars
between Siam and Burma while Ayut'ia was the capital of Siam, and the second volume
relates about the wars between Siam and Burma while T'onburi was the capital of Siam and
subsequently when the capital was transferred to the present site, namely, Krung
Ratanakosin, Bangkok as it is commonly called. I have followed as closely as possible to
the wording of His Royal Highness, avoiding any comment of my own on his statements.
The first volume contains 385 pages of Siamese print and the second volume contains 428
pages. It has taken me nearly a year to translate the first volume and it is my regret that I
cannot undertake the translation of the second volume. I find a great difficulty in
translating even the first volume, because both Siamese and English are not my mother
tongue, and for this reason I ask for the indulgence of those who may read or review my
translation. If I have made available to those who know English and who are unacquainted
with Siamese, I consider that I am fully repaid for the trouble I have taken in translating the
book. I may mention here that His Royal Highness gave me these two books in his palace,
and I consider that I have paid part of his kindness to me while serving under him, by
translating into English at least the first volume of his work.
Dated 26th October 1953,
Toungoo.
Phra Phraison Salarak
Thien Subindu
