CHRISTIAN MISSIONS IN THE NICOBARS.
In Addenda to 'Denmark's Interest in Burma; B.R.S. Journal
Vol. XXIX Part III December 1939, Professor Pearn takes for
granted that the first attempt of Roman Catholic Missions to work
in the Nicobars was made in 1780. According to a well-informed
article by the Rev. C. H. Chard in The Rangoon Church News for
April 1885, there would seem to have been an attempt by Jesuits
nearly seventy years earlier and fifty years before the arrival of the
first Moravian missionaries.
The following is a quotation from Mr. Chard's article:-
"It is to the Church of Rome that belongs the honour of
making the first attempt to plant the Cross on these
beautiful islands. And a strangely noble attempt it was
notwithstanding its failure. What there is known of it is
only to be gathered from the Lettres edifiantes et curienses.
There in a letter from Pere Faure of the Society of Jesus
dated January 17th, 1711 to Pere de la Boessc of the
same society the writer states that he had arrived at
Pondicherry and had pressed the Superior to allow him
to devote himself to the conversion of the Nicobarese,
that he had consequently been chosen with Pere Bonnct
'pour mettre la premiere main a une si bonne oeuvre'.
They started in the Lys-Brillac. When the Nicobars
were sighted this devoted servant of the Lord writes
'How happy shall I be, Reverend father, if, when you
receive my letter, I have already been deemed worthy to
suffer something for Jesus Christ! But you know me
too well not to be persuaded that an equal grace is
reserved for others who deserve it more than I' As
they drew near land (it was somewhere on the coast of
the Great Nicobar) the boat was lowered to put the
good fathers ashore. The ship's company gathered
round to see them depart and as we learn from an eye
witness, were astonished to see with what joy the two
Missionaries went to give themselves up to the mercy of

bag of rice they forced their way into the jungles and
were lost to sight. Of their fate nothing certain is known.
The natives say that after three years they died at the
Great Nicobar of dysentery; according to another account
they were killed at Camorta. Of their devotion and
