SOME ADDITIONS TO
THE BIBLIOGRAPHY
OF SINO-TIBETAN LANGUAGES
by
E. P. Quigly
In the review of the Bibliography of Sino-Tibetan Languages, compiled
by Robert Shafer, JBRS, XL, ii(a), pp. 358- 359, Than Htut draws attention
to certain omissions in this useful reference work. In particular, he mentions
the omission of the first Burmese - English Dictionary of 1826, compiled from
material prepared by Dr. Adoniram Judson and Felix Carey, which has
generally been overlooked by bibliographers. This dictionary contains a
preface by Jonathan Wade, who was responsible for seeing it through the
press in Calcutta.
Robert Shafer, in his bibliography, whilst mentioning, on page 155,
certain grammars and dictionaries by Jonathan Wade, omits several other
works by this missionary, notably the following, which are in the British
Museum:
1. WADE, J., Karen Dictionary. pp. 9-324 only. No title-page. 4to
No date [1843-4].
2. SAU KAU-TOO and J. WADE, Thesaurus of Karen knowledge
comprising traditions, legends or fables, poetry, customs, super-
stitions, demonology, therapeutics, etc. alphabetically arranged, and
forming a complete Native Karen Dictionary with definitions
and examples, illustrating the usages of every word. 4 volumes. 8vo.
Tavoy. Karen Mission Press, 1847 - 50.
3. WADE, J., A Vocabulary of the Sgau Karen Language pp. 1-1024-
8vo. Tavoy. Karen Mission Press, 1849.
4. WADE, J., Karen Varnacular Grammar with English interspersed
for the benefit of Foreign Students. Embracing terminology,
etymology, syntax and style etc. 8vo. Maulmain. American Mission
Press, 1861.

in Burniese, A Dictionary of Buddhism and Burman Literature, which, in the

All these books by Wade are scarce and item 1, the Karen Dictionary
is unique and extremely rare. This incomplete Dictionary is fully described
in an article, entitled "The First Karen Dictionary", published in The British
Museum Quarterly, Vo. XX, No. 3, pp.58-59, London, 1956, by Dr. D. E.

The Karen Dictionary (British Museum 12907.f.16) is a quarto
book, bound in original boards with calf spine. The double-column
pages number from 9 to 324. It bears the British Museum's date stamp of
acquisition "29 Nov. 1855" and, on the inside frontcover 30-shillings has
been written in ink in a contemporary hand.
Dr. Rhodes's article records that “In November 1855 the Museum
purchased a Karen dictionary consisting of a drop-head title and pp. 9-324 of
text, but having no beginning and no end. Until 1956 it was placed among
anonymous dictionaries and catalogued as a work in progress, date
circa 1852.”
On page 9 the dictionary begins with m:
*" prefixed to verbs, denotes the fufure tense 181||”.
The dictionary ends on page 324 with:
"“mel mel, adverb descriptive of sound like that of monkeys,
“ကက
or other animals jumping about in the tops of the trees, or the
agitation of leaves or bushes in other ways."
This incomplete Karen Dictionary is referred to in the preface by C.B.
Bennett to Wade's A Vocabulary of the Sgau Karen Language, item 3 above.
He says:
"A dictionary of the Karen Language was commenced by Mr. Wade,
some years since, and in 1842, a revision and rewriting of the work was
undertaken and the printing commenced in quarto form." But ill-health.
prevented Jonathan Wade from continuing this work and "he was advised
to relinquish his plan of the quarto volume, for a time, after 324 pages had
been printed and to prepare a less elaborate work." Item 1, Karen Dictionary,
in the British Museum, is proof of the “printing commenced in quarto form"
referred to by Bennett. It may be assumed that the first eight pages, including
the title-page, were never printed.
