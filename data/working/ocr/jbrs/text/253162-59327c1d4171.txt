BURIAL MOUND OF THE LAWAS

Letter No. 76/A. C. F., dated Camp the 9th May 1935 from Mr H. A.
Maxwell, Assistant Conservator of Forests, attached to Thaungyin Division,
to the Divisional Forest Officer, Thaungyin Division, Moulmein.
Whilst carrying exploration in the valley of the Onkra Chaung on the
24th April 1935, I discovered a very curious burial mound, said to be the
burial mound of the Lawa people who lived up this valley many years ago.
Very little is known of these people but I was able to get a certain
amount of information on the subject from one of the old inhabitants of
Maung (Maw) E Village. This old man had never seen them himself but said
that they were beginning to disappear from the valley when his father was a
young man.
The Lawas, I gather, were a sub-branch of one of 30 Shan tribes. Like
other Shans they were Nat worshippers but differed from other races of
Shan in their curious burial customs. Their cemetaries took the form of
mounds, generally situated a long way from the village. When a Lawa died
it was customary to bury all the dead person's worldy wealth, generally in the
form of gold and silver bangles and ornaments, in the mound with the corpse.
Before the Lawas left the Onkra valley for Siamese Territory their
numbers had been sadly reduced and what had once been a flourishing
village was reduced to a small settlement of about four houses.
