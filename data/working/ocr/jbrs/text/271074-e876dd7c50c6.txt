PREHISTORIC STONE IMPLEMENTS FROM THE
KONBYINMYINT OF THE IRRAWADDY AND
PAUNGLAUNG RIVERS.
While stationed at Yenangyaung for a
at Yenangyaung for a few days last
December I discovered that prehistoric implements occur abun-
dantly in the base of the Konbyinmyint gravels of that neighbour-
hood. Collections were made from seven localities along the eastern
bank of the river between Sadaing and the mouth of the Pin
chaung. The implements are mostly massive rostro-carinates,
hand axes, and cleavers, up to nine inches in length and several
pounds weight. Some of the smaller specimens resemble the hand
axes recently recorded (J. B. R. S., 1936, P. 163) from the base of the
Konbyin gravels at Thayetmyo, but are much less rolled. The
general aspect of the collection suggests close comparison with
the pre-Chellean or primitive Chellean cultures of India, Africa,
and Western Europe.
In January in the Paunglaung Valley near Pyinmana I found
numerous examples of the same culture, here also in basal gravels
of the Konbyinmyint Terrace.
Further discussion of this abundant new Burma material must
be deferred until it has been compared more closely with contem-
poraneous Indian, African, and European types.
T. O. MORRIS.
