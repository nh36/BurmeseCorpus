Reviews.
FRENCH POLICY AND DEVELOPMENT IN INDO-CHINA.-By Thomas
E. Ennis. (Chicago, Illinois, U.S.A., The University of Chicago Press,
214 pp., map. $3.00)
Professor Ennis, of the University of West Virginia, has put all
interested in the history of southeastern Asia in his debt by the produc-
tion of the book under review. In 190 pages of reading matter, with an
appendix of twenty pages, the author traces carefully the development
of French conquest and administration of Indo-China. Careful attention
is given to the economics of Indo-China and to French social work in
that land. There is an exhaustive bibliography on pages 203-214 listing
all the important source material on French Indo-China from the
beginnings of French contacts in 1625 to the present. The document-
ation is well up to accepted standards of scholarship. The author
quotes an interesting request once made by Dr. Tagore: "I beseech
you, send not only administrators, formulae, and machines, but also
some feeling."
It is regrettable that the mechanical work of printing and binding
is not more praiseworthy.
J. L. C.
