In his article "On the Semantic
Structure of Language”, in Universals of
Language (1963) 114 ff., Uriel Weinreich
remarks (p.114f) that depletion of meaning
is probably a universal phenomenon. It
is a familiar fact that historically many
morphemes which now have grammatical
function only, originally were full words
which gradually (often in compounds)
came to have purely grammatical function.
Sometimes the independent morpheme
with full meaning was lost altogether
while it became an affix in combination
with other forms. A good example of
this is the history of the English suffix

is thus noteworthy as a late example of the

times the full word remained in use, but
was no longer closely associated with the
phonetically identical bound form with
purely grammatical function: e.g. -less,
-ful identical in pronunciation with free
forms less, full in: hopeless, senseless,
hopeful, careful, wakeful etc. Combinations
with less would probably not be inter-
preted by the ordinary native speaker as
compounds of the noun plus the free
form less, and some combinations with-ful
could not be interpreted as noun plus full:
e.g. wakeful.
