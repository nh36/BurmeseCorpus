I. Introduction
2
An important phase of the program for the development of
Atomic Energy in Burma is the prospecting for radioactive minerals
which are the fuels for atomic reactors and which now command a
good price in the world market. The prospecting for radioactive
minerals is done to a large extent with the aid of radioactivity detectors,
popularly known as Geiger Counters.
A reliable radioactivity detector needs to have some meter indica-
tion of the radioactivity either in terms of counts per minute or
milliroentgens per hour. For this, we need to have in addition to the
Geiger Mueller tube which is sensitive to the gamma and beta rays
from the radioactive minerals, some electronic device to convert the
random and irregularly shaped pulses from the Geiger Mueller tube
into well defined pulses of constant height and width. Only then will
the meter reading be a correct indication of the quantity of
radioactivity being measured. For this job, a suitable circuit has been
found to be what is called a monostable multivibrator. Multivibrators,
of many types are well known in the literature of electronic circuits
especially in radar work. But in radar and electronic computers they
were used for time base and master control and synchronising
purposes.
Also the previous multivibrator circuits employed radio tubes as
their active elements. But very recently, transistors have been invented
which require only one-thousandth the power requirements of heated
filament radio tubes. By employing these transistors as the active
elements of multivibrator circuits a good saving in battery drain can be
achieved. It is obvious that this is a great advantage for portable
radioactivity detectors which we want to take into the jungles on long
prospecting trips.

The particular type of multivibrator we are interested in is the
monostable multivibrator. As its name implies this has one stable
state and we can assume for our discussion that it ordinarily stays in
this stable state and that during such times it gives no output. Then if
we apply a trigger pulse to this multivibrator it is disturbed from this
state and gives out a well defined electrical pulse whose characteristics
are fixed by the design of the multivibrator and then it relapses back
to its stable state.
In this way, for every trigger pulse (whose individual shapes and
sizes may vary) we get one output pulse (whose shape and size is well
defined and unvarying). Now these output pulses from a multivibrator
are sent into an Integrator and are used in the radioactivity detector to
actuate a meter. The reading of the meter will indicate in a predeter-
