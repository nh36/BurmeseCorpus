NOTES AND QUERIES.
(1)
Mr. Langham Carter's little book, The Powundaung Caves
(Rangoon Times Press, 12 annas), gives an interesting and instructive
description of this group of caves which lies about sixteen miles
south-west of Monywa. The caves, some of which are apparently
artificial, show traces of Pyu influence, and by tradition the origin
of the caves themselves is ascribed to the Pyu. The earliest
epigraph at Powundaung, however, is dated 600 B.E., and most of
the ornamentation of the caves appears to be of medieval Burmese
type. The caves fall into three groups: one group, and that the
great majority, consists of small caves that are little more than
niches; the second group consists of narrow galleries, anything from
15 to 70 feet in length and 10 to 25 feet in height, but only from
6 to 15 feet in depth; the third and least numerous group consists
of caves of very considerable size. The remarkable thing in all these
caves is that the altars which they contain, and the ornamentation,
have in every case been carved out of the living rock. In this and
certain other features, the caves suggest Indian influence. Mr.
Langham Carter describes the caves in detail, and also gives full
information about how to reach Powundaung and where to stay
while there. The book, the proceeds from whose sale are to go to
war-charities, may be strongly recommended to members of the
Society as an interesting and scholarly account of these caves to
which little attention has previously been drawn.
B.R.P.
