Anglo-French Rivalry in the Far East
Crawfurd, Burney and the Calcutta
Government were interested in Gibson's
Journal of the Burmese Embassy to Vietnam,
Not because they wanted to know what
progress His Burmese Majesty was making
in trying to find allies and friends in sur-
rounding countries, BUT to know the extent
of French influence in trade and politics in
that part of the world.
In 1821 we read of the Crawfurd Mis-
sion to Bangkok and then there was the
Mission of Burney to Rama III.
In 1821 the East India Company sent
Crawfurd to Hué to establish relations with
the new ruler, to see how far French influ-
ence and trade were progressing and to pro-
cure trading rights. Though refused a Royal
Audience he was told that English vessels
would enjoy the same privileges as other
European trading vessels. The British inter-
rest in Cochin-Coina and Gia Long began
when the English firms of Madras and
Calcutta supplied the liberation forces of
Gia Long with arms. Mr Purefoy, agent of
the British firm Aboot and Maitland who
stayed in that country between 1800 and
1807 has left us interesting account.ª

Jean Baptiste Chaigneau
( The Marquis of Victorious Stratagies)
Jean-Baptiste Chaigneau was born on
the 8th August 1769, in the Chateau of
Baizy at Nord d'Auray in Morbihan. Grand-
son and son of Captains belonging to the
French East Indies Company he was also
cousin of Chateaubriand.
Laws and Customs of the Great South
When he was twelve years old he sailed.
as a cabin boy taken prisoner by the English
and interned for some months at St. Helena
First a Second Class Volunteer and then a
First Class Volunteer on the Corvette
la Subtile, he fought for three years in cam-
paigns in the Indian and Chinese seas. In
1788 he passed his hydrographic examina-
tions at Lorient but could not obtain a
commission in the Royal Navy. In September
1791 he left as first Ensign on la Flavie a
merchantman sent out to look for la Perouse.
At Macao la Flavie was blocked by the
English and disarmed. Free from his contract
with his employer, the young Ensign went
in search of adventure to Cochinchina and
was given hospitality by the Bishop
d'Adran who was one of the pillars of Gia
Long's regime in 1794.
Chaigneau began making business trips
to Macao and entered the service of Nguyen-
Anh (who later became Emperor Gia Long)
only after the departure of J. M. Dayot in
1796-97. Commander of le Dragon Volant
( 32 cannons and 300 men) he fought well
in all the campaigns of Nguyen Anh espe
cially in the great naval battle of the 28th
February 1801.
In recompense for his loyal services,
he was named Imperial Delegate attached
to the person of the Emperor, Commandant
of the regiment of the Army of the Centre,
Marquis of Thang Toan, namely Marquis
“of Victorious Strategies." In 1803 he was
made General of Regiment. 11.

The Naturalist Pierre Médard Diard
Pierre Médard Diard, pupil and protegè
of Cuvier left Bordeaux in August 1817 to
take possession of an inheritance in Bengal.
He met Governor T. S. Raffles in Sumatra
and in December 1818 accompanied him
while taking possession of Singapore. In
April 1821 he met J. B. Chaigneau in
Batavia and listening to his arguments and
invitation left for Cochinchina on the
Larose. When he got to Cochin China he
began learning the language and adopted
the day to day dress of the people. With the
help of Chaigneau he obtained permission
from the Cochin Chinese Government to
go about freely in search of insects etc. for
the Museum. He travelled through South
Tonkin, the Central and Southern provinces
of Vietnam and Cambodia. He contacted
the Crawfurd mission and also was of
service to the Burmese Mission by telling
his friend Gibson what the mandarins
thought of the proposed treaty between
Burma and Vietnam. In Cambodia he is
reported to have cured the King of leprosy
and he was the first European to contemplate
the wonders of Angor Wat. He left Cochin
China definitively in 1824. He joined the
services of the Dutch Government and was
for many years, Director of the Botanical
Gardens of Buitenzorg where he drew up a
sytematic list of plants found there. He died
in Batavia on the 16th February, 1863.15

Eugéne Chaigneau
When the French Naval Officer and
Mandarin, Mr. J. B. Chaigneau returned to
Cochin China, he brought his nephew to
be his Chancellor. The young man was
designated in 1825 to direct the affairs of
the Consulate during his uncle's absence,
at Frs 9,000 a year. The Cochinchinese
Government refused to recognise him.
Eugène Chaigneau then left for Calcutta

For reasons of economy the French

The local Vietnamese chronicles of
course give their view of the Burmese Miss-
ion and say that ‘Burmese Messengers came
to offer their submission' but we of Burma
know what is meant when an Eastern King
says that and we also know the pride of
our past kings who never gave their sub-
mission to any Foreign monarch but even
considered foreign embassies to their
courts as tribute missions! Also Kings had
to be flattered and the court scribe of Minh
Mang may have slipped in the word sub-
mission to flatter their Emperor, or perhaps
it was the custom to preface the account of
any mission with a usual introduction that
the newly arrived strangers had come to
offer their submission and tribute to the
